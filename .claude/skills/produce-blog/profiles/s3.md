# Profile s3

Write a short, plainly-stated blog post drawn from the batch of Ark Learning notes resolved from the manifest. Same base instinct as s1 and s2 — condense the notes to one core idea and say it in your own words rather than recapping each video — but built for speed and accessibility, not depth or authority.

## What's unchanged from s1/s2

- **Own the idea.** Digest the notes to a single core takeaway and state it directly. Don't narrate the sources ("the video says…") — the notes are raw material, not the subject.
- **Keep real specifics real.** If a note names an actual tool, book, or brand, keep the real name — don't generalize it into "a productivity app". You're just not obligated to independently re-verify figures via research (see below).
- **Banned AI-fluff words** from `.claude/CLAUDE.md` Section 2 still apply (no *delve, leverage, robust, seamless, testament, unlock, dive into, landscape*, etc.).

## What's different for s3

### 1. No independent research

Skip `WebSearch`/`WebFetch` entirely. Work only from what's already in the resolved Ark notes — nothing external. Don't cite outside sources, and skip the "Sources & further reading" list s1/s2 use; there's nothing external to list. If a note states a figure or study finding, you can mention it, but present it as what the note said, not as something you went and independently confirmed.

### 2. Softspoken, low-key tone

Write like someone thinking out loud, not preaching from a stage. This is the opposite of s1's "confident, declarative tone" — no bold pronouncements, no coming on strong. Hedge naturally where things genuinely aren't certain. Low-key doesn't mean wishy-washy or vague — it means quiet and grounded rather than a big bold headline voice. **Sound like an actual person, not a model summarizing** — that's the whole point of low-key; vary sentence rhythm, skip formulaic transitions, and drop any phrasing that reads like generic AI output.

### 3. Everyday vocabulary

Write like you're talking to a friend, not filing a report. Before reaching for a word with more than two or three syllables, or one that sounds "smart," check if there's a plainer word and use that instead. Cut academic or technical phrasing even if a note itself uses it — restate it plainly.

### 4. Structure: bullets over prose, short paragraphs

Lean on bullet points wherever a list says it faster than a paragraph. Where prose is needed, keep paragraphs to two or three sentences. Skip s1's three-part macro structure (executive view / body / summary) as a hard requirement — s3 doesn't need to say everything three times. Open with a **punchy hook under 50 words** — one to three sentences that state the core idea and make someone want to keep reading, not a throat-clearing intro — then a few bulleted or short-paragraph sections making the point, then a short close.

### 5. Length: under 800 words

Hard ceiling, no minimum. If you're near 800 words and still have more to say, that's a sign to cut, not extend — one strong bullet beats three vague sentences.

## Save the output

Same location and naming as s1/s2: `blog-factory/output/<title-slug>/index.md`. Frontmatter keeps the same shape (`title`, `topic`, `date`, `sources`), but `sources:` here just lists the Ark note names and their origin links — there's no external research to cite.

Word count check:

```bash
awk 'c>=2{print} /^---$/{c++}' "blog-factory/output/<title-slug>/index.md" | wc -w
```

When done, report: the profile used (`s3`), the core idea, the word count, and any rows that failed to resolve.
