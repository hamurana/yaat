# Profile s2

Write one original blog post drawn from the batch of Ark Learning notes resolved from the manifest — same spirit as s1 (your own argument, not a retelling), but at roughly 75% of s1's research depth and formal polish. Use s2 when a fast, solid, well-written post is the goal and exhaustive citation-grade rigor isn't required.

## What's unchanged from s1

- **Own the ideas.** Same core stance as s1: digest the notes to a thesis and argue it in your own voice. No "the video claims…", no retelling. The creators are the seed, not the subject.
- **Three-part macro structure**: executive view → main body → summary with "so what" actions.
- **Output location and naming**: `blog-factory/output/<title-slug>/index.md`, same slug derivation and collision rules as s1.
- **Voice**: confident, declarative tone; stay open to strong counter-evidence rather than cherry-picking; say plainly when a specific claim is genuinely uncertain.
- **Proofread before saving**: re-read the full draft once for spelling mistakes, including proper nouns and any names pulled from research.
- **Banned words** from `.claude/CLAUDE.md` Section 2 still apply, and precision on names/brands/tools still matters — this is still something a reader can act on, not a vague gist.

## What's dialed back for s2 (the ~75% dial)

### 1. Read everything first — the thesis bar doesn't move

Read all referenced notes in full and find the one thesis angle the batch collectively supports — same method as s1 (name the shared upstream variable, or lean into an internal disagreement as the angle). This step doesn't get lighter; a weak thesis undermines everything downstream regardless of how much research backs it.

### 2. Research the headline claims only, not every claim

Where s1 runs ~6–8 searches covering every core claim, s2 scopes research to the **2–4 claims the thesis actually leans on** — the headline stat, the central mechanism, the one counter-argument worth engaging. Skip independently re-verifying claims the notes already state clearly and uncontroversially; trust the note.

- A good, reputable secondary source (a well-known publication, an established research-summary site) is fine here — no need to chase down the canonical named study behind every technique the way s1 does.
- Still cite what you use, inline as a markdown link, with a shorter "Sources & further reading" list at the end. Author/year/journal precision is a nice-to-have, not a requirement — a working link and a clear line on what it shows is enough.
- If a figure from the notes and a figure from a quick search disagree, flag it and use judgment — you're not obligated to resolve it with further digging the way s1 is.

### 3. Register: conversational-confident, not academic-adjacent

Keep the confident tone described above, but write closer to how you'd explain the idea to a smart friend than how you'd write a heavily cited essay. Shorter paragraphs, more direct address, fewer qualifying clauses. Topic-register judgment (evidence-first for Health, numbers-first for Investment, personal for Style/Charisma) still applies — s2 just spends less real estate justifying each point.

### 4. Length: shorter cap

Target roughly **1,200–1,800 words** — noticeably shorter than s1's 3,000-word ceiling. No minimum: say what the argument supports and stop. If a topic genuinely needs more room to land the thesis it's fine to run longer, but treat 1,800 words as the point where you should double-check every section is earning its place.

### 5. Precision rules — same floor as s1

Even at 75%, never generalize a specific name into a vague category — "magnesium glycinate, 200–400mg elemental" stays specific, never "a magnesium supplement". What's relaxed is *how much independent verification* backs each figure (see step 2 above), not how specifically the post states it.

## Save the output

Same mechanics as s1: `blog-factory/output/<title-slug>/index.md`, frontmatter with `title`, `topic`, `date`, `sources`. Word-count check:

```bash
awk 'c>=2{print} /^---$/{c++}' "blog-factory/output/<title-slug>/index.md" | wc -w
```

When done, report: the profile used (`s2`), the thesis angle, the sources actually researched and cited, the word count, and any rows that failed to resolve.
