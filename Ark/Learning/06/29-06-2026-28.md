**The Hook & Core Thesis**

After years of wearing an **[[Apple Watch]]** Ultra, **[[Garrett Crespo]]** demoted it to a fitness-only tool — the endless notifications were the breaking point — and rediscovered traditional watches. The deeper point: a smartwatch is disposable, but a real watch *carries a story* you can pass on.

---
**Key Arguments Checklist**

- **Notifications killed it** — even battery/fitness pings were too much; he wanted something on his wrist that just tells time.
- **Smartwatches have a shelf life** — software cutoffs and dead, unserviceable batteries make them disposable; you'll never keep your old ones.
- **A traditional watch holds memories** — the real reason to own several is that each ties to a life event.
- **Three watch technologies cover everything:** quartz digital, quartz analog, and mechanical.
- **Quartz is reliable, accurate, and cheap**; mechanical is gears-and-springs with a smooth sweep and needs servicing.
- **Start cheap and let the collection grow** — but watch out, it's addictive.

---
**High-Yield Assets**

*The three watch technologies:*
1. **Quartz digital** — a **[[quartz crystal]]** vibrates **32,768 times per second** under voltage; an integrated circuit reads that frequency for extreme accuracy (gains/loses only seconds per month/year). Fewer parts = very reliable, but cheaper to replace than repair.
2. **Quartz analog** — same quartz tech, but traditional hour/minute/second hands that **tick once per second** (vs a smartwatch's emulated smooth sweep).
3. **Mechanical** — no battery or electronics, just **gears and springs**; ticks multiple times per second for a smooth sweep. Either **automatic** (a weight winds it as you move) or **hand-wound** (wind every 24–48h — common even on high-end watches).

*Specific watches mentioned:*
- **[[Casio F91W]]** — ~$22 at Walmart, ~7-year battery; the "Toyota Corolla of watches." Single green LED backlight.
- **Casio A168WA** — the F91W's flashier sibling: metal bracelet, chromed body, **electroluminescent** backlight (which has a finite lifespan).
- **Casio calculator watch** (~$40) — *Back to the Future* nostalgia.
- **Casio Royale** — multiple **world time zones** (great for travel — switched zones flying to Taiwan with no manual adjust), 100m water resistance, tiny time-zone map in the corner, 10-year battery.
- **[[G-Shock]]** (Casio) — built for maximum durability (one survived a helicopter drop; the Hydraulic Press Channel imploded one past 200m). Some come with solar.
- **[[Citizen]] Brycen chronograph** — quartz analog with a 60-minute chronograph, date window, 24-hour subdial, and **[[Eco-Drive]]** (light/solar charging — never needs a battery change; started ticking instantly after sitting in a dark closed box).
- **[[MoonSwatch]] 1965** — Swatch × Omega collaboration commemorating the **[[Omega Speedmaster]]**'s 1965 certification for all manned spaceflight; 65-minute / 19-hour chronograph hands, glowing 60-marker, **tachymeter** bezel (measures speed over a known distance).
- **[[Timex Expedition]]** — his ~20-year field watch: 24-hour inner ring, date, glowing hands, and **Indiglo** full-face backlight.
- **[[Seiko 5]]** — automatic dive-style watch on the **NH35 movement**, with a day-date function and a timing bezel.
- **[[Invicta Pro Diver]]** — an homage (or "ripoff") of the **[[Rolex Submariner]]**; also uses the **NH35**, making it one of the cheapest ways into that movement. The Grand Diver rates to 300m.
- **Seagull 1963** — a hand-wound mechanical chronograph (referenced, hadn't arrived yet).
- **Reference points:** the **Omega Speedmaster "Dark Side of the Moon"** (hand-wound, still thousands of dollars); astronauts wore the Speedmaster, divers the Rolex Submariner, and James Cameron strapped a Rolex to his submersible at the Mariana Trench.

**Case Studies / Anecdotes**

- **The move to Taiwan** — packing to move countries, he kept finding watch after watch and took the constant-notification Apple Watch off "like the universe was telling me" to collect again. The **MoonSwatch** is the first watch he bought in Taiwan.
- **The Timex Expedition (Eagle Scout)** — bought ~20 years ago from the trading post at **Camp Philmont** (the most expensive thing he bought there) for rope climbing, swimming, and the shooting range; he plans to pass it to a child who takes up scouting/outdoors.
- **The Invicta Pro Diver (graduation gift)** — a titanium Pro Diver his parents gave him at high-school graduation was his entry into mechanical watches; he skipped a service and the rotor weight fell off. He bought another to celebrate landing his teaching job.
- **The G-Shock (photography job)** — bought on his boss's advice ("get one that'll survive hell"); every scratch recalls shoots he did.

---
**The "So What?" Factor**

If notifications are wearing you down, demote the smartwatch to fitness duty and pick up a real watch. Understand the three technologies so you buy what fits your life: a cheap **Casio F91W** or **G-Shock** to start, an **Eco-Drive** quartz analog if you never want to change a battery, or a **Seiko 5** automatic to enter mechanicals. Unlike a disposable smartwatch, a well-maintained watch outlives software cutoffs and accrues the memories of where you wore it — something worth passing down.
