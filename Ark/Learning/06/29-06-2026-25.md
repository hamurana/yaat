**The Hook & Core Thesis**

Summer tempts men into lazy, ill-fitting choices that quietly tank their image. [[Real Men Real Style|Antonio Centeno]] walks through nine common warm-weather mistakes and the breathable, flattering swaps that keep you looking like a man to be taken seriously.

---
**Key Arguments Checklist**

- **Stop defaulting to sandals/flip-flops** — breathable leather shoes can be just as cool and far sharper.
- **Don't go full white-monochrome** — wear white pieces as separates.
- **Undershirts and gymwear belong under shirts or at the gym** — not out in public.
- **Pick a classic sunglass shape** and protect it in a case; never wear shades indoors.
- **Most men should skip board shorts** — classic swim trunks flatter more body types.
- **Cargo shorts are overplayed** — switch to a cleaner, shorter cut.
- **Never wear clothes that are too tight** — fit beats showing off the build.
- **Dress for the message you want to send**, not just to turn heads.

---
**High-Yield Assets**

1. **Sandals/flip-flops overuse** — at a non-swimming pool party, upgrade to **perforated leather** shoes (holes let feet breathe, worn with no-show socks) — e.g. a **[[horse-bit loafer]]** with a curved toe — and let the shoes do the talking with linen trousers and a simple polo.
2. **All-white monochrome** — white linen shirt, white cotton chinos, white sneakers are great *separates* but too much worn together (save the full look for an actual white party).
3. **Undershirts / tank tops as t-shirts** — undershirts (sold in packs, lightweight, deep V-neck) go *under* shirts; sleeveless undershirts and tanks/cut sleeve-holes are gymwear — and **gymwear should stay at the gym**.
4. **Sunglasses** — stick to one of three classics: **[[Aviators]]** (teardrop shape flatters nearly every face), **[[Wayfarers]]** (higher contrast, a 50+ year design), or **[[Clubmasters]]** (Antonio's favorite — contrast without being over the top, and less common). Always use a **soft case**; he scratched a pair of **[[Maui Jim]]**s by forgetting them on his chest indoors.
5. **Sunglasses indoors** — avoid; pop them on the head or in a pocket case instead.
6. **Board shorts** — ~**80% of men** should skip them. Originated with **1960s** surf culture (less to catch on the board), but mass-manufactured in the **1980s–90s** they were cut for thin, fit bodies; they exaggerate a gut or make a short power-lifter look stout. **Speedos** are comfortable but socially limited to parts of Europe.
7. **Classic swim trunks** — elastic waist, a **shorter inseam** (especially for men 5'6"–5'7") for better proportion.
8. **Baggy cargo shorts** — fine as a Disneyland-dad utility item, but overplayed as a daily default. Swap for a **shorter, non-cargo** cut in light gray or tan worn with a polo.
9. **Too-tight clothing** — the "stuffed sausage" look after a 20–30 lb gain. Let out a jacket/trousers if there's material, otherwise pack it away and hit the gym — and even with a great build, avoid overly tight clothes that send the wrong message in a networking setting.

---
**Case Studies / Anecdotes**

- **The scratched Maui Jims** — Antonio left his sunglasses on his chest, started working indoors at a Midwest hardware store, and scratched the lens — his cautionary case for always using a soft case.
- **The "muscles from Brussels" friend** — a fit friend at a conference could turn heads with a tight shirt, but Antonio's point is that the attention can be a detriment when you'd rather be known as the smart, capable one; dress for the setting and the message.

---
**The "So What?" Factor**

Heat is no excuse for sloppiness. Trade flip-flops for breathable leather, wear white as separates, keep undershirts and gymwear in their lane, and protect a classic pair of sunglasses in a case. Choose swimwear and shorts that flatter your actual build rather than an idealized one, and never let anything fit too tight. The deeper lesson: clothing is a tool to send the message you want — use it to become the confident man you know yourself to be.
