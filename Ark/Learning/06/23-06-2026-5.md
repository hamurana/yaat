**Discouragement Is Not Depression**

Many people conflate feeling down with having a mental illness or being cut off from progress. The key distinction: discouragement, disappointment, and drudgery are temporary states — not permanent diagnoses. Feeling low at a particular moment doesn't mean your life is over. Don't let debt define your depth. Don't let small setbacks dictate your entire direction.

**The Reframe: Correction and Redirection**

When things go wrong, one way to interpret it is as abandonment. Another is as correction — being redirected toward something better. New levels bring new challenges; if you understand what those challenges are teaching you, you can move through them rather than being crushed by them. The discomfort isn't punishment — it's the necessary friction of growth.

**Vision as an Anchor**

In the midst of discouragement, what counters it most effectively is having a clear enough vision that you can orient toward it even when you can't feel it. The goal isn't to feel good about your current situation — it's to remain pointed at where you're going. When you have that, the darkness of a rough patch doesn't mean you're lost.

**The Practical Response to Feeling Negative**

- Shake off discouragement before it settles — don't rehearse your setbacks by replaying them
- Get over disappointment actively, not passively; sitting with it and expecting it to lift on its own doesn't work
- Deal with the drudgery of the work you've been given — consistency through boring and hard periods is what most people won't do, which is exactly why it compounds into an advantage
- Don't pray and do nothing; pray and then act on the direction you receive
