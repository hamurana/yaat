**The Reframe: You're Not Overthinking — You're Under-Processing**

Most people who feel mentally overwhelmed assume they're overthinking. The real diagnosis is under-processing. We live in a fast world that rewards rapid consumption and immediate action: hear something, move to the next task, the next hour, the next responsibility. We rarely stop to actually process what we've taken in.

The result is that information, experiences, emotions, and decisions pile up unprocessed — and that backlog is what creates the feeling of being overwhelmed. It's not that your mind is too active. It's that you haven't given it enough time to catch up with everything it's received.

**The Fix**

Three concrete steps to process what you're accumulating:

1. **Journal** — Write thoughts down. Getting things out of your head and onto paper externalises the mental load, creates clarity, and separates what matters from noise.

2. **Set goals** — Clarity on direction cuts through overwhelm. When you don't know where you're going, everything feels equally urgent. Goals create a filter: does this move me forward or not?

3. **Prioritise and repurpose** — Restructure your time around what actually matters. Not everything in your inbox deserves your attention at the same level. Learning to rank and sequence reduces the sensation that everything needs to happen at once.

The core insight is simple: overwhelm isn't a sign that you're broken or that life is too much. It's a signal that your processing hasn't kept pace with your receiving. Slow down enough to catch up.
