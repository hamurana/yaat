**7 ETF Investing Mistakes Costing You Money** (Martik Finance)

A concise rundown — here are the seven mistakes:

---

**1. Assuming an ETF is well diversified without checking** Not all ETFs are equally diversified. Always check the fact sheet for number of holdings, sector exposure, and concentration in top holdings. Example: XLV (healthcare) holds ~65 companies with the top 10 making up over 50%, while VHT holds ~400 companies with top 10 at 44% — same sector, very different risk profile.

**2. Thinking ETFs are risk-free** Broad market ETFs still carry full market risk. The S&P 500 dropped ~50% in 2008. Investors who see "diversified" and assume "safe" tend to panic-sell during crashes and lock in losses. ETFs reduce individual stock risk, not market risk.

**3. Ignoring fees (expense ratio + trading commissions)** Passive ETFs have low fees; actively managed ETFs typically charge above 1% annually. Brokerage trading commissions also add up, especially for frequent buyers.

**4. Ignoring tracking error** A passive ETF should closely follow its index — but fees, rebalancing delays, and liquidity issues can cause it to lag. A high tracking error means you earn less than the index you thought you were buying into. Always compare tracking error when choosing between similar ETFs.

**5. Chasing past performance** A 30–50% return last year doesn't predict next year. What was hot often underperforms after. Focus instead on the ETF's strategy, holdings, diversification, and fees relative to your goals.

**6. Trying to time the market** Waiting for the "perfect" entry point almost never works — even for professionals. Missing just the 10 best days of the S&P 500 over 20 years cuts total returns by nearly half. The solution: dollar-cost averaging and staying invested consistently through volatility.

**7. Investing in leveraged ETFs without understanding the risks** 2x or 3x leveraged ETFs reset daily, which causes "volatility decay" — losses compound faster than gains when markets move up and down. Even if the underlying index ends flat, a 2x leveraged ETF can end significantly lower. Not suitable for long-term or beginner investors.
