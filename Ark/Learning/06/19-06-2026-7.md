## What Is an ETF?
**Exchange Traded Fund** = basket of investments (stocks, bonds, securities) packaged into one tradable unit. Combines mutual fund diversification with stock trading flexibility.

**Example:** Vanguard S&P 500 Index ETF (VFV) holds 505 different companies. Instead of buying Apple, Microsoft, and Nvidia separately, you own pieces of all through one ETF—instant diversification.

## Key Concept: NAV
**Net Asset Value** = actual value of all holdings ÷ number of units outstanding. The true value of one ETF unit.

ETFs have two prices:
- **NAV:** Calculated at end of each trading day (true value)
- **Market Price:** Determined by supply/demand during trading (what you actually pay)

*Ideal situation:* These track closely (high liquidity). VFV example showed market price within 0.02% of NAV.

## Where to Buy ETFs
Trade on stock exchanges like regular stocks via:
- Traditional banks
- Discount brokers
- RoboAdvisor platforms

Many brokers now offer **commission-free ETF trading.**

## Five Critical Evaluation Factors

### 1. Understand Risk Level
Every ETF gets a risk rating (low to high). Don't stop at the rating—examine deeper metrics:

- **Standard Deviation:** How much returns typically vary (volatility)
- **Beta:** How it moves vs. benchmark (1.00 = moves exactly with benchmark)
- **R-squared:** How perfectly it tracks the benchmark (1.00 = perfect tracking)
- **Worst-case scenario:** Check worst 3-month return (VFV example: −13.68%, turning $1,000 into $863)

**Ask yourself:** Can you handle this short-term loss without panicking?

### 2. Examine Track Record
Look at performance across multiple timeframes:
- Check best and worst annual returns
- Calculate compound annual return (VFV: $1,000 invested since inception → $6,561 by April 2025 = 16.25% annual compound return)
- Compare 1-year, 3-year, 10-year returns
- Review best and worst 3-month returns to understand volatility range

**Remember:** Past performance never guarantees future results.

### 3. Understand What You're Actually Buying
- **Holdings:** What does it own? (VFV: 505 large-cap US companies)
- **Top 10 holdings:** VFV example = Apple 6.8%, Microsoft 6.2%, Nvidia 5.6%, Amazon 3.7%, Alphabet 3.6% (35.6% of fund)
- **Concentration risk:** Notice how holdings change as market values shift
- **Diversification:** 505 holdings provides excellent diversification

Knowing your holdings shows how investments behave under different market conditions.

### 4. Understand the Costs (Critical)
**MER (Management Expense Ratio):** Percentage of assets charged annually
- VFV example: 0.09% = $0.90 per $1,000 invested/year
- Actively managed funds often charge 2%+
- A 0.41% difference compounds to thousands of dollars over 30 years

**Other fees:**
- Trading expense ratio (VFV: 0.00% = negligible)
- Bid-ask spread (VFV: 0.02% = excellent liquidity)

Lower MER = more money compounds over time.

### 5. Examine Sector Allocation
Shows how investment distributes across industries, revealing economic exposure:

- Financials: 14.4%
- Healthcare: 10.8%
- Consumer Discretionary: 10.4%
- Communication Services: 9.3%
- Industrials: 8.6%
- Consumer Staples: 6.2%
- Energy: 3.2%
- Utilities: 2.6%
- Real Estate: 2.2%
- Materials: 2.0%

**Significance:** If you already own tech-heavy funds, adding this further concentrates tech exposure. Rising interest rates may benefit financials while challenging utilities. Tech corrections would significantly impact this fund.

## Finding the Information
**ETF Facts:** Regulatory information (ticker, exchange, currency, benchmark)
**Fund Facts:** Current detailed information (net assets, performance, holdings, sector weights)

**Required by law to be freely available** on provider websites. Always download and read before investing.

## The 5-Factor Checklist
1. Risk and volatility levels
2. Returns and performance history
3. What the ETF invests in
4. MER and fee competitiveness
5. Sector allocation fit with your portfolio strategy

## Key Insight
Investing in ETFs can be excellent wealth-building, but only with **informed decisions.** Do your homework, read the documentation, understand exactly what you're buying, and ensure it matches your risk tolerance and portfolio strategy.
