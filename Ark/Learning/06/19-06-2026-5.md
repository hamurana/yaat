## Two Kinds of Confidence
**External confidence** projects outwardly—making deals, gaining friends, influencing others flow naturally. **Internal confidence** is comfort in any situation, which feeds external confidence. Mastering both creates genuine respect.

## The External Layer: Body Language

### Asymmetrical Positioning & Ownership
Don Draper sits asymmetrically, spreading himself comfortably. This posture naturally relaxes others. Move freely through environments, signaling comfort and ownership. When standing, feet should be at least shoulder-width apart; arm positions should invite rather than block.

### Strategic Eye Contact
Don uses variable eye contact strategically. Initially, he gives limited attention (almost disinterested), making others work to win his attention—a powerful psychological reversal. **When making key points, narrow your eyes and deliver piercing eye contact.** This captivates people and emphasizes your most important statements.

### Non-Fidgeting
Fidgeting reveals internal anxiety. Control physical movements—this is harder to fake than body posture because emotions often override conscious effort.

## The Deeper Layer: Non-Reactivity
True confidence shows through **not reacting emotionally** to pressure or mistakes. When two men hit on his wife, Don calmly roleplayed indifference, signaling power through composure. Overreacting makes things worse and signals you're pressured by circumstances.

**For leaders in crisis:** Emotional expression matters, but unchecked emotions undermine problem-solving. Remaining silent while others panic buys valuable thinking time, and it inspires resolve rather than confusion.

## The Advanced Layer: Thinking Under Pressure

Don succeeds not through fast responses but by **slowing down** to think calmly. When a client rejected his pitch, he gave himself a full 20 seconds to respond—appearing composed rather than frantic.

**General rule:** We demand 30% less response time from others than from ourselves. Pause when pressure feels urgent. You'll look more in control and make better decisions.

## The Strategic Layer: Not Trying to Convince

Don is persuasive not by badgering but by **framing himself as an equal partner** in negotiation. He screens clients as much as they screen him. People determine interest partly by how much others want or need them—they like people interested in them, but not *too* much. **Share your interest, but be willing to walk away if it's not reciprocated.** This signals confidence and power, not petulance.

*Caveat:* Walking away emotionally (yelling, telling someone they'll regret it) signals immaturity, not confidence. Instead, adopt the mindset: "We're not a match; I wish you the best."

## The Deepest Layer: The Belief You'll Be Okay

The foundation of effortless confidence is **believing you'll be okay no matter what happens.** Develop this belief by living it—doing the social things you fear, proving to yourself you survive.

## Living the Truth
In Mad Men's final seasons, Don increasingly tells hard truths at personal cost: confessing his orphaned childhood to Hershey (losing the account and getting fired), admitting moral failings to colleagues. By accepting the worst-case scenario and living through it, he internalized genuine confidence. **When you've already lived your worst fear, confidence becomes natural.**

## Key Insight
Respect comes from demonstrating you're not controlled by external circumstances—not through aggressiveness, but through calm, strategic composure grounded in genuine self-belief.
