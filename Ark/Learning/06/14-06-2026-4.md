**Index Funds vs ETFs — Key Takeaways**

The video argues these two are nearly identical in many ways, but small differences matter long-term.

**Index Funds** are passively managed baskets of stocks that track a market index (e.g. S&P 500). Key traits:

- Priced once per day at end-of-day NAV (net asset value) — no intraday trading
- Very low fees: 0.02%–0.20% expense ratio
- In some countries (e.g. Spain), you can switch between index funds without triggering a capital gains tax event — useful for long-term rebalancing
- Offered by providers like Vanguard, Fidelity, BlackRock

**ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds)** work similarly but trade on stock exchanges like regular shares — price fluctuates throughout the day. Key traits:

- More flexibility — buy/sell any time during market hours
- Can be passive (tracking an index) or actively managed
- Come in two dividend flavours: distributing (pays cash quarterly) or accumulating (automatically reinvests dividends back into the fund)
- The VOO (Vanguard S&P 500 ETF) is used as an example — 0.03% expense ratio, full replication, distributing

**Three common misconceptions busted:**

1. ETFs aren't automatically better than index funds — intraday trading flexibility is irrelevant for long-term investors
2. Actively managed ETFs don't reliably beat the market — only ~3 in 10 active funds outperform over the long run (Morningstar)
3. Diversified ≠ risk-free — an ETF tracking volatile sectors like tech or crypto can swing just as hard as individual stocks

**Bottom line:** The right choice depends on your country's tax rules and how you invest. Either way, what matters most is staying in the market, keeping fees low, and thinking long-term.
