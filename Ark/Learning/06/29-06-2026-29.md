**The Hook & Core Thesis**

Luxury brands jacked prices far past inflation without adding quality or innovation — and shoppers revolted. The industry lost 50 million consumers in two years, and the damage was largely self-inflicted by **[[brand fatigue]]**, price gouging, and falling craftsmanship.

---
**Key Arguments Checklist**

- **The price hikes were the core self-own** — far above inflation, with no matching innovation or quality.
- **Luxury defies normal supply/demand** — higher prices usually *raise* desire ([[Veblen good]]s), but the brands pushed too far.
- **Post-COVID "revenge spending" spiked then broke** — the unprecedented price jumps that followed turned consumers off.
- **Gen Z went vintage, second-hand, and boutique** instead.
- **China cooled** — younger consumers favor local/niche brands, and the government actively discourages flashy luxury.
- **Quality control slipped** at top houses, accelerating the exodus.
- **The winners** are scarcity players (Hermès) and quiet/small brands (Miu Miu, The Row); rich consumers shifted to luxury *experiences*.

---
**High-Yield Assets**

- **The flagship example:** **[[Chanel]]** Medium 2.55 leather flap bag — **$5,800 (2019) → $10,800 (2025)**, an **86% increase**, not from inflation.
- **Market collapse:** the luxury industry **lost 50 million consumers in two years**; only **one third** of the sector is still growing — despite **58 million** households earning $1M+ (2023).
- **[[Veblen good]]s / the Veblen Goods Curve** — economist **[[Thorstein Veblen]]**: for luxury, demand *rises* with price because the high price signals rarity and quality.
- **"Revenge spending"** — post-COVID savings + boredom drove a 2021 demand surge; brands then raised prices to an unprecedented degree, after which US and China spending slowed.
- **Price-hike data:** luxury prices rose **61% in the US from 2019–2024** (vs ~2% inflation pre-pandemic, when hikes ran 5–7%/yr). Examples: **Canada Goose** parka **$995 → $1,650 (+66%)**; **Prada Galleria Saffiano** bag **+111%**.
- **[[Brand fatigue]]** — coined by **[[Michael Kors]]** at New York Fashion Week; driven by logo-loudness, e.g. **[[Louis Vuitton]]**'s monogram recycled from the 1932 Noé bag through a 2024 iPhone 16 Pro Max case. Counterfeits worsened it.
- **Quality misses** — cited at **[[Rolex]]** (uneven, misaligned stitching, plastic finish) and others; quality dropped as prices soared.
- **Where the money went:**
  - **Second-hand:** the resale luxury market hit **$47 billion in 2023** (+4%), led by Gen Z; **[[Mercari]]** (Japan) had ~$6.4B GMV, ~70% from the US.
  - **Quiet luxury:** **[[The Row]]** (Mary-Kate & Ashley Olsen, 2008) — quality, minimalism, no logos.
  - **[[Miu Miu]]** (Prada's sister brand, 1992) — sales **+105%**, helping **[[Prada]]** Group grow 18% in Q3 2024, while **[[Gucci]]** struggled.
- **The scarcity winner:** **[[Hermès]]** earned **$12B+** in the first three quarters of 2024 (+14% YoY, +7% in Asia ex-Japan) by deliberately limiting supply — the Birkin is "a game you'll never win."
- **Luxury experiences:** private-island rentals (**$50,000–$1M/night**) and Switzerland's **Glacier Express VIP train** ($540/ticket, consistently sold out).

---
**Case Studies / Anecdotes**

- **The Birkin "game"** — Hermès won't just sell you a bag; you have to build a purchase history first, turning acquisition into a game most never win — the model behind its outperformance.
- **The Louis Vuitton monogram lineage** — the same iconic print from the 1932 Noé bag → Neverfull (2007) → 2013/2019 pouches → a 2024 iPhone case, used to illustrate decades of logo recycling feeding brand fatigue.
- **"Luxury money is like matter and energy"** — it can't be created or destroyed; the wealthy didn't lose money, they just redirected it from handbags to experiences.

---
**The "So What?" Factor**

The takeaway is a cautionary business lesson: raising prices far beyond value — without innovation or quality — erodes even the strongest brands, especially with a value-conscious, anti-logo Gen Z. Watch where demand is flowing: scarcity (Hermès), quiet luxury (The Row, Miu Miu), and second-hand resale are winning, while logo-loud price-gougers are bleeding customers. For a shopper, it's a nudge toward vintage, boutique, and quality-over-logo; for a brand, proof that pricing power has limits.
