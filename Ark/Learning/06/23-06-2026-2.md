**The Seven Types**

**1. Gossips** — People who talk about others behind their backs. If they're doing it about others around you, they'll do it about you when you're gone. Interrupt it early: "I don't want to hear that." Listening to gossip either pulls you into it or plants negative feelings about the person being discussed.

**2. Jealous haters** — People who try to devalue you or shut down your wins. Paradoxically, they often actually want to see you succeed — they just don't know how to express it. The reframe: critics make you do it better; haters make you do it faster. Recognise them by how they respond to your good news — instant minimisation or dismissal.

**3. Time wasters** — People unaware of time, who ramble on irrelevant topics or ask for your attention without purpose. Often not intentional. Kindly redirect: ask them for the bottom line, let them know you have important things to attend to and will circle back.

**4. Money wrappers** — People who consistently ask for things, disappear when the bill arrives, or spend your money without reciprocating. Check your statements: who grabbed your money? Are you buying things for people who need it, or just because they're asking?

**5. Oversensitive people** — People carrying excessive emotional baggage who feel perpetually attacked. They can exist in your life but shouldn't have a large role if you have ambitious goals. Their emotional demands consume the bandwidth that direction requires.

**6. Excuse makers** — People who always have reasons why something won't work, who are always tired, broke, or unavailable. Sometimes they're protecting you from their own fear — but that fear is often illegitimate. Learn to identify the excuse, acknowledge it, and dismiss it if the action is still right for you.

**7. Miseducated people** — Not uneducated, but carrying wrong beliefs about what success requires. Credentials and degrees don't immunise against this. Watch for wolves in sheep's clothing: frenemies who have the language of support but the intent of obstruction.

**The Summary Principle**

You can't always avoid these people — they show up as coworkers, family, old friends. The goal isn't avoidance so much as accurate categorisation: know whether someone is an ally or an acquaintance. Gravitate toward people who raise your ceiling. The company you keep shapes your trajectory more than almost any other input.
