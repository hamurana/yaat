**The Hook & Core Thesis**

One specific carbohydrate — [[resistant starch]] — actively reduces the two most dangerous kinds of body fat (liver fat and visceral fat), and you can manufacture it at home for free by cooling and reheating ordinary starchy foods. It's not about a powder or a magic number; it's about choosing the resistant version of carbs you already eat.

---
**Key Arguments Checklist**

- **Two hidden fats do the most damage:** [[visceral fat]] (packed around your organs) and liver fat — both drive type 2 diabetes and heart disease.
- **[[Resistant starch]] behaves like fiber, not starch.** It resists digestion in the small intestine and feeds gut bacteria instead of spiking blood sugar.
- **It works even without weight loss.** Benefits trace back to gut bacteria via the [[gut-liver axis]], not just calories.
- **Refined starch is the opposite:** rapid glucose spike → big insulin surge → insulin is a storage hormone that parks fat in the liver and around organs.
- **You can create resistant starch at home** by cooking then cooling starchy foods ([[retrogradation]]).
- **Keto works but isn't sustainable** for most people; a smaller, repeatable habit beats an extreme diet you'll quit.
- **The real lesson over the number:** reduce carb frequency, and when you do eat carbs, make them resistant or whole.

---
**High-Yield Assets**

- **The dose in the research:** headline studies used **~40 grams of resistant starch per day**, delivered as a powder (easier to measure in a trial).
- **Key study:** a **4-month randomized controlled trial in *[[Cell Metabolism]]* (2023)**, ~**200 people** with fatty liver disease given resistant starch vs. an identical-looking ordinary starch at ~40g/day. The resistant-starch group saw a real drop in liver fat plus improved liver enzymes — **much of it without weight loss**.
- **Separate trial:** several weeks of resistant starch significantly reduced deep **visceral fat**.
- **Mechanism:** resistant starch reaches the large intestine intact, where gut bacteria ferment it into **[[short-chain fatty acids]]**, the most important being **[[butyrate]]**.
- **[[Retrogradation]]:** cooking then cooling potatoes, rice, and pasta re-crystallizes digestible starch into resistant starch — cold potato salad or day-old refrigerated rice/pasta has more than the same food eaten hot.
- **The bread trick:** freezing bread then toasting it from frozen lowers the blood-sugar response vs. fresh bread (shown across several studies).
- **No-prep sources:** **beans, lentils, chickpeas** (among the best — a reason the longest-living populations eat so many), **slightly under-ripe green bananas**, intact whole grains like **oats and barley** (cold overnight oats give more), and **cashews**.
- **Whole grains named:** **quinoa**, **bulgur**.

---
**Case Studies / Anecdotes**

- **His own kitchen routine:** when the household buys a big seeded fermented sourdough, they chop it up immediately and freeze it, then always toast or defrost slices as needed.
- **Self-experiment:** while wearing a blood-sugar monitor for a month, he confirmed his own glucose response to bread was noticeably lower when it had been frozen and toasted versus eaten fresh.
- **The "balanced plate" build:** pair resistant carbs with plenty of veg, real crunch, a good protein source, and healthy fats — then a short walk afterward lets muscles mop up glucose before it does harm.

---
**The "So What?" Factor**

Don't chase 40 grams of powder — chase the principle. Eat carbs less often, and when you do, pick resistant or whole-food sources: batch-cook rice and potatoes and refrigerate them, freeze your bread and toast from frozen, and lean on beans, lentils, and chickpeas. The goal is to reduce the frequency and intensity of glucose spikes, which underpin the heart attacks, strokes, dementia, and cancers worth avoiding. Small, repeatable swaps — not dogma — compound into a big effect over time.
