**Before You Buy an ETF: 5 Critical Evaluation Factors**

An ETF (exchange-traded fund) is a basket of investments — stocks, bonds, or other securities — that trades on an exchange like a stock. It combines the diversification of a mutual fund with intraday trading flexibility. The video uses Vanguard's **VFV** (S&P 500 ETF, TSX) as a running example throughout.

**Key concept: NAV vs. Market Price**
NAV (Net Asset Value) = total holdings value ÷ units outstanding — the "true" per-unit worth. During trading hours, the market price fluctuates via supply and demand; it settles back near NAV by end of day. A tight bid-ask spread (VFV: 0.02%) signals high liquidity and fair pricing.

---

**Factor 1 — Risk & Volatility**
Every ETF carries an official risk rating (low → high), but go deeper into the metrics: standard deviation measures how much returns vary year to year (VFV: 11.76%), beta shows sensitivity to the benchmark (VFV: 1.00 = moves 1:1 with the S&P 500), and R-squared confirms tracking fidelity (VFV: 1.00 = perfect). Always check the worst 3-month return (VFV: **−13.68%**, turning $1,000 into $863) — if you can't hold through that without panic-selling, the risk level is too high. Full risk detail is in the provider's prospectus.

**Factor 2 — Track Record**
Compare returns across 1-year, 3-year, 10-year, and since-inception timeframes. VFV: 1-year +20.73%, 3-year annualised +24.84%, 10-year +15.39%. $1,000 invested at inception → **$6,561** by April 2025 (16.25% CAGR). Tight alignment with the benchmark return is what you want from an index fund. Past performance doesn't guarantee future results, but consistent benchmark tracking is a positive signal.

**Factor 3 — What You're Actually Buying**
Look at the index tracked and the top 10 holdings. VFV tracks the S&P 500 (large-cap US equities) and holds 505 companies. As of April 2025, top 5: Apple 6.8%, Microsoft 6.2%, Nvidia 5.6%, Amazon 3.7%, Alphabet 3.6% — top 10 together = 35.6% of the fund. Holdings shift with market values over time (Nvidia grew to 8.0% by September). Understanding the composition tells you how the fund will behave under different market conditions.

**Factor 4 — Costs (MER)**
The Management Expense Ratio is the annual fee deducted as a percentage of assets. VFV MER: **0.09%** (90¢ per year per $1,000). This is extremely low vs. actively managed funds (often 2%+). A seemingly small 0.41% difference between two similar ETFs compounds into thousands of dollars of lost growth over 30 years. Also check the Trading Expense Ratio (VFV: 0.00%). Watch for expense waivers — without them the MER could be higher.

**Factor 5 — Sector Allocation**
The sector breakdown reveals your economic exposure. VFV: Information Technology 30.4% (grew to 34.8% by September), Financials 14.4%, Healthcare 10.8%, Consumer Discretionary 10.4%, Communication Services 9.3%, Industrials 8.6%, Consumer Staples 6.2%, Energy 3.2%, Utilities 2.6%, Real Estate 2.2%, Materials 2.0%. The heavy tech weighting means a tech correction hits this fund hard. If you already hold tech stocks, this amplifies concentration risk; if you want tech exposure, the allocation may be intentional.

---

**Where to Find the Data**
ETF providers are legally required to publish two documents: *ETF Facts* (regulatory — ticker, exchange, currency, benchmark, account eligibility) and *Fund Facts / Fact Sheet* (detailed — performance, top holdings, sector weights, fees). Download and read both before investing.

**Bottom line:** Low-cost, passively managed index ETFs suit most long-term investors. Run the full 5-factor check every time: risk tolerance vs. volatility, track record vs. benchmark, holdings composition, MER, and sector exposure relative to your overall portfolio.
