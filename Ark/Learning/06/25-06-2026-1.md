## What Makes a Gentlemanly Haircut

A gentleman's haircut should project sophistication, respectability, and professionalism — clean, polished, and timeless. The key balance: not so high-maintenance it consumes your morning, not so utilitarian it signals you've given up. The anchor criteria: does it look self-respecting without veering thuggish or cartoonish?

---

## The 6 Styles

**1. Classic Side Part (The Gentleman's Cut)**
The definitive choice — scissors only, no clippers. Seen on Cary Grant, Sean Connery, Don Draper, and Eggsy. Two variants:
- *Old-school:* longer length, slicked down with pomade (shiny, 50s–60s feel)
- *Modern:* shorter, matte wax or clay (George Clooney)

The side part frames the face the way a navy suit frames an outfit — works on nearly everyone, versatile across hair types. Described as the "navy suit of men's hairstyles."

**2. Slicked Back**
Combed straight back, no part. Associated with Gordon Gecko, Patrick Bateman — now resurging with finance bro culture. Requires a chiseled face and a full head of hair. Not recommended if your hairline is receding; the style only amplifies it.

**3. Curtains / "Old Money" Hairstyle**
Free-flowing bangs falling over the forehead. Popular in the 90s (DiCaprio, Depp), revived by Timothée Chalamet — actually traces back to Oscar Wilde. Youthful and rebellious, looks good dressed up, but signals "posh boy" more than professional. High maintenance (blow dryer, sea salt spray required). Best for young men not yet concerned with authority.

**4. Ivy League (Harvard / Princeton)**
A crew cut with slightly longer top. Lower maintenance than a side part while still being more sophisticated than a buzz cut. Matt Damon, Ryan Reynolds, Ryan Gosling, Brad Pitt territory. Can be styled front-back or down — two looks from one cut. Works even on thinner hair. Downside: unremarkable. Won't win compliments, but genuinely timeless and modern.

**5. Pompadour**
Heavy volume above the forehead — roots in James Dean and Elvis. Impressive but high maintenance: blow dryer, multiple products required. Easy to tip into cartoonish territory. Best version: James Dean's natural-looking volume. A toned-down variant resembles Harvey Specter in *Suits*. Still carries a youthful, slightly rebellious spirit.

**6. High and Tight**
Military-origin cut: very short or shaved sides blended into a longer top. Popularized in civilian life through *Peaky Blinders*. Warning: avoid an extreme disconnected undercut — a gradual fade looks far more refined. Very low daily maintenance (product on top, done), but requires frequent barber visits to keep sides sharp. People will assume military or law enforcement.

## Choosing Your Style

Four factors determine the right cut:
1. **Hair type** — thickness, texture, natural volume
2. **Lifestyle** — how much morning maintenance you'll actually do
3. **Facial features** — chiseled vs. soft, hairline health
4. **Professional context** — wanting authority (side part, Ivy League) vs. youthful energy (curtains, pompadour)

The side part and Ivy League are the most universally reliable. The slicked-back and pompadour demand specific face types and full hair. The high and tight is clean but carries military connotations. Curtains work young but undermine professional credibility.
