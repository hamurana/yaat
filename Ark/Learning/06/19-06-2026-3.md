## The Problem with Market Cap Alone
Two companies with identical market caps can represent vastly different values if one carries heavy debt while the other holds substantial cash. Market cap alone is misleading because it ignores financial liabilities and assets.

## Market Capitalization (Market Cap)
**Definition:** Total value of company equity
**Formula:** Share Price × Total Outstanding Shares

**Example:** 10 million shares at $50 each = $500 million market cap

Market cap gives a quick snapshot of investor sentiment but tells only part of the story. It's used to weight index funds like the S&P 500, where larger companies have more influence.

## Enterprise Value (EV) — The Complete Picture
**Definition:** What it would actually cost to acquire an entire business
**Formula:** Market Cap + Total Debt − Cash

Enterprise value reflects the true acquisition cost because buyers assume existing debt and gain existing cash reserves.

## Real-World Comparison
- **Company A:** $500M market cap + $200M debt − $50M cash = **$650M EV**
- **Company B:** $500M market cap + $0 debt − $100M cash = **$400M EV**

Though both have identical market caps, Company A is actually more expensive due to debt burden, while Company B appears financially healthier.

## What High vs. Low EV Means

**When EV > Market Cap:** The company carries significant debt, meaning acquisition would involve taking on financial obligations beyond just buying stock.

**When EV < Market Cap:** The company has strong cash reserves and minimal debt, suggesting financial stability and flexibility for investments or expansion.

## Understanding the Full Picture
High debt or excess cash reveals strategic intent: Is the company investing in growth, struggling financially, or preparing for expansion? Numbers alone don't tell the full story.

## EV Multiples — How Investors Use It

**EV/EBITDA Ratio**
- Compares enterprise value to earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization
- Lower ratio = undervalued (more earnings for lower price)
- Higher ratio = potentially overvalued
- Popular metric among professional investors

**EV/Revenue Ratio**
- Shows how much paid per dollar of company sales
- Lower ratio = lower valuation per sales dollar
- Useful for comparing unprofitable growth companies
- Doesn't account for profit margins or cash flow, so use alongside other metrics

## Why This Matters for Investors
Professional investors and analysts prefer enterprise value because it provides a fuller, more accurate picture of what a company actually costs. Market cap alone can lead to poor investment decisions by ignoring a company's true financial obligations and assets.
