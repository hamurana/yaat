**Dividend Stocks vs Growth Stocks — Key Takeaways**

The video argues that neither style is inherently superior — both can build wealth or destroy it, depending on how you use them.

**Dividend Stocks** pay you a share of profits regularly (usually quarterly). The main categories are REITs (like owning property without the headaches), consumer staples (Coca-Cola, P&G — recession-resistant), financials (JP Morgan, Goldman Sachs), and energy (Exxon, Chevron). One key warning: a suspiciously high yield (6–7%+) can be a red flag — it may signal a struggling company trying to attract investors, and a falling stock price will wipe out any yield advantage.

**Growth Stocks** (Tesla, Amazon, Nvidia) pay no dividends at all — every dollar goes back into the business. The bet is purely on future price appreciation. Riskier with smaller companies, but historically the biggest wealth creators over long time horizons. Microsoft and Google were once growth stocks before maturing into dividend payers.

**The hidden trap — taxes:** Dividend investors get taxed on income each year, which erodes compounding. Growth stocks defer taxes until you sell. One solution mentioned is **accumulation ETFs**, which automatically reinvest dividends internally so you avoid the annual tax drag.

**The data:** Since 1940, roughly 34% of S&P 500 total returns have come from dividends. Meanwhile, between 2010 and 2020, the S&P soared 400%+ largely on tech growth. Both approaches have strong track records.

**Bottom line:** Know your goals, timeline, and risk tolerance — and consider blending both styles rather than picking a side.
