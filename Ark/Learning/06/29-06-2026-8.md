**The Hook & Core Thesis**

The Old Hollywood hairstyle — a natural-length, side-parted, pomade-slicked cut — is a timeless, masculine alternative to fleeting trends like the mullet or zoomer bangs. It's far easier to achieve than men assume.

---
**Key Arguments Checklist**

- **Three defining features:** a slick, shiny oil-based pomade; natural length (no clippers, undercut, or fade); and usually a side parting (or slicked straight back).
- **It's a scissor-only cut.** Choose a hair stylist or traditional barber, not a clipper-only shop "blaring rap music."
- **Length matters.** Medium length — too short won't comb into clean lines, too long looks greasy and unkempt.
- **The product is non-negotiable: oil-based pomade**, never water-based, for an authentic look.
- **Best on shown faces.** The style reveals the full face, so it flatters naturally handsome, sharply defined features.
- **It's a mature, congruent look** for men who dress traditionally (suits and ties), though it pairs with modern clothes for contrast.

---
**High-Yield Assets**

- **Era:** the predominant style for well-to-do men from the **1920s to the late 1960s** (golden age of Hollywood).
- **Real-life icons:** [[Cary Grant]], [[Humphrey Bogart]] (who slicked straight back), [[James Stewart]].
- **Fictional icons:** [[Don Draper]] (Mad Men), [[Sean Connery]]'s James Bond.
- **What to tell your barber:** ask for a "classic side part scissor cut," show photos of Don Draper or Cary Grant, and say you want to look like "a leading man from the 1940s."
- **Comb:** a simple Wahl pocket comb.
- **Pomade brands:** vintage survivors [[Black and White]], [[Murray's]], [[Royal Crown]] — but the host's top pick (unsponsored) is **[[Suavecito]]** oil-based pomade (slick without excess grease; specifically the oil-based, not water-based, tin).
- **Product note:** oil pomade won't fully shampoo out in one wash — leave it in and top up; hair stays combable (not dry/crusty) all day.
- **The barbershop visited:** Merchant & Rhoades in Chicago (described as the city's most luxurious).

---
**Case Studies / Anecdotes**

- **The host's own switch** — moved from a pompadour to this style because it's quicker and easier in the morning; his hair was already the right length.
- **Pete vs. Don** — both Mad Men characters wear similar hair, "but Pete looks like a dog and Don looks cool," illustrating that features and execution matter.
- **Hairline reassurance** — works across hairlines: Don Draper (and the host) have high hairlines / large foreheads, while a low hairline with a widow's peak also suits it — even men losing some hair.

---
**The "So What?" Factor**

Drop the trend-chasing and build one timeless look. Grow to medium length, find a scissor-skilled barber, ask for a "1940s leading man" side-part cut, and style with an oil-based pomade (Suavecito recommended) over a Wahl comb. It projects confidence and success and skews mature — decide if that's the image you want, then pair it with traditional or modern clothes to taste.
