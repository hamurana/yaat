**Tip 1 — Realize There's So Much Money Out There**

The foundational mindset shift: money is everywhere, not scarce. Instead of resenting the wealthy, study them. Most successful businesses aren't brand new ideas — they're existing ideas with a twist (Uber didn't invent taxis; it made them cheaper and easier). The crypto boom of 2021 created fortunes not from the technology itself but from people noticing where money was flowing and positioning themselves accordingly. Once you believe money is abundant, you stop making excuses and start spotting opportunities.

**Tip 2 — Don't Focus on Saving**

Obsessive saving locks up capital you need for testing business ideas and side hustles — the real wealth builders in early life. Having a 6-month emergency fund is sensible, but hoarding cash beyond that gives money too much power over you. The path to early wealth is combining the "fast lane" (income-generating hustle and business) with the "slow lane" (consistent long-term investing). Don't deprive yourself of small joys to save $50; don't blow your money on pointless things either. Use money strategically.

**Tip 3 — Quit Drinking**

Alcohol is a depressant. Numbing your brain and body when they're at peak performance is a waste of your greatest asset: youth. Beyond the health cost, the financial and time cost adds up fast. The "right of passage" framing is just following what most people do — and following what most people do gets you what most people have.

**Tip 4 — Buy a Luxury Watch**

Three reasons: (1) It opens conversations with wealthy, successful people — a social networking tool. (2) Certain watches (Rolex Submariner, Daytona, GMT Master II) hold or appreciate in value — they function as an asset, unlike most consumer goods. (3) Wealth is portable in watch form across borders in a way cash isn't. Build a relationship with an authorized dealer early; retail access to premium Rolex pieces becomes harder over time.

**Tip 5 — Destroy Your Ego**

Arrogance prevents learning. The people who can't improve are those too proud to take feedback from someone more successful than them. Separate pride (healthy) from ego (blocking). Self-reflect deeply on weaknesses and form a plan to address them. Free education (YouTube, books) covers virtually every skill gap. Skills compound — improving communication, business acumen, or storytelling pays dividends across every area of life.

**Tip 6 — Build a Credit Score**

Without a trust fund, leveraging debt is one of the fastest paths to wealth creation — but only with good credit. "Never a borrower nor a lender be" is advice designed to keep people in their class, not lift them out of it. Three rules: (1) Pay on time — always; (2) Keep utilization below 30%; (3) Don't apply for too much credit at once. Start with a phone contract in your name; get a credit card at 18. A good credit score takes years to build and can be destroyed in one wrong move.

**Tip 7 — Follow the Money**

Passion alone doesn't generate wealth — but passion plus market demand does. If your passion doesn't pay, flip the script: apply your skills toward a version of your passion that does. Example: Rick Shields wanted to be a PGA pro, wasn't good enough, but combined his golf talent with video and became the world's most subscribed golf YouTuber. Find where money is already flowing and position yourself in that stream with skills you enjoy.

**Tip 8 — Become a Quitter**

The sunk cost fallacy is real: people stay in failing projects, bad investments, and wrong paths because they've already put time or money in. The money is gone regardless. Cut losses early and move resources to what's working. The ability to quit what isn't working — and do it without emotional attachment — is a core skill of wealth-builders.
