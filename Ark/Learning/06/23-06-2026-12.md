**The 15–20 Year Window**

Brain scans of people in their 40s and 50s who go on to develop Alzheimer's already show amyloid plaques building up — sometimes decades before the first symptom. The disease process doesn't begin in your 70s. This is also why catching early signs matters: every year of lifestyle intervention is a year the disease has less ground to take.

In Alzheimer's, two proteins accumulate: amyloid (forms plaques between neurons) and tau (tangles inside neurons, collapsing them from within). They damage neural connections progressively — one neighbourhood of the brain at a time.

**The Six Signs**

**1. Repetitive short-term memory loss**  
Not occasional forgetfulness, but asking the same question within the same conversation with no memory of asking before. Telling the same story at lunch they told at breakfast. Forgetting the kettle boiled and boiling it again. The hippocampus — the brain's memory recorder — is typically the first structure damaged. Older memories (stored when the hippocampus was healthy) remain vivid; new ones don't get written down. Hence: remembers their wedding from 40 years ago but can't recall breakfast.

**2. Difficulty with familiar tasks**  
Struggling with a recipe made hundreds of times. Getting lost on a route driven for 30 years. Making errors with household bills that were always managed correctly. Studies show everyday task performance — especially financial management — starts slipping years before any formal diagnosis.

**3. Personality and mood shifts**  
Chatty people becoming withdrawn. Social hosts cancelling plans. A previously gentle person becoming irritable or anxious about leaving home. Families often explain this as "they're just a bit different now." There is growing research interest in *mild behavioral impairment* — persistent, new-onset changes in mood or behavior later in life that may be early neurological footprints.

**4. Getting lost in familiar places**  
The spatial navigation system sits adjacent to the hippocampus and is often damaged around the same time — sometimes before memory loss appears. Hesitating at familiar junctions, wrong turns to a friend's house visited for years. Researchers have built video-game navigation tests that predict Alzheimer's risk in people in their 40s and 50s.

**5. Struggling to find words**  
Pausing mid-sentence more frequently for words that should be instantly accessible. Replacing specific words with vague descriptions: "that thing on your wrist" for watch, "the thing you boil" for kettle. A 2021 study found subtle language changes in writing samples predicted Alzheimer's more than 7 years before diagnosis.

**6. Impaired judgment**  
Sending money to phone scammers. Taking medication twice or missing doses (a common A&E presentation). Leaving the cooker or gas on. Personal hygiene slipping in someone previously meticulous. This reflects damage to the prefrontal cortex — the brain's executive function centre managing planning, impulse control, and consequence awareness. Unexplained financial losses in the years before diagnosis are one of the strongest warning signs.

**What To Do**

- One or two signs alone are usually not dementia — sleep problems, depression, thyroid disorders, vitamin deficiencies, and medication side effects can all cause similar symptoms
- Write down specific observed changes and bring a family member to the GP appointment (their external view is often more revealing)
- A thorough assessment includes blood tests, medication review, and physical examination before any referral to a memory clinic
- Lifestyle factors (diet, movement, sleep, blood pressure control) have real, evidence-backed potential to slow progression — the doctor plans a separate video on this specifically
