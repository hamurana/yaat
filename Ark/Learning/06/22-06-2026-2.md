**What Is an ETF?**

An ETF (exchange-traded fund) is a basket of investments — stocks, bonds, or other securities — packaged into a single unit that trades on a stock exchange like a regular stock. It combines the diversification of mutual funds with the trading flexibility of individual stocks. The example used throughout is VFV, the Vanguard S&P 500 Index ETF, which holds 505 companies and trades on the Toronto Stock Exchange.

**Net Asset Value vs. Market Price**

Every ETF has two prices: NAV (net asset value), calculated at end of day and representing the actual worth of its holdings, and the market price, driven by supply and demand during trading hours. For a well-run ETF like VFV, these track very closely — the bid-ask spread was just 0.02%, meaning you buy and sell very near the true underlying value.

**The Five Factors to Evaluate Before Buying**

**1. Risk and Volatility**
Every ETF carries a risk rating from low to high. Dig deeper into volatility metrics: standard deviation (how much returns vary), beta (how closely it tracks its benchmark — 1.00 means moves in lockstep), and R-squared (what % of movements are explained by the benchmark). Also check worst-case scenarios: VFV's worst 3-month return was -13.68%, turning $1,000 into $863. Ask honestly if you can hold through that.

**2. Track Record**
Review performance across multiple timeframes. VFV returned 35.24% in 2024, 27.64% in 2021, but dropped 12.69% in 2022. A $1,000 investment at inception would have grown to $6,561 by April 2025 — a 16.25% compound annual return. Past performance doesn't guarantee future results, but it shows whether the fund actually tracks its benchmark.

**3. What the ETF Owns**
Read the fund facts carefully. VFV tracks the S&P 500 large-cap US market. Top holdings as of April 2025: Apple (6.8%), Microsoft (6.2%), Nvidia (5.6%), Amazon (3.7%), Alphabet (3.6%). The top 10 represent 35.6% of the total fund. Holdings shift with market values — Nvidia grew to 8.0% by September, reflecting the tech surge.

**4. Fees (MER)**
Management expense ratio is the silent wealth killer. VFV's MER is just 0.09% — 90 cents per year on every $1,000 invested. Actively managed funds often charge 2% or more. A 0.41% difference between two similar ETFs compounds to thousands of dollars lost over 30 years. Always compare MERs before committing.

**5. Sector Allocation**
Where your money is concentrated determines your exposure to economic cycles. VFV's breakdown: Information Technology 30.4%, Financials 14.4%, Healthcare 10.8%, Consumer Discretionary 10.4%, Communication Services 9.3%. Tech grew to 34.8% by September. If you already own tech stocks, this ETF concentrates that exposure further. A tech correction would significantly impact the fund.

**Where to Find This Information**

Two key documents: the ETF Facts (regulatory info: ticker, exchange, benchmark, account eligibility) and the Fund Facts/Fact Sheet (total net assets, performance, top holdings, sector weightings). Providers are required by law to make these available on their websites.
