**The Premise**

The creator makes videos about digital minimalism and still falls into doom-scrolling. He doesn't claim to be an expert — just someone actively trying. These 11 habits are ones he's personally implementing.

**The 11 Habits**

**1. Create empty space during the day**
Pick a daily window — even 10–15 minutes — with no phone, no music, no entertainment. Kitchen tasks or driving in silence work well. This provides a mental reset and grounds you in the present. It trains tolerance for stimulation-free time.

**2. Delete one high-dopamine app**
You may not notice the gap immediately, but over time you'll realize how much mental space that app was consuming. Deleting it creates friction — you won't re-download, re-sign in, and re-authenticate just to check it. At minimum, delete the app from your phone even if you keep the account; the friction alone is often enough to break the habit.

**3. Rewatch, replay, reuse instead of seeking novelty**
The addiction of YouTube Shorts and Reels is primarily novelty-seeking. Instead, return to favorite content, music, or shows with consistent tone rather than constantly chasing something new. The periods in the creator's life with more mindful content consumption were, in retrospect, happier periods overall.

**4. Keep your phone physically further away**
Obvious but underused. When your phone is in another room or your backpack, you don't scroll to fill idle moments. The walk to retrieve it gives you time to ask: why am I even going to get it? That pause alone often stops the reach. Leave the charger in the bedroom — the phone stays there.

**5. Do one activity at a time**
Modern brain rot trains constant attention-splitting: watching something while eating, watching two things simultaneously. Over time, the brain loses tolerance for slower moments. Deliberately doing one thing at a time makes days feel slower but better — tasks completed with more focus, pleasures enjoyed fully rather than half-noticed across multiple things.

**6. Stop treating boredom like an emergency**
We've lost the ability to just sit in a waiting room. The moment silence appears, we reach for a phone automatically. The challenge: catch yourself before you doom scroll without intention, and be okay with being bored. Entertainment has become impulsive — often not even enjoyed, just consumed because not consuming felt uncomfortable.

**7. Consume slower content on purpose**
Curate your subscriptions, your playlists, your library. Whatever you're consuming directly affects how you feel afterward. "Slower" doesn't mean boring — it means books, drawing, walking, music, lower-stimulation games. The content environment you're surrounded by shapes your baseline mood.

**8. Keep fewer windows open**
Finishing a task to completion before opening the next one. Simplifying to one or two browser tabs at a time makes focusing on the task at hand easier and reduces the mental overhead of context-switching.

**9. Have one offline hobby**
Something not connected to the internet, not monetized, just for disconnecting. Daily walks, gym, sketching, reading. Being engaged offline for the sake of enjoyment — not for content, not for an audience, not as a side hustle.

**10. Stop consuming background content all day**
Background content started as a productivity crutch ("at least I'm learning while doing chores") and gradually degraded into random entertainment in the background. The problem: everything starts to feel boring unless enhanced. An activity that doesn't require audio enhancement becomes intolerable. The goal is to be present in the task — not make the task tolerable.

**11. End nights without algorithms**
Don't let an app decide what you watch before sleeping. Be in control of the content — choose actively rather than following the recommendation queue. Nighttime scrolling is nearly never a good idea. The creator's preferred ending: watching a chosen show or reading, being in his own world. If you take one tip: spend tonight without your phone and notice how you feel the next day.
