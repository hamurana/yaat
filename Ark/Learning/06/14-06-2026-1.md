**How to Invest in ETFs for Beginners (Starting with $5,000)**

**What is an ETF?** An ETF (exchange-traded fund) is a basket of hundreds or thousands of companies in a single purchase — instant diversification. Unlike individual stocks (high risk, single company) or mutual funds (higher fees, actively managed), ETFs passively track an index like the S&P 500 and charge very low fees (0.03–0.3% per year vs 0.5–1.5% for mutual funds).

**3 Things to Check Before Buying an ETF**

1. **Expense ratio** — must be low (under 0.2%). This fee is charged regardless of performance.
2. **What it actually tracks** — don't trust the name; look at the underlying index and what's inside.
3. **Track record** — how has it historically performed?

- **VOO** (S&P 500) — 50% / $2,500 — the core anchor

**After Buying — Do Almost Nothing**

1. **Hold through drops** — the market _will_ fall 20–30% at some point; the ones who lose permanently are those who panic-sell.
2. **Auto-reinvest dividends** — set and forget; dividends buy more shares, compounding quietly in the background.
3. **Rebalance once a year** — check if allocations have drifted significantly and trim/add accordingly. Otherwise, close the app.

---

The core message: time in the market beats timing the market, and keeping costs low (low expense ratios) is just as important as picking the right funds.
