**The Hook & Core Thesis**

**[[Dan Go]]** wore four wearables at once for a week and they wildly disagreed — one read 81 minutes of deep sleep where two said 47. The real lesson: trust your trends and how you actually feel, treat every metric as "directionally correct" rather than gospel, and never let a screen override your own body.

---
**Key Arguments Checklist**

- **The deep-sleep numbers are guesses, not facts** — a 76% spread between devices on the metric people stress about most.
- **Once you know your trends, constant daily data stops informing and starts overriding you** — you feel great until the screen says you shouldn't.
- **Trust sleep *duration* and trends; ignore the stage-by-stage breakdown.** Never compare your deep-sleep number to a friend on a different brand.
- **Recovery / HRV from wrist and finger devices isn't accurate enough to make decisions off** — only act on extreme readings.
- **"Directionally correct" beats "perfectly accurate"** for steps and most metrics.
- **The devices can still transform your life** — awareness creates accountability — but no wearable is perfect.

---
**High-Yield Assets**

- **Devices tested (all self-bought, no sponsorship):** **[[Apple Watch]]**, **[[Whoop]]**, **[[Oura Ring]]**, and the new **[[Fitbit Air]]**. He *declined* a free Whoop unit offered on X to protect the results ("truth is always cheaper when you get the gear yourself"). A planned **[[Garmin]]** Fenix got lost in transit.
- **Sleep findings:** total sleep agreed within an hour. Deep sleep split into two camps — **Apple + Oura ~47 min**, **Fitbit + Whoop ~70–81 min** (a 76% spread). REM: Apple highest (~2 hrs), the other three ~95–100 min.
- **Accuracy reference:** a **[[Wall Street Journal]]** test against a real sleep lab found **Apple's sleep staging came closest to lab truth** — suggesting the higher numbers from other devices are flattering, not accurate.
- **Steps test vs a [[pedometer]]** (more accurate because it rides on the hip near your center of mass, not a busy wrist): **Apple Watch and Fitbit Air** got within a few hundred steps; **Whoop over-counted** (steps function still in beta); **Oura** tracked in line with Apple but weekly totals ran low because he kept taking the ring off.
- **Battery / form factor:** Apple Watch must be charged daily (its biggest weakness); the other three have great battery life. Fitbit Air is the thinnest/lightest; Whoop is bulkier and gets in the way when lifting.
- **App experience ranking:** **Whoop wins** (wall of data, AI daily summary, the **daily journal** that actually changes his behavior) → **Oura** second (calm, quiet, almost boring) → **Apple Health** third (numbers "sit quietly") → **Fitbit Air on Google Health** last (shallow data).
- **Subscriptions:** Apple and Fitbit Air need none; Oura and Whoop charge monthly. **Whoop cancellation took 10 minutes** — desktop-only, with a forced 5-minute exit survey.
- **Best source of truth for HRV/recovery:** a **chest strap**, not wrist/finger devices.

---
**Case Studies / Anecdotes**

- **The recovery score that argued with him** — after years on an Oura ring, he'd wake feeling "ready to run through a wall," check a recovery score of 67, and instantly feel worse. The number was winning the argument against how he actually felt.
- **The broken Apple Watch in Bali (January)** — after 5 years of daily tracking, his watch broke abroad and he didn't replace it. He stopped outsourcing how he felt to his wrist, stayed wearable-free for five months, and "life actually got better."
- **The "REM vs deep" irony** — the device stingiest on deep sleep turned out to be the most generous on REM, underlining how little the stage breakdowns can be trusted.

---
**The "So What?" Factor**

Buy the wearable that fits *you*: Apple Watch for real-time everything (if you'll charge it daily), Apple or Fitbit Air to avoid subscriptions, Whoop for the best app and deeper metrics (mind the cancellation hoops), Oura if you like the ring and don't need 24/7 wear. But don't let any of them override your judgment — use them to check trends and catch extreme readings, decide you feel great, and get on with your day. Dan's own plan: a dumb watch for the time, Apple Watch occasionally for phone-free gym sessions.
