## The Breaking Point
Most investors quit investing right before their money starts doing all the work. They lack understanding of the specific breaking point where compounding transforms from frustrating to miraculous. The **9-7-5-1 Rule** predicts when this happens.

## The Problem: Alex's Story
After three years of disciplined investing ($250/month into an index fund), Alex accumulated just $11,000. Frustrated, he questioned whether compounding even works, assuming it only benefits those already wealthy. This exact moment causes most people to abandon investing.

## How Compounding Actually Works
Compounding doesn't grow linearly—it curves almost flat for years, then turns nearly vertical. Picture a snowball rolling downhill: initially it picks up almost nothing, appearing useless. But given enough hill, the same process that felt worthless becomes transformative. **The snowball doesn't change; it just finally has enough mass to work.**

## The 9-7-5-1 Rule Explained

**9% Annual Return**
- S&P 500 has delivered ~9.6% compound annual growth since 1871 (with dividends)
- Real returns (inflation-adjusted): ~6.9%
- This is the baseline assumption for long-term investing
- Not guaranteed yearly, but reliable over rolling 7-year periods (every 7-year period since 1926 showed positive average returns)

**The Acceleration Pattern**
With $1,000/month investment at 7% annual return:
- **Years 1-6.5:** Reach first $100k (feels slow)
- **Years 6.5-11.1:** Next $100k takes only 4.6 years
- **Years 11.1-14.5:** Third $100k takes just 3.4 years
- **Total to $1M:** ~27.5 years

## The Mental Shift at $100k
At the beginning, your contributions drive growth. At $100k with 7% returns:
- Annual growth: $7,000
- Monthly passive growth: $583 (without adding a dollar)
- Like having a silent business partner working 24/7

This is when the game changes. Once you hit $200k, growth is $14k/year. At $500k, it's $35k/year. Your portfolio becomes semi-autonomous.

## Critical Insight: Non-Reactivity
In high-pressure situations, people typically give themselves only 30% as much time to respond as they'd give others. This applies to investment decisions during volatility. The key is slowing down:
- Market dropped 37% in 2008
- Market dropped 19% in 2022
- Yet every rolling 7-year period since 1926 ended positive

When you feel pressure to react, pause. The presentation perspective and decision-making improve dramatically with a deep breath and slowdown.

## The Real Game Changer
Most people miss the breaking point because they quit during the "flat part" of the curve. Understanding this specific framework makes patience no longer suffering—it becomes strategic.
