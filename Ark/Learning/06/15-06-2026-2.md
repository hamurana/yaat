**Your Blood Pressure Problem Isn't Salt — It's This**

Another Dr. Alex video, and a companion piece to the liver one. The central argument: 50 years of "eat less salt" advice hasn't fixed hypertension because it targets the wrong mechanism.

---

**How blood pressure actually works**

It's driven by three forces: cardiac output (how hard the heart pumps), blood volume (total fluid circulating), and vascular resistance (how constricted or relaxed the arteries are). All three must be addressed — not just one.

---

**Salt: the volume side**

Salt raises blood pressure by pulling water into the bloodstream, temporarily expanding plasma volume. In healthy people, the kidneys simply excrete the excess sodium and pressure normalises. Salt sensitivity varies widely — some people are highly affected, others barely at all. So salt is a _volume_ problem, not a disease driver in itself.

---

**Sugar: the hormonal side (the bigger villain)**

Sugar operates through three separate pathways:

1. **Insulin → sodium retention** — Insulin is a sodium-retaining hormone. Every time blood sugar spikes, insulin rises and signals the kidneys to hold onto sodium, raising blood volume — even on a low-salt diet.
2. **Sympathetic nervous system activation** — Chronically high insulin raises heart rate, boosts cardiac output, and constricts blood vessels.
3. **Fructose → uric acid → arterial stiffness** — Fructose is metabolised in the liver, generating uric acid as a byproduct. Uric acid impairs nitric oxide production, stiffening blood vessels and preventing them from relaxing.

---

**The metabolic pressure trap**

In people with insulin resistance (the majority of adults in the UK/US), insulin never fully drops — so kidneys stay locked in sodium-retention mode all day. Cutting salt barely helps because the hormonal system is overriding the volume signal. Meanwhile, high insulin also damages the endothelium, reducing nitric oxide and raising vascular resistance. Both arms of the blood pressure equation are being driven up simultaneously.

---

**Processed food: the real villain**

Ultra-processed food combines high sodium _and_ refined carbs — sodium causes the initial pressure spike, and sugar prevents the body from correcting it. Add in low potassium (which normally helps kidneys excrete sodium) and you have a perfect storm. Hypertension is overwhelmingly a disease of industrial food systems, not home cooking.

---

**What actually lowers blood pressure long-term**

Three biological levers need to be pulled:

- **Insulin drops** → kidneys release sodium normally again
- **Nitric oxide rises** → blood vessels relax and resistance falls
- **Renal sodium handling normalises** → kidneys respond to actual intake, not hormonal override

Practical interventions that work through these levers:

- **Exercise** (especially aerobic) — stimulates nitric oxide production that lasts hours after the session
- **Dietary fiber** — slows glucose absorption, flattens insulin spikes, allows kidneys to regulate sodium properly
- **Sleep** — restorative sleep activates the parasympathetic nervous system, lowering both cardiac output and vascular resistance

---

**On medication**

Blood pressure drugs work (ACE inhibitors, diuretics, calcium channel blockers) — but they control pressure without fixing the underlying cause. They work _best_ when combined with lifestyle changes that address the metabolic drivers. Patients who lose visceral fat, eat whole foods, and sleep well often need lower doses over time.

**Bottom line:** Hypertension is not a salt problem or a sugar problem in isolation — it's a processed food problem that breaks blood pressure regulation at multiple points simultaneously.
