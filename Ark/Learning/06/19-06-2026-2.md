## Overview
Restaurant behavior serves as a significant indicator of character, especially in social and romantic contexts. This video breaks down eight critical etiquette mistakes that damage first impressions and should be avoided at all costs.

## The Eight Mistakes

**1. Not Making a Reservation**
A gentleman never gambles with dinner plans. Even spontaneous meals warrant a phone call to ensure seating availability. Who makes the reservation depends on who controls the evening—the inviter assumes this responsibility. Book as far in advance as practical to avoid disappointment.

**2. Being Late**
Punctuality is non-negotiable for dates and business dinners. Budget 10-15 minutes of travel buffer and arrive early if possible. If delays are unavoidable, communicate immediately and honestly so your guest can decide whether to wait or reschedule.

**3. Not Standing to Greet Guests**
Always stand when guests arrive, regardless of their status. This gesture shows respect and genuine pleasure at their presence, enabling proper handshakes or embraces that sitting prevents.

**4. Failing to Offer Compliments**
Conversation naturally includes compliments. On dates, praise outfit choices or personality traits you've discovered. As a guest, compliment the host's restaurant selection or menu recommendations. This demonstrates attentiveness and warmth.

**5. Buttering Whole Bread Slices (Bread Etiquette)**
This British and European rule is overlooked in the US but remains more elegant: tear bread into bite-sized pieces, butter each piece individually, then eat. Leaving bite marks on buttered bread is visually unpleasant for others dining.

**6. Being Rude to Service Staff**
Perhaps the most relationship-damaging mistake. Rudeness to servers signals arrogance and hints at how you'll treat your date in the future. Always say thank you for food and drinks, ask how your server is doing when they introduce themselves, and leave generous tips. Staff rarely control food quality or pacing alone.

**7. Poor Menu Choices**
Avoid foods that look messy while eating (noodle soup, sloppy burgers) on dates. Skip strong-smelling options like garlic or onions for obvious reasons. When splitting costs, order mid-priced meals and avoid expensive drinks like champagne or rare whisky—respect others' budgets.

**8. Ignoring Unspoken Dynamics**
Be aware of the social context. Pay attention to the overall flow and dynamics of the evening, and adjust your behavior accordingly.

## Key Takeaway
Your restaurant behavior reveals character. These mistakes range from minor faux pas to evening-ruining behavior. Mastering these rules creates positive impressions in crucial social and romantic contexts.
