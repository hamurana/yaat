## The Study (Nature Communications, 2015)

Researcher Stephen O'Keefe took two groups of 20 people on two different continents and swapped their diets for two weeks:
- **Group A:** 20 African Americans from Pittsburgh (typical Western diet)
- **Group B:** 20 rural South Africans from KwaZulu-Natal (traditional African diet, ~55g fibre/day)

The groups ate each other's diets for exactly 14 days. Before and after, researchers performed colonoscopies on every participant — direct physical access to the bowel lining.

**Why these groups?** African Americans have bowel cancer rates ~13× higher than rural Africans (~65 per 100,000 vs. below 5 per 100,000). Both populations are genetically very similar. The gap can't be genetics — it has to be diet and lifestyle. Previous research had confirmed these low cancer rates weren't a measurement artifact: colonoscopies in rural South Africa found almost no precancerous polyps.

---

## What They Measured (and Found)

Researchers tracked four markers:
1. **Inflammation in the gut lining** — a major driver of cancer development
2. **Cell division rate in the bowel** — abnormally fast division signals rising cancer risk
3. **Gut microbiome composition** — protective vs. harmful bacterial species
4. **Butyrate levels** — a short-chain fatty acid produced by protective gut bacteria; one of the body's most powerful natural anti-cancer compounds

**Results after 14 days:**

| Group | Direction | Inflammation | Cell division | Protective bacteria | Butyrate |
|-------|-----------|-------------|---------------|---------------------|----------|
| African Americans (switched to African diet) | Improved | ↓ Down | ↓ Slowed | ↑ Increased | ↑ Rose |
| South Africans (switched to American diet) | Worsened | ↑ Up | ↑ Accelerated | ↓ Fell | ↓ Dropped |

Both groups mirrored each other in opposite directions — *in just two weeks*. The enormous cancer risk gap between the populations essentially flipped.

---

## Why Fibre Is the Mechanism

The single factor that best explained the results: **dietary fibre**.

- African diet: ~55g fibre/day from maize, beans, vegetables, whole plant foods
- American/UK diet: ~14g fibre/day from refined carbs and processed meats

Fibre is food for protective bacteria in the colon. When fibre arrives, those bacteria ferment it and produce **butyrate**, which:
- Is the preferred fuel for cells lining the colon — literally nourishing the bowel wall
- Reduces inflammation in the gut lining
- Encourages abnormal cells to die off rather than divide (anti-cancer)
- Strengthens the gut barrier (leaky gut drives chronic inflammation throughout the body)

Without fibre, protective bacteria starve, butyrate plummets, and the bowel lining becomes vulnerable to slow damage that leads to cancer. The South Africans on their normal diet were bathing their colons in butyrate daily. Two weeks of Western eating collapsed that protection completely.

---

## What This Means

Cancer risk is usually thought of in terms of decades — smoking → lung cancer takes 20+ years. This study showed the bowel's cancer risk markers move **in days**. The bowel is constantly responding to what you feed it. This is both alarming and hopeful: the damage works quickly in the wrong direction, but the recovery also works quickly in the right direction.

Two weeks of fibre-rich eating was enough to measurably lower cancer-related signals in people who had spent their entire lives eating a Western diet.

## The Practical Advice

**UK recommendation:** 30g fibre/day. Most people eat 14–20g. The gap is large and easy to close.

Key changes:
- Build meals around vegetables, whole grains, fruits, nuts, seeds, and fermented foods
- **Beans and lentils 4–5× per week** — particularly powerful because of resistant starch, which is one of the best ways to fuel butyrate production
- Swap white bread for seeded sourdough; swap white rice for quinoa, bulgur wheat, or mixed grains
- Have raw veg or a salad starter before main meals
- Snack on nuts and seeds instead of processed food
- Aim for **30 different plant foods per week** (herbs, spices, beans, grains, nuts, seeds, fruits, veg all count)

**On supplements:** Fibre supplements deliver one or two isolated fibre types. Your microbiome needs hundreds of different kinds. Supplements let you feel like you've ticked a box without changing your diet. No population with the world's lowest bowel cancer rates takes fibre supplements — they just eat real food. Save the money and change the food.

## The Key Insight

Your body doesn't wait decades to respond to what you eat. It starts changing now, this week, in either direction. 14 days is enough to start moving the needle — in either direction.
