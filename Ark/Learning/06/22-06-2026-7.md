**Seek Hard Things**

A good life isn't built from pleasure and money — it's built from challenge. Neuroscientist Gregory Burns distinguishes happiness (passive, requires little effort) from satisfaction (requires novelty and challenge). Satisfaction is what humans actually crave, and you only experience it when mastering something new. When you're in your 20s, treat yourself as an uncut diamond: you need the sharp edges of challenge to be cut into shape. By your 30s and 40s, you'll start to shine.

**The Sunk Cost Trap**

The "never quit" mentality has a dark side. Annie Duke's book *Quit* argues that strategic quitting is often the smarter move. The sunk cost fallacy — continuing with something because of past investment, not future potential — shows up everywhere: holding a bad investment hoping it recovers, staying in a relationship because "I've already put in three years." The time is already gone. The only question is what you do from now.

**Know Where You're Going**

"If you don't know where you're going, you'll wind up somewhere else." — Yogi Berra. Vague ambition without direction leads to climbing the ladder of success only to find it's against the wrong wall. A simple vision board — images of your ideal home, people who matter, places you want to go, who you want to become — forces you to articulate your direction. The goal isn't aesthetics; it's daily alignment. Sign off on it daily, affirming you'll take one step toward it.

**Build a Team of Winners**

Your environment shapes your trajectory more than your individual willpower. On winning teams: people push themselves, support each other, own both victories and failures, and treat setbacks as feedback rather than assigning blame. Seek out conferences, masterminds, and communities where people are doing ambitious things. The compound effect of being surrounded by people who raise your standards is enormous. Formalise the relationship — weekly check-ins, accountability partners, or paid coaching create skin in the game.

**Habits That Build Self-Respect**

- Read ingredient labels; cut refined sugar and high-fructose corn syrup from your diet
- Stop drinking soda (treat it as dessert, not a daily beverage)
- Cut back on alcohol — most bad decisions happen under the influence
- Live below your means: save aggressively, pay off high-interest debt first, cook at home to redirect spending toward loan payments
- Learn to say no by default — protect your priorities and explain your reasons when declining; people who matter will understand
- Be honest and direct with people; give criticism privately, praise publicly
- Write things down — carrying a notebook signals presence, commitment, and follow-through
- When you see something great in another person, say you're proud of them; be generous with honest compliments
