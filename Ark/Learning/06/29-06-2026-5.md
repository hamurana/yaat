**The Hook & Core Thesis**

The leanest people don't chase variety — they run a small, repeatable meal rotation so fat loss survives willpower. Eating roughly the same three meals daily is the underrated hack that got the host to 12% body fat at 46.

---
**Key Arguments Checklist**

- **Systems beat willpower.** You make ~200 food decisions a day, each one a "tax," and willpower is a depreciating asset that runs out by evening — exactly when discipline fails.
- **Repetition removes decisions.** Locking down breakfast, lunch, and weekday dinners means no thinking, so the decision budget is saved for meals that matter.
- **Front-load protein.** Getting ~50g of protein before 9 a.m. is called the single most important fat-loss and muscle-building move most people skip.
- **Fiber controls hunger.** Psyllium husk (soluble fiber) slows digestion, keeps you full for hours, and feeds gut bacteria — it appears in two of the three meals.
- **Control breeds flexibility.** Knowing exact calories/macros Monday–Friday lets you splurge on weekends without being "the weird dad measuring his food."
- **Don't overhaul — stack.** Adopt one meal for 7 days, then add a second, then a third.

---
**High-Yield Assets**

- **Context:** host is 46, trains 3×/week, has coached entrepreneurs and high performers for over a decade.
- **A 2026 study** tracked dieters by food variety: those who ate a *less* varied diet and stuck to it lost about **37% more weight** than those who kept switching.
- **Meal 1 — "Glop":** 300g low-fat Greek yogurt + 1 scoop whey protein powder + 1 tbsp psyllium husk + 100g frozen fruit. Mixed the night before so the fruit thaws and psyllium expands by morning; ~30 seconds, hits ~50g protein before 9 a.m.
- **Meal 2 — "The flexible meal" (the controversial one):**
  - *Training days:* 2 tbsp psyllium husk + a big glass of water 20–30 min before **three bags of protein chips** (≈60g protein, ~10g fiber with the psyllium), plus a quality greens powder for micronutrient insurance. He prefers protein via chips over a shake.
  - *Non-training days:* a local **meal-prep service** with pre-portioned meat/veg/carbs — open, heat, eat. Pitched as cheaper and healthier than DoorDash/food court ordering.
- **Meal 3 — "The anchor meal" (the one he cooks):** 300g+ lean-to-moderate protein (sirloin steak, salmon, chicken, lean ground beef) + 100–200g cruciferous veg (broccoli, cauliflower, green beans) + 100–200g single-ingredient carb (sweet potato, potato, rice, legumes). **Air fryer** is the "secret weapon."
- **Cut target:** ~2000 calories/day; leftover macros filled with post-dinner snacks (protein ice cream, even cereal).
- **The Blue Zones precedent** — [[Okinawans]] in Japan and [[Sardinians]] in Italy, and nearly every centenarian, eat mostly the same foods day after day, decade after decade.

---
**Case Studies / Anecdotes**

- **Coaching pattern** — every client who gets lean and stays there "stopped trying to be creative with their food"; the fittest people run a rotation of 3–5 meals.
- **"Uncle Dan eats chips for lunch"** — the self-aware framing of the controversial protein-chips lunch, defended as macro-friendly, portable, and zero-thinking.
- **Homemade chicken katsu** — made in the air fryer; his wife and kids "lost their minds over it." The anchor meal is his favourite "not because of the food, but because of the ritual" of cooking for family and controlling what's on their plates.

---
**The "So What?" Factor**

Stop relying on daily willpower and build a system you can run tired and busy. Pick one meal from the three, eat it every day for a week, then stack the next. Front-load protein, lean on fiber to stay full, and automate the controllable meals so you're free to enjoy weekends without tracking. The diet that makes you lean isn't the exciting one — it's the one you barely have to think about.
