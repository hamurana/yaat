**The Hook & Core Thesis**

A man who commands respect masters a small set of fundamentals across style, communication, practical life-saving, and mindset. **[[Antonio Centeno]]** of [[Real Men Real Style]] argues these are the high-leverage **[[Pareto principle|20%]]** that delivers 80% of the result — and that watching isn't enough; you have to act.

---
**Key Arguments Checklist**

- **Style comes down to fit, fabric, and function** — nail those and your clothes fade into the background so *you* shine.
- **There is always a dress code** — decode it and build a default "uniform" so you never have to think.
- **Posture is the cheapest confidence hack** — shoulders back, sit up straight; people read your stance.
- **Eye contact has rules** — duration and ratios signal confidence vs weakness or aggression.
- **Silence is a tool, not a threat** — use the pause, or acknowledge the awkwardness to break the ice.
- **Hard conversations: praise in public, critique in private.**
- **Know the practical life-savers** — CPR, Heimlich, changing/inflating a tire, jump-starting a battery, sewing a button.
- **Define success on your own terms** — and learn to say no without apology.

---
**High-Yield Assets**

- **The [[Style Pyramid]]** — three layers: **fit** (clothing that drapes well — right inseam on shorts, a hat that frames the face), **fabric** (quality make), and **function** (right piece for the occasion; shoes must match the formality of the outfit).
- **Dress codes** are shaped by **culture, location, and weather**. Example: a lawyer at a construction firm still dresses sharp to "protect the company," even if the CEO dresses down for the job site.
- **Eye contact rules (Western / US):**
  - Hold each glance **~1.5–2 seconds** (long enough to register eye color).
  - **Speaking** one-on-one: eye contact ~**50%** of the time, more for key points. Speaking to a group of three: ~**90%** split across people (30/30/30, 10% free).
  - **Listening: the 50/70 rule** — ~**70%** eye contact while listening, with continuous nodding.
  - Too long (3–5s) reads as aggressive or attraction; a darting half-second reads as weak or untrustworthy. Mind where the "free" 30% of your gaze lands.
- **Hard conversations:** address issues early, one-to-one, so the other person doesn't put on a front — **praise in public, critique in private.**
- **Life-saving basics (learnable in ~10 minutes):** **CPR** (keep the heart pumping) and the **Heimlich maneuver** (including on yourself).
- **Car basics:** ~25% of people don't know how to **inflate a tire**; ~75% can't **change one** (loosen lug nuts while the tire is still on the ground, then jack up; don't drive fast/far on a spare). **Jump-starting:** connect **red first, then black**; reverse on removal (black, then red); carry a portable jump battery; **read the manual**.
- **Sewing on a button** as the gateway to self-reliance (then mending holes/tears). High-end hotels stock emergency sewing kits.

**Case Studies / Anecdotes**

- **The wrong wall** — Antonio's most-heard regret from thousands of viewers: a man spends years climbing a ladder to the top of a wall, only to realize he's on the wrong wall. Chasing another man's definition of success guarantees you'll never be satisfied.
- **His own med-school detour** — Antonio started down the med-school route because his parents wanted it, then realized it wasn't what *he* wanted, and changed direction.
- **Rambo stitching his own wound** — invoked to reframe sewing as a manly skill rooted in self-reliance, not a feminine chore.
- **The military "attention" stance** — people consistently perceive service members as taller and more confident purely from erect posture, showing how much stance communicates.

---
**The "So What?" Factor**

Pick the high-leverage fundamentals and actually practice them. Build a default wardrobe uniform around fit/fabric/function, fix your posture today, drill the eye-contact ratios in real conversations, and spend an afternoon learning CPR, the Heimlich, a tire change, and a jump-start. Then define success on your own terms and protect your time with boundaries — watching a video won't change anything; putting it into action will.
