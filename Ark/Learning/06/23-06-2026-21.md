**The Starting Point: Blood Work, Not Trends**

Dan Go's philosophy used to be simple — eat real food, sleep, train. That still covers 90% of the answer. But at 40, after a decade of clean eating and consistent training, his blood work showed deficiencies in magnesium, vitamin D, and omega-3s — the basic building blocks for energy, mood, and performance. The reason wasn't diet failure; it was that modern food, indoor living, and Canadian winters create gaps you can't always eat your way out of. The only way to know is to test. His core message: don't copy someone's supplement stack — get blood work every 6–12 months and let data drive decisions, because what was optimal at 40 may not be at 50.

**The 7 Supplements**

**1. Creatine — for the brain, not just the biceps**

The most studied performance supplement in existence. Not a steroid — a molecule the body already makes, also found in food, but you'd need 2.5 lbs of raw steak daily to hit a useful dose, and cooking destroys some of it. At 5g/day it supports muscle building and recovery; at 10g/day research shows cognitive benefits. Dan noticed sharper thinking and better recovery. A client taking 10g/day went from doing darts math on paper to doing it in his head within weeks.

**2. Magnesium Glycinate — calm and sleep**

200–400mg at night. Around 75% of people in the West are deficient, even those eating clean, because modern soil has fewer minerals than it used to. Magnesium is involved in 300+ processes — muscle building, nervous system regulation, stress. The glycinate form absorbs well without the laxative effect of magnesium citrate. Dan's first noticeable effect was being able to actually wind down after a stressful day; sleep quality improved measurably. Can also be taken in the morning if days are highly stressful.

**3. Vitamin D3 — mood and appetite**

2,000–4,000 IU in the morning. Despite the name, vitamin D is actually a hormone produced from sunlight exposure. If you live north of Atlanta or spend most of your day indoors, you're likely deficient — Dan estimates ~70% of his clients are low. His personal experience: optimal D levels produce noticeably better mood and reduced appetite, two effects most people don't associate with vitamin D. Key pairing: take with magnesium, which helps vitamin D absorb and function properly.

**4. Psyllium Husk — fiber and gut health**

One of the most underrated supplements. 90% of Americans don't get enough fiber. Fiber feeds gut bacteria, slows blood sugar spikes, and reduces appetite without effort. Dan settled on two tablespoons 30 minutes before dinner — it reliably kills the urge to snack at night. In his experiment with two tablespoons three times a day, he dropped ~4 pounds in a month and his lipid panel and ApoB (a key predictor of heart disease risk) both improved.

**5. Greens Powder — nutrient insurance**

Not a vegetable replacement — a backup plan for travel days, busy weeks, and restaurant meals. A quality greens powder (or a basic multivitamin) fills dietary gaps. Dan frames it as nutrient insurance rather than a meal replacement.

**6. Glycine — the cheap sleep fix almost no one takes**

5–10g before bed. An amino acid the body makes but likely not in sufficient amounts for full sleep and recovery benefits. Also a building block for collagen, supporting skin elasticity with age. Dan's sleep data showed measurable improvements in REM and deep sleep — the stages where memories consolidate and brain/body repair happens. The mechanism: glycine lowers core body temperature, signalling the body it's time for deep sleep. Costs roughly 10 cents a day.

**7. Omega-3s — inflammation and joint health**

1–2g of EPA and DHA daily, stored in the fridge immediately after opening (fish oil goes rancid at room temperature, and rancid omega-3s do more harm than good). The modern diet is overloaded with omega-6s from processed food and low in omega-3s — an imbalance that drives inflammation, mood swings, and heart disease risk. Since correcting his deficiency, Dan experiences less joint pain, lower inflammation markers, and improved mood. When injured or inflamed, he increases the dose — it's his first intervention before anything else.

**The Meta-Point**

The supplements themselves are secondary to the testing habit. Blood work reveals what your body actually needs. Without it, you're guessing — and the most expensive supplement is the one that solves a problem you don't have.
