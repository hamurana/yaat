**The Inflection Point**

At $1,000/month invested with a 7% annual return adjusted for inflation, the first $100k takes 6.5 years. The second $100k takes 4.6 years. The third takes 3.4 years. The million-dollar mark arrives around year 27.5. The pattern isn't unusual — it's the mathematical structure of compounding, and the reason $100k functions as such a psychologically important milestone.

**Why the Early Phase Feels Brutal**

In the beginning, almost all portfolio growth comes from your own contributions. You put in $1,000/month and the account grows slowly. After year 1 or 2, the numbers are discouraging. Most people who quit investing do so in this window — they expected compound interest to feel exciting and instead it feels like counting pennies. This is normal; the mechanism hasn't switched on yet.

**The Switch**

At $100k with a 7% return, the portfolio generates $7,000 in growth — over $580/month — with no additional contributions. At $200k, that's $14,000/year. At $500k, $35,000/year — comparable to a full-time salary. Once passive returns exceed monthly contributions, the compounding flywheel takes over and the investor becomes progressively less essential to their own wealth growth.

**Six Habits to Reach $100k Faster**

1. **Focus on earning over pure saving.** Side hustles or income-generating projects you enjoy are more sustainable than aggressive expense cutting. Keep the day job for stability; build something on evenings and weekends.
2. **Build an emergency fund first.** 6–12 months of expenses in cash prevents forced selling during downturns — one of the most common ways investors derail compounding at the worst possible time.
3. **Automate everything.** Set automatic transfers from bank to brokerage, with automatic investment. Remove willpower from the equation entirely.
4. **Eliminate bad debt first.** High-interest debt (credit cards) is compound interest working against you. Paying it off is a guaranteed return equal to the interest rate.
5. **Avoid lifestyle inflation.** The period between earning more and spending more is when the most wealth gets built. Keeping expenses stable while income rises compresses the timeline dramatically.
6. **Stay boring.** No crypto, no meme stocks, no TikTok tips. Diversified index funds and time. Speculative investments wipe out both capital and the willingness to invest again.
