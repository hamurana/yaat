## The Four Options

Using a £30,000 Audi A3, 15% deposit (£4,500), 3-year term as the comparison baseline:

**1. Hire Purchase (Finance)**
Pay deposit + borrow the remainder at ~11% interest. Monthly payment: ~£810. Total paid: ~£34,000. Then own the car outright. If sold for ~£16,000, net cost to have the car for 3 years: **~£17,500**.

**2. Lease**
Monthly payment: ~£345 (much lower because you only pay for the depreciation portion, not the full car value). At end of term, you return the car. Net cost for 3 years: **~£17,000**. Slightly cheaper than hire purchase when selling at estimated resale price. Option to buy at end exists but isn't pre-agreed.

**3. PCP (Personal Contract Purchase)**
The most popular option — and the most pushed by dealerships because it's the most lucrative for them. Monthly payment: ~£453. At end:
- **Return the car**: total cost ~£21,000 (worse than leasing by ~£4,000)
- **Keep the car**: pay final £15,000 balloon payment → total cost ~£36,000 (worse than hire purchase)

The dealership profits by locking you into a cycle: trade in at end → new PCP → repeat. They tweak deposits and "dealer contributions" to make PCP look attractive on a monthly basis without showing total cost.

**4. Buy Outright**
Pay £30,000 day one. Sell for £16,000 after 3 years. Net cost: **~£14,000** — the cheapest option on total cost. However: **opportunity cost** — £30,000 invested in e.g. the S&P 500 over 3 years could generate returns that exceed the extra financing cost. This is the key comparison to run.

---

## The Key Verdict

| Option | Net 3-Year Cost |
|--------|----------------|
| Buy outright | ~£14,000 (cheapest) |
| Lease | ~£17,000 |
| Hire Purchase | ~£17,500 |
| PCP (return) | ~£21,000 |
| PCP (keep) | ~£36,000 (most expensive) |

PCP is almost always worse than the alternatives on total cost. Dealerships push it because it generates recurring revenue. Lease beats PCP if you plan to return the car. Outright purchase wins on total cost but locks up capital.

---

## Psychological & Practical Factors

**Case for hire purchase / outright:** If you keep cars long-term, owning outright eliminates monthly payments and gives full freedom (modifications, unlimited miles). Maintenance costs fall on you as the car ages.

**Case for lease / PCP:** Lower monthly payments, newer car, no maintenance worries (trade in before warranty expires ~3 years), no need to manage resale. Drawbacks: must keep car in pristine condition, mileage caps (penalties per mile over limit), restrictions on modifications.

**The smarter alternative to a new car:** Buy a pre-owned car that's 2–3 years old. Someone else has absorbed the sharpest depreciation. Significantly cheaper than new while still being recent enough to be reliable.

---

## The Accountant's Recommendation

Always calculate the **total cost** across the full term — not just the monthly payment. The monthly payment is how dealerships obscure true cost. Run the numbers: deposit + all monthly payments + any balloon payment − estimated resale value = actual cost of having the car. That number is what matters.
