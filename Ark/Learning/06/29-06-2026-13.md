**The Hook & Core Thesis**

Against the doomer narrative that the game is rigged against young men, 2024 is actually the best time ever to be one. The trap is trying to live a life from 50 years ago; open your mind to the modern world's possibilities across five areas — money, skills, dating, travel, and health.

---
**Key Arguments Checklist**

- **Stop romanticising the dead game.** The old single-income, pension-at-retirement life isn't worth wanting — and it's gone.
- **This is the first generation that can make money online**, removing the physical startup costs and gatekeepers of the past.
- **Any skill is learnable online for free** — video learning beats textbooks, and often beats parts of formal education.
- **Dating apps are a net upside** despite the downsides — unprecedented access to potential partners outside your social circle.
- **Geo-arbitrage is now available to ordinary people**, not just the 1%.
- **Gen Z can be the healthiest generation ever** — cheap gyms, free knowledge, better food options, less drinking.
- **It all begins with mindset** — social media overemphasizes the downsides.

---
**High-Yield Assets**

- **Finances:** remote jobs remove the need to live in expensive cities (NY, SF); moving to low-cost states like **Texas** means 0% state income tax. Investing now has zero barrier to entry — financial education free on YouTube, stock trading on your phone without a broker, and low-cost online businesses (drop shipping, affiliate marketing, content creation).
- **The niche advantage** — classic men's style YouTube channels out-succeed magazines like **Esquire** and **GQ** precisely because online businesses can serve niche communities profitably.
- **Skills:** online courses for guitar, options trading, magic, etc.; the host learned **Premiere Pro** from free YouTube tutorials after three years of journalism school failed to teach it (too fast, too many students). [[Duolingo]] cited over a textbook for Spanish.
- **Dating:** profiles let you pre-screen for type and shared interests — more effective than speed dating or singles bars. Advice: invest in high-quality photos, a funny engaging bio, and even premium subscriptions (cheaper than an old-fashioned dating agency); seek dating advice from channels run by women, not "men telling you what they think women think."
- **Travel / [[geo-arbitrage]]** — earn in a strong currency (dollars), spend where the cost of living is low (Thailand, Costa Rica). Enabled by remote work, cheaper international flights, and **Airbnb** for long-term stays.
- **Health stats:** **87% of Gen Z** work out 3+ times a week; 24-hour gym memberships for **$10/month**; **27% of Gen Z never drink alcohol**. Free YouTube workouts and downloadable plans; healthier fast food ("eat every meal at Chipotle and have a six pack").

---
**Case Studies / Anecdotes**

- **The host's own remote setup** — a W2 employee earning slightly above average who no longer needs to live in New York for the job.
- **Meeting his girlfriend Augustina on Tinder** within a few weeks — a couple they'd likely never have crossed paths otherwise; she chimes in that men fixate on photos and skip reading bios.
- **A month in Buenos Aires** — working remotely while he and Augustina take Ubers everywhere, eat out every meal, and have cocktails for under ~$50 USD/day, as a live demo of geo-arbitrage.

---
**The "So What?" Factor**

Reject the doomer mindset and use the tools this era hands you. Take a remote or online income and consider where you live (low-tax states, low-cost countries); learn the skills you want for free online; treat dating apps as a real channel worth optimizing; travel via geo-arbitrage; and exploit cheap gyms, free fitness content, and better food to get healthy. Every generation has trade-offs — focus on the genuinely unprecedented advantages of this one.
