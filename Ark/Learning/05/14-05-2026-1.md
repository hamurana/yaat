**"The First Signs of Heart Disease Aren't What You Think"**

An A&E doctor with over a decade of experience shares what he's learned from seeing thousands of heart attacks — and why most people miss the warning signs entirely.

---

**The core message:** Heart disease develops silently for 10+ years before a dramatic event. The gap is filled with quiet signals, not silence.

**How it starts — the biology:**

- Damage begins at the _endothelium_ (the inner lining of blood vessels), which produces nitric oxide to keep vessels flexible
- Years of high blood sugar, poor diet, and lifestyle stress degrade this lining, stiffening arteries
- The heart compensates by working harder and thickening — but a thicker heart is less efficient and struggles to refill between beats
- The coronary arteries also narrow, creating a chronic oxygen deficit the heart silently adapts to

**The subtle early symptoms people dismiss:**

- Getting unusually tired climbing one flight of stairs or walking to the shops
- Needing an extra pillow to sleep comfortably
- Slightly swollen ankles by evening
- A resting heart rate that's crept up (e.g. 65 → 75 bpm)
- Vague chest, jaw, or neck discomfort
- Poor, unsettled sleep

These mimic "being a bit unfit" or "getting older" — which is exactly why they're dangerous.

**Hidden risk factor:** Insulin resistance can silently damage blood vessels for years _before_ blood sugar shows up as abnormal on standard tests.

**Sleep matters more than most doctors say:** Under 6 hours of sleep = significantly higher heart attack and stroke risk across all populations, because the heart never fully recovers overnight.

**The hopeful part — what actually works:**

- **Aerobic exercise** (walking, cycling, swimming) restores vessel health within _weeks_ by stimulating nitric oxide production
- **Reducing refined carbs** lowers insulin and reduces vascular stress — it's not primarily about weight loss
- **Monitor blood pressure at home** a couple of times a week (more accurate than one annual GP reading)
- **Take sleep seriously** — and get tested for sleep apnea if you snore, wake with headaches, or never feel rested

**Bottom line:** Heart disease is not inevitable. The window for reversing the biology is years wide — but only if you know what signals to pay attention to.
