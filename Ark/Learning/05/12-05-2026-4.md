Another **Charisma on Command** video, about 10 minutes long. This one is a breakdown of interviewer **Stephen Bartlett's** conversational skills.

**Topic:** How to make deep connections through listening, using Stephen Bartlett's interview style as the model.

---

**1. Almost never interrupt** Most people constantly interrupt to correct, joke, or switch topics. Giving someone uninterrupted space to complete their thoughts is the fast track to deep connection. The only exception: interrupt to take someone _deeper_ into their emotions — e.g. pausing someone who rushes through something emotionally significant to ask "you really mean that?"

**2. Offer the least invasive support possible** When someone gets emotional, the instinct is to reassure them or change the subject — but this actually shuts people down. Instead, sit in silence and let them process. Stephen is shown sitting quietly for three full minutes while a guest grieves. In highly emotional moments, _say less_. Short, open phrases like "What did she do?" invite people back into their feelings far more effectively than long reassurances.

**3. Physical touch as a superpower** When words feel insufficient, appropriate touch (a genuine hug, heart to heart, held longer than normal) can calm someone's nervous system. The key is to be _with_ them, not trying to fix or rush them out of their emotion.

**4. The empathetic mirror** After someone shares something vulnerable, reflect the emotional core of what they said back to them in your own words. The distinction made here is important: _encapsulation_ (reflecting what was actually said) vs _anticipation_ (projecting what you expect them to mean based on your own experiences). The former creates connection; the latter causes misunderstandings.

**5. Ask "chunky questions"** To break out of small talk and into meaningful conversation, ask questions that cut straight to what matters — e.g. "What is your deepest fear?" or "If today were the last day you could send a message to your mother, what would you say?" A useful segue: _"I heard this really great question I want to ask you"_ — it gets people excited to answer.

**6. Presence is the foundation** All of the above rests on one thing: genuinely dropping your own agenda and letting your attention rest fully on the other person. The temptation is always to prove yourself — to be funny, helpful, impressive. Real connection happens when you stop performing and just follow with curious attention.

---

**The challenge:** Give someone 10 minutes of fully attentive listening. Start with a chunky question, then apply the rest. You'll likely be surprised how deep a connection forms in a short time.
