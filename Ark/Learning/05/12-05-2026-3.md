Another **Charisma on Command** video, this time about ~13 minutes long, using **Ryan Gosling** as the case study. Here's the summary:

**Topic:** Six habits to make people love being around you.

---

**1. Go in to have fun, not to be liked** Trying too hard to be liked reads as needy. Shift your mindset to simply enjoying the conversation. A practical technique: use absurd analogies to make things funnier (e.g. describing a messy dog situation as "a fecal Jackson Pollock").

**2. Use the mind's eye method** Paint a ridiculous mental image to get laughs. Vague descriptions aren't funny; vivid, specific ones are. Also works well with deadpan delivery — just make sure the joke is absurd enough that people know you're joking. A smirk or smile helps signal playfulness when being deadpan.

**3. Share embarrassing stories confidently** Counterintuitively, sharing cringe-worthy stories from your past makes you look _more_ confident, because you're willing to show parts of yourself others would hide. It also makes you feel more confident over time, as you internalise that you don't need to be perfect to be liked. When something awkward happens _in the moment_, call it out and laugh at it rather than pretending it didn't happen.

**4. Create authentic connection** Authenticity is what creates genuine connection, not just exchanging information. You can encourage it by gently calling out inauthenticity (done with humour, not judgment) and complimenting it when you see it. If someone admits something vulnerable or honest, acknowledge it genuinely. Don't be fake — but when you truly admire something about someone, say so.

**5. Make everyone feel good, not just people you're trying to impress** Truly charismatic people acknowledge everyone, including those who normally get overlooked. When you receive compliments, respond with playful humility rather than deflecting or boasting.

**6. Self-validation and keeping perspective** Ryan Gosling's calm reaction when La La Land was wrongly announced as Best Picture winner at the Oscars is the centrepiece example — he stayed unfazed because he kept perspective ("they just read the wrong name, no one was hurt"). Much of confidence is internalising that the stakes aren't as high as anxiety makes them feel. The mantra: _want it, don't need it._
