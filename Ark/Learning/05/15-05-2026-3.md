**"8 Habits From Tommy Shelby That Make People Respect You" — Summary**

Another Charisma on Command video, this time using Tommy Shelby from _Peaky Blinders_ as the model. Here are the 8 habits covered:

---

**Body Language**

1. **Slow, relaxed movements** — Moving unhurriedly in high-pressure situations signals you don't feel intimidated or scared. Speed and fidgeting read as anxiety.
    
2. **Deliberate eye contact** — Holding eye contact during conflict signals confidence. But use it strategically: hold to show you're not intimidated, look _down_ to de-escalate, or break _to the side_ (not down) to defuse without projecting fear.
    
3. **Non-reactivity** — Don't react visibly to hostility or provocation. Staying calm where others would lose it is one of the most powerful ways to project confidence. The goal is genuine inner calm, not suppressing panic.
    
4. **Scale gestures to your audience** — In large groups, use bigger, broader hand gestures to command the room's attention.
    

---

**How You Speak**

5. **Use conviction in word choice** — Say "I will" instead of "I hope to" or "I want to." Language of certainty projects decisiveness and authority.
    
6. **End statements with a downward inflection** — Upward inflection makes statements sound like questions, implying uncertainty. A falling tone signals confidence and finality.
    
7. **Speak slowly and use pauses** — Silence draws people in. Pause right before the key point. If you're worried about being cut off, don't rush — just maintain the same slow cadence until you finish your sentence.
    

---

**Underlying Power**

8. **Carrot and stick leverage** — Tommy's calm comes from knowing he holds something others want or fear. In real life, this means building genuine value and options for yourself. The video's example: instead of begging for a raise, ask your boss what specific targets would earn you a promotion, hit them, and simultaneously line up competing job offers. That's your carrot (value you've created) and stick (alternatives you have).

---

The throughline: respect doesn't come from being loud or aggressive — it comes from projecting calm, certainty, and genuine leverage.
