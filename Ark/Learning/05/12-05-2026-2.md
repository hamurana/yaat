**Topic:** How to be funnier in everyday life, using Ryan Reynolds and Ricky Gervais as case studies.

---

**Two Core Techniques for Making People Laugh:**

**1. Surprise (Subverting Expectations)**

- **Sincerity fakeout** — Set up as if you're going to say something genuine, then undercut it with a punchline (e.g. Ricky Gervais at the Golden Globes: "On a serious note, looking at all the faces here reminds me of the great work done this year by cosmetic surgeons").
- **Opposite answer** — When asked a question with an obvious answer, say the opposite (e.g. Ryan Reynolds asked his daughters' ages: "I have no idea").
- **Absurd answer** — Give the most ridiculous answer that could still plausibly fit the question.
- **Tip:** Pause for one second before the punchline — it makes people mentally try to complete the sentence, so the fakeout lands harder.

**2. Specificity**

- Vague statements aren't funny. Painting a vivid, ridiculous mental image is. Instead of "I don't like the news," say "The news is like pouring piping hot lemon juice into your eyeballs."
- Gervais calling someone pale isn't funny — saying they look like a "baby fish you can see through" is.
- **Pro tip:** Once a joke works, save it. Comedians frequently reuse winners.

---

**Mindset:**

- Stop filtering yourself out of fear of bombing. Self-censorship kills wit. The willingness to have a joke fall flat is exactly what lets funny people develop quick wit over time.
- Ryan Reynolds openly tries multiple versions of a joke until one lands — lower your filter and see what works.

---

**Being Funny vs. Being a Jerk:**

- Tease yourself too, not just others — it signals you're playful, not a bully.
- Tease things that aren't real insecurities (e.g. Ryan joking he looks tiny next to The Rock).
- Don't be sarcastic all the time — balance it with genuine warmth. Ricky Gervais, despite his brutal roasts, privately checked with people before roasting them at award shows.
