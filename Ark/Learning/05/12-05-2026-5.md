This one is by the same doctor from the first video (Dr. Alex, emergency medicine), also about ~20 minutes long. It's a deep dive on a topic touched on in that first video — post-meal movement.

**Core argument:** What you do in the 90 minutes _after_ eating often matters more than the meal itself.

---

**The Three Things to Do After Eating:**

1. **Go for a walk** — 15–30 minutes after dinner. Glucose that would sit in your bloodstream for 2–3 hours gets cleared in half the time. No equipment needed, and it doubles as fresh air and family time.
    
2. **Do squats** — Even just a few sets during TV ad breaks. Your quads and glutes are the largest muscle groups in the body; when they contract, they create a massive metabolic demand and pull glucose directly out of the blood without needing insulin to do it.
    
3. **Do housework** — Washing up, hoovering, laundry after dinner. It's not structured exercise, just movement — but bending, lifting and walking up stairs adds up to significant muscle activity while getting chores done anyway.
    

You don't need to do all three — just pick whichever one you can sustain daily.

---

**The Science (why this works):**

- Glucose rising after a meal is normal and not the problem. The real villain is **chronic insulin exposure**.
- Every time glucose rises, the pancreas releases insulin. Done repeatedly over years, cells stop responding — that's **insulin resistance**, the root cause of type 2 diabetes, heart disease, fatty liver, PCOS, and certain cancers.
- Crucially, **insulin resistance is silent** — your fasting glucose on a blood test can look completely normal while insulin has been quietly doing damage for 10–15 years in the background.
- When muscles contract, they absorb glucose directly from the bloodstream via a pathway that **doesn't require insulin** (GLUT4 transporters). This reduces how much insulin the pancreas needs to produce, and lowers how long it stays elevated.
- **Frequency matters more than duration**: Three short movement bouts after meals beats one hour at the gym followed by sitting all day. Continuous glucose monitor data shows spreading movement throughout the day reduces 24-hour glucose levels by 10–15%.
- **Sitting after meals is actively harmful** — it shuts off the muscle glucose uptake pathway, forces insulin to do all the work, and pushes glucose into fat storage (especially around the liver and abdomen). You can go to the gym five days a week and still develop insulin resistance if you sit for hours after every meal.
- High glucose also causes **glycation** — glucose molecules stick to proteins in blood vessel walls, making them stiff, raising blood pressure, and increasing heart attack and stroke risk over decades.

---

**The big picture:** Each walk after dinner reduces insulin exposure by roughly 20–30% for that meal. Compounded across 10,000+ meals over a lifetime, that fundamentally shifts your metabolic trajectory — like compound interest, but for your health. The benefits won't be felt tomorrow, but they're accumulating silently at the cellular level every time you choose to move.
