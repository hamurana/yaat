## The Hook & Core Thesis
A breakdown of [[Alfie Solomons]] from *Peaky Blinders* — four habits that instantly command more respect (leave his violence aside). Though scripted, the psychology is real: **calm under pressure, strategic eye contact, control of your own attention, and honesty even against your own interest** all project the deep confidence that earns respect. The tips project confidence instantly; the underlying *internal* confidence takes longer to build.

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **Calm where others show fear is the fastest respect-builder.** Staying composed in high-pressure moments reads as strength.
- **Eye contact is a tool to be used deliberately, not held constantly** — the direction and duration of your gaze/breaks send precise signals.
- **Being selective with your attention makes people crave it** — don't let others dictate where you look or who you engage.
- **Honesty against your own interest signals unshakable confidence** — "whatever the consequences, I'll be okay."

## High-Yield Assets (the 4 habits)
1. **Be calm in high-pressure situations.** Alfie stays composed where most would panic (reacting coolly to Tommy's threats). In an argument, **deliberately wait 2–3 seconds before responding** — it looks poised and buys calm. Backing insight: we demand faster responses of ourselves than of others — people give themselves only ~**30%** as much time to respond as they'd give someone else — so when you feel you must act *now*, **pause** instead of looking frantic.
2. **Hold eye contact strategically, especially in conflict** — but *not* constantly (mutual holding just escalates tension). Calibrate to your goal:
   - **Break by looking down** if your goal is to **de-escalate**.
   - **Hold** if your goal is to **show you aren't intimidated**.
   - **Hold 3–5 seconds then break to the side (not down)** to de-escalate *without* signaling fear or discomfort (as Alfie does with a murderous Arthur Shelby — de-escalating without ceding power).
3. **Don't let others dictate where your attention goes** — be selective with it. Alfie refuses to defer to the more powerful mob boss **Luca Changretta** (keeping his eyes shut, doing an exercise "to understand the blind"), and chooses to address Tommy rather than the glaring Arthur. Being selective makes people **crave your attention** — once they have it they have it fully, and can't just demand it back.
4. **Be honest even when it goes against your best interests** — Alfie won't be spared for reasons he considers wrong. In real life: **don't apologize just to avoid conflict** (do apologize when genuinely wrong, but if you aren't sorry, don't say you are — a common dating mistake that sets you up to be walked on). Honesty against your interest screams self-confidence: "whatever the consequences, I know I'll be okay."

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **Alfie vs. his right-hand man** reacting to Tommy's grenade threat — the side-by-side that shows how a calm reaction vs. a fearful one changes your impression of each man.
- **Alfie and Luca Changretta** — refusing to open his eyes for a richer, more powerful boss — the vivid illustration of controlling your own attention.
- **The friend who won't break focus**: gives whoever he's speaking to undivided attention and won't turn to an interrupter until they've finished. Annoying if you're the interrupter, great if you're the one being heard — and it makes everyone **value his attention** because you can't just demand it back.

## The "So What?" Factor
To command respect: pause 2–3 seconds before responding in tense moments (you have more time than you feel), use eye contact deliberately — break down to de-escalate, hold to stand firm, break to the side to do both — and be selective with your attention so people value having it. Most importantly, stop apologizing just to keep the peace: honesty even when it costs you signals the deep confidence that earns respect. The behaviors project confidence now; genuinely building it internally is the longer work underneath.
