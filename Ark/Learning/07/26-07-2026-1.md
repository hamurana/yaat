**The Hook & Core Thesis:** About 80% of people who lose weight gain it back because the entire weight-loss industry teaches [[Health|the "drop phase"]] (losing weight) and almost nobody teaches the "lock phase" (keeping it off) — the two require completely different skills.

**Key Arguments Checklist** (Dan Go's six science-based secrets for the lock phase, in his stated order):
1. **Restrain the brain** — pick a form of eating restriction that feels *least* restrictive to you personally (calorie awareness, low-carb, or eating on a fixed schedule), not the most extreme version an influencer promotes. The form that works long-term is whichever one your brain stops fighting.
2. **Keep yourself honest (the "body dashboard")** — people who keep weight off long-term share one trait: regular self-monitoring (scale, waistline, body fat %, blood work, or photos). The critical mistake is stopping measurement the moment you hit your goal — that's exactly when tracking matters most.
3. **Understand your body's defense mechanisms (the "wave method")** — significant weight loss spikes ghrelin (hunger hormone) and suppresses leptin (satiety hormone), and can slow metabolism, because the body reads weight loss as a survival threat. Instead of losing weight in one straight-line push, lose 10–12% of body weight at a time, then take a deliberate "diet break" at maintenance calories lasting at least half — sometimes the full length — of the time it took to lose that weight, before resuming.
4. **Build a daily movement floor** — losing weight also subconsciously reduces non-exercise movement (steps can drop 25–50%, along with less fidgeting), quietly cutting calorie burn. Exercise is described as "pretty bad" for losing weight but "exceptional" for keeping it off, because it sensitizes hunger signals, keeps metabolism elevated, and preserves muscle built during the drop phase.
5. **Make sustainability structural (the "90-10 rule")** — 90% of calories from whole, single-ingredient, fiber-rich, high-protein sources, and 10% from anything you want (a client dinner, birthday cake, night out). Rigid, all-or-nothing diets are named as one of the strongest predictors of long-term failure, because breaking the rule once triggers an "I already failed" binge response.
6. **Shift identity during the process, not after reaching the goal** — a cited systematic review found the one factor separating long-term maintainers from everyone else was forming a new identity ("this is just the way I eat" rather than "I'm on a diet") *during* weight loss, not once the goal was hit.

**High-Yield Assets:**
- **Ghrelin** (hunger hormone, rises after weight loss) and **leptin** (satiety hormone, suppressed after weight loss).
- **The wave method** — lose 10–12% of body weight, then a maintenance-calorie diet break lasting half-to-full the duration of the loss phase, repeat.
- **The two-set method** — lower-volume, higher-intensity strength training, referenced as covered in a separate Dan Go video; used as 2×30-minute sessions per week.
- **8,000 steps/day** cited as the daily movement floor used with clients.
- **The 90-10 rule** — 90% whole/fiber-rich/high-protein foods, 10% deliberately flexible.
- **The "body dashboard"** — Dan Go's term for a self-monitoring system (scale, waistline, body fat %, blood work, or photos).

**Case Studies/Anecdotes:**
- **Mark**, a hedge fund manager with three kids, had lost and regained 40 pounds twice. The fix wasn't a new diet but matching his eating schedule to his actual lifestyle (fixed meal times, last meal 4+ hours before bed) instead of trying to eat like a bodybuilder.
- **Paul**, a founder who sold his company at 52, hadn't weighed himself in months out of fear of the number. Told to weigh in daily for two weeks with no other changes, he lost 5 pounds from self-monitoring alone.
- **Neil**, an entrepreneur with three kids, tried to lose 50 pounds in one push, stalled at 30, and rebounded to a 45-pound net gain. Using the wave method on a second attempt, he has maintained a 47-pound loss for three years and returned to playing tennis.
- **Carl**, a frequently-traveling operator, lost 28 pounds, dropped his program when busy, and regained 20. Rebuilding around a non-negotiable 8,000-step floor plus two 30-minute two-set-method strength sessions a week, he lost 22 pounds over 18 months and maintains 15% body fat.
- **Sharon**, a CFO with client dinners three times a week, lost 22 pounds but repeatedly derailed during business travel until the 90-10 rule gave her planned flexibility instead of an all-or-nothing standard.
- **Robert**, a serial entrepreneur in his late 50s, had lost and regained 30–35 pounds before. The change that stuck was reframing his identity as "an athlete" (which he stated to himself, his family, his team, and his trainer) rather than chasing more motivation or a better program — three years later he remains lean and more athletic than before.

**The "So What?" Factor:** Losing weight (phase one) is the goal everyone chases, but keeping it off (phase two) is the actual skill that changes a life — and it requires different tools entirely: a personally sustainable restriction system, ongoing self-monitoring that doesn't stop at goal weight, planned diet breaks instead of one continuous push, a non-negotiable daily movement floor, built-in dietary flexibility (90-10, not all-or-nothing), and an identity shift made *during* the process rather than treated as a reward for finishing it.
