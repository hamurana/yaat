## The Hook & Core Thesis
Texting well builds relationships and anticipation — the through-line is **getting her thinking about you so she's excited to see you**. Framed for single men *and* long-married ones: the same techniques that build early attraction also revive a decade-old marriage. (Developed with texting expert [[Claudia Cox]] of Text Weapon.)

*(Note: the title says "7 texts"; the video delivers **5 tips + 2 bonus rules**.)*

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **Anticipation is the goal, not the individual text.** Every tip aims to get her thinking about you between meetings, so she arrives already excited.
- **Communication skill beats surface traits.** The opening anecdote proves texting can outweigh looks, fitness, personality, and career.
- **Vulnerability is a feature, not a weakness.** Occasionally dropping the "tough guy" front and putting yourself out there (risking rejection) is necessary — "if you never try, you're never going to succeed."
- **Long-term couples need this most.** Most of the tips are aimed at people who've stopped doing them after years together.

## High-Yield Assets (the 5 tips + 2 bonus rules)
1. **Text her something thoughtful.** Most men don't text at all, assuming she "knows how we feel" — she doesn't. Example (early dating / a work colleague): *"You did a great job getting everything done for the conference. I'm amazed at how you handle stress — and how cute you are 🙂"* — keeps it professional but points out you find her attractive.
2. **Show vulnerability.** Drop the tough-guy look occasionally. Examples: *"Last night was one of those rare occasions I never wanted to end,"* or *"It's not every day I meet someone like you. I want to know more."* You're exposing yourself to rejection, which is the point.
3. **Compliment her.** Obvious but forgotten in long relationships. Example: *"I'm sure you get this all the time, but you are incredibly attractive."* For established couples, send a compliment for no reason — *"I loved the way you looked heading out the door this morning… can't wait to see you"* — to get her mind going.
4. **Remind her of a shared memory** — it must be about *both* of you and *positive*, so she associates you with a great feeling. Examples: *"Can't find my favorite boxer shorts — I wonder if they're under your bed"* (playful) or *"Sitting at the office thinking about last night — your laugh, the conversation, everything was perfect"* (good after a date or two; doesn't insinuate too much, prompts her to recall the wine, the food, the movie's twist).
5. **Tell her how much she means to you** — calibrated to the relationship's stage. For a 10-year marriage: *"You mean everything to me. You've given me four beautiful children, you make my life so much better, I can't imagine life without you. I love you"* — then just send it. For a 10-day relationship: *"What are your best qualities, besides being cute, smart, and sexy? Dying to find out"* — pays compliments, shows curiosity about the future, and is a little vulnerable.
- **Bonus — DO use emojis** (show emotion; one every other sentence, don't overdo it). Cited: studies suggest emoji users are perceived as more fun, more ambitious, more enjoyable — and it correlates with more sex.
- **Bonus — AVOID misspellings.** Reread before sending; typos drop the perceived intelligence of the sender and can offset all the emoji goodwill.

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **The "amazing texter" anecdote** that opens the video: a beautiful female friend dated a man she described as unattractive, overweight, rude, and without a great job — yet in a long-distance relationship (seeing each other every other month) she couldn't wait to get off the plane because he built so much anticipation via text that she looked past all his faults. The proof that texting skill can outweigh every surface trait.

## The "So What?" Factor
Use text to keep her thinking about you between meetings — whether you're dating or ten years married. Rotate the five moves: something thoughtful, a moment of vulnerability, a genuine compliment, a shared positive memory, and (calibrated to the stage) how much she means to you. Sprinkle emojis for warmth, proofread to avoid looking careless, and accept that vulnerability risks rejection — which is exactly why it works.
