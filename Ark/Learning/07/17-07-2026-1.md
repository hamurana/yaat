## The Hook & Core Thesis
[[Dr Alex]] (UK emergency medicine doctor, 10 years) argues that colon cancer is one of the few cancers where your plate has a real, powerful say — close to *half* of all bowel cancers could be prevented through diet, healthy weight, and activity — and the single most protective food choice is **fibre**, "the closest thing we have to a natural shield for your gut," which most people eat nowhere near enough of.

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **Fibre is the #1 protective food, and the evidence is among the strongest in all of nutrition.** One of the largest analyses ever done, pooling almost **two million people**, found that for every **10g of fibre** added to the daily diet, bowel cancer risk fell by roughly **10%** — and protection was strongest for fibre from whole grains.
- **How fibre physically protects the colon.** It passes undigested into the large bowel and does three mechanical jobs: bulks up stool so waste moves through faster (harmful substances spend less time pressed against the colon lining), soaks up water to dilute cancer-causing substances so they're less concentrated against vulnerable cells, and keeps everything regular.
- **The microbiome mechanism is the remarkable part.** Gut bacteria ferment fibre — their favourite food — and produce [[Short Chain Fatty Acids]], the most important being **[[butyrate]]**. Butyrate is the *preferred fuel* for the cells lining your colon, literally nourishing the tissue where colon cancer would otherwise begin. It also calms inflammation in the gut wall and keeps the gut barrier strong and sealed.
- **The protection travels beyond the gut.** Growing evidence suggests these short chain fatty acids move out into the body and help *train the immune system*, including the immune cells whose job is to find and destroy abnormal, potentially cancerous cells. (Dr Alex flags this mechanism is still being figured out and doesn't want to overstate it — but the broad picture is clear.)
- **Eat both types of fibre — real plants give you both.** The coarse "roughage" type (skins of fruit/veg, whole grains, nuts, seeds) bulks the stool; the fermentable type (beans, lentils, onions, garlic, slightly underripe green bananas, mushrooms, berry skins) feeds the bacteria. Whole plant foods deliver a generous mix of both, so no need to overthink the ratio.
- **Most people fall badly short.** A sensible daily target is ~**25–35 or 40g of fibre** (roughly **14g per 1,000 calories** as a *minimum*, not the target — protection keeps climbing the more you eat). Yet in the UK and America people get barely *half* of even that minimum, leaving many colons unprotected.
- **Real food beats supplements — always.** No fancy fibre powder needed; real food does the job far better and delivers the vitamins, minerals, and plant compounds nature packaged alongside the fibre, which supplements never will.

## High-Yield Assets
- **The headline study**: ~2 million people; every +10g daily fibre → ~10% lower bowel cancer risk; whole-grain fibre most protective.
- **[[World Cancer Research Fund]]** considers whole grains strongly protective against bowel cancer and suggests around **three good portions of whole grains a day**.
- **The fibre target numbers**: ~25–35/40g per day; ~14g per 1,000 calories as a floor; Dr Alex estimates he personally eats ~50–60g.
- **Top foods (his "crown" picks)**: lentils, beans, chickpeas, and whole grains like **barley** and **quinoa**.
- **The resistant-starch trick**: cook potatoes, rice, or pasta, then *cool them down* before reheating or eating cold — this converts some starch into **[[resistant starch]]** that gut bacteria love.
- **Plant variety rule**: the more different plants you eat across the week, the wider the range of helpful bacteria you feed — aim for as many different plants per plate as possible.
- **Practical swaps**: extra portion of beans/lentils/chickpeas; swap white bread for whole grain, seeded, or properly fermented sourdough; berries + seeds on porridge, lentils at lunch, beans/veg at dinner, nuts as a snack gets most people to target.

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **His own intake**: Dr Alex estimates he eats around 50–60g of fibre a day — deliberately well above the minimum, because the research shows protection keeps climbing with more fibre.
- **The bean salad habit**: he mentions making a bean salad he loves "every single week" as his own go-to easy fibre boost.
- **Why bowel cancer is rising in the young**: he ties the trend directly to modern diets stripping out fibre and replacing it with refined [[Ultra-Processed Foods]] — "your gut bacteria pay the price."

## The "So What?" Factor
Add a little more fibre the very next time you eat — a handful of nuts, a bean salad, anything with crunch from whole plant foods. It's one of the cheapest, simplest, most reliable defenses against bowel cancer, and preventing the disease is infinitely better than treating it once it develops. The power is literally in your hands three times a day.
