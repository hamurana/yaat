## The Hook & Core Thesis
[[Antonio Centeno]] ([[Real Men Real Style]]) runs down eleven maximum-versatility "wear anywhere, please everyone" men's fragrances — dominated by the blue-fragrance family and its modern classics — with the practical angle throughout being *when to buy blind versus when to test first*, and which concentration or flanker of each line gives the best value.

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **Most of the list is safe to buy blind — crowd-pleasers and compliment-getters** — with one deliberate exception (the new Valentino) that must be smelled first because of its polarizing note combination.
- **[[Bleu de Chanel]] (2010) is "the GOAT"**: the fragrance that kicked off the entire blue craze, a modern masterpiece — versatile, elegant, suitable for any occasion. The EDP and Parfum variants are smoother but project less and cost more; the brand-new L'Exclusif works for fall/winter but is hard to find — his pick is the **EDT** for the most bang for your buck.
- **Blue fragrances defined**: mass-appealing, fresh, aromatic, sometimes slightly aquatic — the category you can wear to the office or on a date; versatility is the whole point.
- **[[Terre d'Hermès]] is the sleeper "compliment monster."** Considered a masterpiece within a year of release — it revived the Hermès fragrance house and established perfumer [[Jean-Claude Ellena]] as one of the world's top noses. Yes, it has some weirdness (some people get a "rotten orange" facet), but he calls it a potential signature scent that most guys never think to pick up — and his own giveaway go-to.
- **Y by Yves Saint Laurent**: a refined blue (apple, sage, amber notes) and one of the most popular men's fragrances out there — though, tellingly, he's never once been stopped and asked about it. The whole YSL line is consistently strong; an easy gift for a designer-brand guy.
- **MYSLF by YSL** gets hate because the flankers all smell nearly the same — his advice is to skip the comparisons and just get the **EDP**, which "covers all bases" and is extremely likable.
- **Valentino Uomo Born in Roma — the brand-new purple flanker** [Transcript unclear on the exact flanker name — heard as "Purple Melancholia"]: lavender makes it strongly masculine while coconut makes it fun — heavy, rich, and light all at once, creamy, tropical, mysterious. He liked it, but *get your nose on it before buying*. The purple bottle finally makes it distinguishable in a line whose bottles all look alike.
- **Burberry Hero (all five in the line)**: woody, simple, versatile, masculine crowd-pleasers that make great gifts — the darker ones toward the Parfum get richer.
- **Dior Homme — specifically the 2020 version** (Dior keeps reshuffling versions): masculine, woody, fresh, classy — the one to wear when you're getting dressed up.
- **Prada Luna Rossa**: clean, modern, slightly metallic; the original is a perfect summer scent. Don't sleep on the flankers — **Luna Rossa Carbon** is popular because it's like Dior Sauvage but cleaner, easier to wear, and light enough that you're not "choking people out."
- **Versace Dylan Blue** (incense + citrus): didn't start the blue movement but "wrapped it up" — the perfected blue for projection and overall scent profile.
- **Chanel Allure Homme Sport Eau Extrême**: aromatic woods, fresh spicy citrus-green, a touch of vanilla and musk — a four-season fragrance and his #1 recommendation for a guy who isn't into fragrances at all.
- **Versace Eros Energy** for going out: a bright, fun lemon-lime-grapefruit opening over the classic Eros club-scent DNA, with a patchouli/musk/oakmoss base kept from going sour by blackcurrant at its center. Spray generously for the club.

## High-Yield Assets
- **The eleven**: Y (Yves Saint Laurent) • MYSLF EDP (YSL) • Valentino Uomo Born in Roma purple flanker • Burberry Hero • Bleu de Chanel EDT • Dior Homme (2020) • Prada Luna Rossa (+ Carbon flanker) • Versace Dylan Blue • Chanel Allure Homme Sport Eau Extrême • Terre d'Hermès • Versace Eros Energy.
- **Concentration strategy**: Bleu de Chanel — EDT over EDP/Parfum/L'Exclusif on value; MYSLF — EDP over original/Absolu (they smell too similar to matter).
- **Use-case picks**: fragrance-newbie gift → Allure Homme Sport Eau Extrême; signature-scent sleeper → Terre d'Hermès; club night → Eros Energy; summer → Luna Rossa original; dressing up → Dior Homme 2020; safe designer gift → Y by YSL.
- **Named perfumer**: [[Jean-Claude Ellena]] (Terre d'Hermès).
- *A mid-video sponsor segment for a niche Persian-inspired house (heard as "House of Deston," with several of its own fragrances) is an ad-read and omitted per policy; the closing plug for his collection-downsizing video is channel promo, also omitted.*

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **The Y paradox**: it's one of the best-selling men's fragrances, yet no woman has ever stopped him to ask what he's wearing — his implicit warning that popularity and compliment-pull aren't the same metric.
- **"Sorry guys"**: he jokes that readers will complain he's giving away their secret go-to (Terre d'Hermès) — the ladies "come out of the woodwork" for it.
- **The thousand-bottle collection**: he owns close to 1,000 fragrances and has offloaded over 100 in recent weeks as part of a slow collection purge.

## The "So What?" Factor
A shortcut map for building (or gifting into) a versatile fragrance wardrobe: anchor on the blue-fragrance classics (Bleu de Chanel EDT, Dylan Blue), add the underrated signature-scent candidate (Terre d'Hermès), match the remaining picks to use cases — office, summer, club, dress-up — and only insist on testing first when a scent is deliberately polarizing (the new Valentino). Buy the value concentration, not the prestige one.
