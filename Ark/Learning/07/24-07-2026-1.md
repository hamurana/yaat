**The Hook & Core Thesis:** Women pick up fast on whether a man's [[Charisma|manners]] are genuine character or a performance aimed at impressing her — the etiquette slips below are all tells that separate the two.

**Key Arguments Checklist** (interview with etiquette expert Molly Tomlin, hosted by James of Gentlemen's Collective):
- **Forgetting basic courtesies** — holding a door only for your date, rather than for anyone behind you, reads as performative; doing it for everyone signals it's genuine character, not a show for her benefit.
- **Complimenting only her looks** — praising appearance alone signals surface-level interest; complimenting her opinions, energy, and the way she carries herself signals you're actually listening.
- **Dressing too casually for the occasion** — mismatched effort (him underdressed, her dressed up) makes her feel she's attending a different event than he is.
- **No solid plan for the date** — a vague plan forces her to manage logistics and "stay in her head"; a clear plan (time, place, a backup activity if things go well) shows foresight and lets her relax into the experience.
- **Rudeness to service staff** — reveals character reserved for people who "can't do anything for you"; kindness that's conditional on what someone offers back is an instant red flag, especially to women who've worked service jobs themselves.
- **Poor or scanning eye contact** — looking around a room instead of at your date reads as disinterest regardless of intent; deliberate eye contact is where connection and attraction actually form.
- **Turning the date into an interview** — stacking questions without offering your own observations or vulnerability feels like an interrogation; it also one-sidedly withholds the vulnerability that mutual connection requires.

**High-Yield Assets:**
- The "old-fashioned gentleman" habit of rising from the table when your date steps away and rises again when she returns.
- Offering to help with her coat ("May I help you remove your coat?") as an underused courtesy.
- Reframe for over-questioning: pair each question with an offered observation of your own, so it reads as conversation rather than interview.

**Case Studies/Anecdotes:**
- Molly's husband regularly rises when she leaves and returns to the table — a habit other couples notice and compliment.
- Molly values her husband's compliments on *how she carries herself* and *how she listens to others* far more than compliments on appearance, since she has built her identity around etiquette and elegance.
- James admits he was "really guilty" of scanning a room instead of maintaining eye contact until, around age 23, he studied communication skills and started making it deliberate — a change he says improved connection with friends, colleagues, and dates alike.
- The "Home Depot vs. red carpet" image: a man dressed for chores next to a date dressed up, signaling mismatched effort.

**The "So What?" Factor:** Nearly all seven mistakes collapse into one test: is the behavior something you'd do even when nobody's watching or benefiting you? Genuine courtesy, eye contact, and interest read as consistent character; anything that switches on only around the person you're trying to impress reads as performance — and women notice the difference quickly.
