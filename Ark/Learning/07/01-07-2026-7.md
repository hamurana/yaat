**The Hook & Core Thesis**

Most business owners do ~20 jobs and still feel behind. The ones who grow — calmer, working less, earning more — do only **four**: build a sales system, make it measurable, build a team to run it, and work on themselves. Everything else is you failing to do your actual job as the *owner*.

---
**Key Arguments Checklist**

- **You are the owner, not the most senior employee.** These should be separate jobs; treating sales as a "when-I-get-to-it" task is the most expensive habit almost nobody notices.
- **How you *feel* about sales is irrelevant.** Feeling anything about it just means you haven't built a system to take the pressure off your shoulders.
- **A sales process that lives in your head isn't a system.** It's a very tired, annoyed owner — and you can't delegate what isn't written down.
- **You can't improve what you can't see.** Running sales on gut feel is what creates the [[feast and famine cycle]].
- **Tying income to hours in your day is a trap.** Closing every deal yourself is "a stressful job masquerading as a profitable company."
- **You are the ceiling.** The business grows only as big as the owner does — the [[plant-in-a-pot]] problem.

---
**High-Yield Assets**

The four jobs, kept specific:

1. **Build the sales system.** Stop "selling by ear" — a spider-web process improvised deal-by-deal that only works while *you* run it. Map every step a client goes through, from first hearing about you to paying the invoice: how they find you and become a lead, the questions that frame their problem and position you as the solution, what happens after a good call, what happens when a prospect goes quiet. Build a **straight line of sequential activities** documented well enough that a hired salesperson could drive revenue *tomorrow* — not after the usual 3–6-month ramp. Result: sales stop depending on your mood and memory, and become **[[standard operating procedures]]** you can hand off.
2. **Make the system measurable.** You can't track an invisible process — "like driving at night at high speed with no speedometer, no fuel gauge, no headlights." Put numbers on it: conversations per week, leads → discovery calls, opportunities stuck at the decision phase, close rate, total **pipeline value**. Tools scale with size: a **CRM**, a dashboard, or — under ~$1M/year — even a **spreadsheet**. Goal: move from *having a feeling* about the numbers to *actionable data on a screen*, so you can sit down with a coffee at month-start and know what's coming instead of doing "pipeline math at 3am."
3. **Build the team that runs the system.** Now that it's documented and working, hand it (or parts of it) to someone else so you stop being the bottleneck. This is *why* job one matters — you can't hand over what lived in your head. The common failure: hiring salesperson after salesperson expecting them to extract the process from your head themselves. With a written process you can train someone to follow it and drive revenue while you sleep, spend time with kids, or work *on* the business. The business can then grow past your personal cap on weekly conversations.
4. **Work on yourself.** You can build a perfect, measurable, staffed system and it still falls apart — the reason is always **you**. The **[[plant-in-a-pot]]** analogy: a plant only grows as big as its pot allows; your business is the plant, you're the pot. The skills/mindset/limiting beliefs that got you to your current revenue aren't the ones that reach the next level. Constantly ask "how am I holding the business back?" — for some it's refusing to stop doing delivery, for some it's avoiding the numbers, for some it's a limiting belief that unconsciously sabotages any system. Grow yourself and the pot gets bigger; the business grows to fill it.

**Case Studies / Anecdotes**

- **"Jim":** sells high-tech engineering services to the aviation market, stuck at ~**$50,000/year for about a decade**. He wrongly believed every client needed a bespoke approach, so each opportunity took ~10× longer than it should to manage, propose, and close. In one 1-on-1 call plus a couple of consulting calls, they pulled his whole sales process out of his head, cut the unnecessary parts (that buyers didn't care about), and gave him a seamless step-by-step plan — expected to skyrocket his conversion rate.
- **The "blank stare":** owners joining the mentorship can't answer what's in their pipeline, what's likely to close this month, or their total pipeline value — the root of feast-and-famine, which isn't bad luck or the market but an inability to see what's coming until it's too late.
- **The mindset flip:** clients join thinking they need a sales system built; often, once a few "mental game" issues are resolved, the rest of the sales bottleneck disappears — *they* were the problem.

---
**The "So What?" Factor**

Stop acting like your business's most senior employee. In order: (1) get your sales process out of your head and onto paper as sequential steps, (2) attach numbers so you can see the pipeline, (3) document it into SOPs and delegate so you're not the bottleneck, and (4) work relentlessly on your own skills, habits, and limiting beliefs. Get these four right and you break the feast-and-famine cycle and grow revenue past yourself.
