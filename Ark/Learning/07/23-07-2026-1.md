### The Hook & Core Thesis
Being well-dressed is defined as much by what you avoid as by what you wear — nine specific items and habits instantly undercut a man's style.

### Key Arguments Checklist
- Never wear a designer-logo T-shirt — visible branding reads as insecurity, not taste; "you are the brand."
- Never let a wedding suit outshine the groom's — it's an etiquette issue, not a style one; go conservative (e.g. stone [[linen]] single-breasted).
- Never wear Velcro — on wallets or shoes, it reads as juvenile; use buckles, laces, or slip-ons instead.
- Never wear a pre-tied bow tie — it looks too perfect and artificial; a self-tied bow tie's slight imperfection is what makes it elegant.
- Never wear sweatpants outside the gym or workout — Karl Lagerfeld's line "sweatpants are a sign of defeat" anchors this; swap in linen drawstring trousers for lounging.
- Never match a pocket square and tie exactly — coordinate shade/tone but keep pattern and color distinct, or skip the pocket square entirely.
- Never wear flip-flops off the beach or poolside — loafers, espadrilles, or leather sandals are the summer alternatives.
- Never chase a hairstyle trend (undercut, man bun) — classic cuts (side part, slick-back, crew cut) age better and suit more face shapes.
- Never skip black tie when the invite says "optional," and never wear black tie when the dress code doesn't call for it — both misjudgments read as socially off.

### High-Yield Assets
- Quote: Karl Lagerfeld — "sweatpants are a sign of defeat."
- Footwear alternatives to flip-flops: loafers, espadrilles, leather sandals.
- Pocket square fallback: white presidential fold.
- Suit recommendation for a summer wedding: stone linen, single-breasted.
- Classic hairstyle options named: side part, slicked-back, crew cut.

### Case Studies/Anecdotes
- The presenter cites a video where a woman named a Velcro wallet as her biggest dating turn-off — "at least he's still pulling out his wallet," but leather is non-negotiable.
- He notes he isn't wearing a pocket square with his own outfit in the video, using it as a live example that "no pocket square" is itself a valid choice.

### The "So What?" Factor
Restraint is a style signal. Skipping logos, matching sets, trend haircuts, and dress-code mismatches does more for perceived status than any single purchase — the omissions are what separate a gentleman from someone just wearing expensive clothes.
