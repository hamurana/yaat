**The Hook & Core Thesis**

Investing isn't about picking the perfect stock — it's about building a **system that decides for you when your instincts can't**. Run every opportunity through fixed rules, a comparison lens, and a clear sense of your role, and the paralysis disappears.

---
**Key Arguments Checklist**

- **A system beats stock-picking.** Without one, people freeze, treat everything as equally risky, or sit on cash and chase headlines (FOMO into Bitcoin/gold).
- **Time is your best business partner.** [[Compounding]] works slowly, then better than any partner you'll find — so start as early as possible.
- **It's not what you make, it's what you keep.** Taxes and fees are the biggest [[friction]] on wealth.
- **Real risk is not understanding what you own** — specifically, where you sit in the **[[capital stack]]**.
- **Vet deals with the [[Four Goods]]:** good people, good intentions, good rationale, then good contracts.
- **Score investments on four axes** to compare them objectively.
- **Know your role:** active, thematic, or passive — and "if it's passive for you, it's active for someone else."

---
**High-Yield Assets**

**Rule 1 — Compounding / time in the market.** Three savers: one never invests, one starts-and-stops repeatedly, one invests and lets time take over — the third wins big. "Time is your business partner." Applied: his kids **Lara (14, graphic design)** and **Neil (9, good with AI)** hold real paid jobs in his business; he routes their income into a **[[Roth IRA]]** to compound (they don't know yet).

**Rule 2 — Friction (taxes & fees).** On good returns you may lose **~1/3 to taxes** and **another 10–20% to fees** (fees hide because they compound), leaving only **50–60%** as real return. Reduce friction via: tax-efficient vehicles (**[[IRA]]/[[401k]]** — no tax at the time), **[[Opportunity Zones]]** (US; hold 10+ years → tax-advantaged), and **[[depreciation]]** (book losses against income). Maxim: *"if you can defer your taxes, you've actually avoided your taxes."*

**Rule 3 — Risk has two layers.** (1) Can you explain how the business works? If not, don't invest. (2) Where do you sit in the **[[capital stack]]** — because if the business fails, you're paid top-down. Using **Amazon** as the example: **senior debt** (a bank, e.g. Bank of America) paid first → **mezzanine debt** (an investment bank, e.g. Goldman Sachs) → **preferred equity** (voting/board control, e.g. Jeff Bezos) → **common equity** (you, last). Home analogy: mortgage (bank) first, your equity second. Implication: lending as **debt** is often safer than investing as **equity**.

**The [[Four Goods]] (deal checklist):**
1. **Good people** — "you can't make a good deal with a bad person." Do a **mutual background check** (offer to be vetted, ask to vet them).
2. **Good intentions** — under pressure people act on their incentives; know why they're in it and how much **skin in the game** they have. "It's not *if* things go wrong, it's *when*."
3. **Good rationale** — a "spreadsheet problem": ask for their **financial model**; serious companies send it instantly, others stall because it doesn't exist.
4. **Good contracts** — only after the first three are right.

**The comparison scorecard (4 axes, 25 points each / 100):**
- **Capital preservation** (will you get your money back?), **tax efficiency**, **cash flow/yield**, **growth**.
- Worked examples: **Apple** ≈ 20/0/5/25 = **50**; **Bitcoin** ≈ 5/0/0/25 = **30**. Arbitrary but a fast way to compare.

**Three investor roles:**
- **Active** — full-time/professional, does the day-to-day work (real-estate operator, trader, PE manager). He was a professional investor at Goldman Sachs.
- **Thematic** — bets on a theme via ETFs/baskets (tech ETF, AI ETF, India ETF, gold).
- **Passive** — gives capital to active investors (e.g. multifamily syndication operator; a hedge fund; he's a passive LP in **[[Andreessen Horowitz]] (a16z)**'s reported $15B fund). Key rule: *if it's passive for you, it's active for someone else* — a "daisy chain" of passive-into-passive is where scams live.

**Three compressed lessons (from ~20 years):**
1. **Time in the market > timing the market.** A study of three entrants — best timing, worst timing, all-cash — showed best timing won, *worst timing came second*, and **all-cash lost**. Sitting on the sidelines is the worst outcome.
2. **Taxes are the #1 drag** — after-tax, after-fee returns are the real returns.
3. **Risk & reward** — reward is upside, risk is downside protection; manage it by knowing your spot in the capital stack.

**Case Studies / Anecdotes**

- **The $1M loss:** after **six months consulting** for a company that was doing well, he invested **$1M — $800k as debt, $200k as equity** — reasoning the protected debt would shield the equity. Within **weeks**, the CEO/founder **vanished**, the company folded; he even hired a **private investigator** and couldn't find him. The whole investment went to zero — the experience that made him "gun shy," document his learnings, and create the Four Goods.
- **His kids' Roth IRAs:** Lara and Neil's real wages compounding quietly until the day they open the accounts and see six figures — his living proof of compounding.

---
**The "So What?" Factor**

Stop guessing. Build a repeatable pipeline: (1) start early to let compounding work, (2) judge returns *after* taxes and fees using tax-advantaged vehicles, (3) know your position in the capital stack before you commit, (4) screen deals with the Four Goods, (5) score options on preservation/tax/cash flow/growth, and (6) decide whether you're active, thematic, or passive. Pick one investment you've been stuck on and run it through the system today.
