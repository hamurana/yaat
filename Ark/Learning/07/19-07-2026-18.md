## The Hook & Core Thesis
Most men fall into a shaving routine they're not happy with because "you don't know what you don't know." Distilled from interviewing 3,000+ men and testing 200+ products: nine shaving mistakes, whose common root is **using a blade that isn't sharp enough and not lubricating properly**. A sharp blade plus high-fat lubrication beats any multi-blade cartridge system on both quality and cost.

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **Sharpness is everything, and cartridge marketing hides it.** Gillette/Dollar Shave Club advertise convenience, handles, lubrication strips, and blade count — never blade sharpness, because a duller blade means fewer cuts but worse shaves and more repeat cartridge sales.
- **Judge cost per shave over ten years, not upfront price.** A free handle is deceptive; cartridges compound to ~$1,000+/decade while a double-edge razor lands under $200.
- **Technique matters as much as tools** — hair growth direction and multi-pass shaving determine closeness and irritation.
- **Lubrication is the only thing protecting your skin** — "there's no such thing as over-lubricating," and cheap aerosol foams don't have enough fat.

## High-Yield Assets (the 9 mistakes)
1. **Blades not sharp enough.** Cartridge systems (2–8 cents… actually $2–8 per cartridge) are a moneymaker with dull blades that cause razor bumps and irritation. Options in ascending quality: **double-edge (DE) razors** (blades 3–30¢ each; grandpa's method); modern **single-blade systems** (**Bevel**, **OneBlade**) that use sharper, thicker blades. His personal favorite: a **Feather blade** (very sharp) in a slanted **Merkur** razor (more aggressive).
2. **Focusing only on upfront cost and convenience, not quality and cost-per-shave.** The math (shaving 5×/week): Dollar Shave Club ≈ $8/mo → ~$1,000/decade; latest Gillette ≈ $17–18/mo → ~$2,100/decade; **DE razor** ≈ $50–60 handle + <$1.50/mo → **under $200/decade** (~11× cheaper than Gillette); electric ≈ $200 upfront + head/yr → ~$800/decade (quicker, not as good); straight razor ≈ $166 + $40/yr sharpening → ~$566/decade (best quality, slowest). "Once you've had a good shave, a cartridge razor literally isn't going to cut it."
3. **Not understanding your facial hair's growth direction.** Hair grows different directions on different parts of the face; shaving straight down blindly cuts with, across, and against the grain unevenly. Spend ~10 minutes at the mirror mapping your growth (some sketch arrows on a face diagram) to minimize passes and irritation.
4. **Not shaving in multiple passes.** Closest shave comes from going against the grain (the blade lifts the hair and cuts below skin level), but against-the-grain alone is too aggressive. Proper DE sequence: **pass 1 with the grain** (removes bulk, least aggressive) → **pass 2 across the grain** → **pass 3 against the grain** (closest). His shortcut: with-the-grain then one against.
5. **Not properly lubricating skin before/while shaving.** Lubrication lets the sharp blade glide over skin cutting hair not skin. Lubrication comes from **fat** — the higher the fat, the better the glide. Aerosol foams and transparent gels (like cheap canned **Nivea**) are mostly air and chemicals, low fat. Rule of thumb: **less water = more fat**; creams in metal/plastic **tubes** and **hard shaving soaps** have far more fat than spray cans.
6. **Not using a shaving brush to build lather.** A brush creates a rich lather and lifts hair follicles off the skin for a better shave. Cheap brushes use **boar hair** (harsh); he prefers **badger**, best quality being **silvertip** (finer). High-end brushes can exceed $200. If you skip the brush, a **high-fat cream applied by finger gets you 95% there**.
7. **Not incorporating an electric razor into the routine.** For speed: electric + high-quality shaving cream ≈ **3–4 minutes**, the best value-to-time ratio. To approximate a DE shave faster: electric with cream first to remove bulk (~2–3 min), then **one pass against the grain** — ~8–10 minutes total, same day-long smoothness. Electric also travels in carry-on where a DE blade can't.
8. **Not following a proper pre-shave routine.** Hot water/hot towel (or a hot shower) to soften, then a **high-fat pre-shave oil** (barrier), then high-fat cream (lathered with a brush). Adds 2–10 minutes but essential for razor bumps, irritation, sensitivity, or very sharp blades. Finish with an **aftershave/post-shave** cream to soothe. His time-saver: shave right after a hot shower.
9. **Giving up a new routine too quickly.** A friend switched to a DE razor, cut himself due to unrefined technique, and reverted. Technique takes practice — **try it at least 10 times** to find the right blade angle before judging.

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **His own evolution**: taught by his dad to use a **Gillette Mach 3**, which he disliked for years until he discovered better options — the origin of the yearlong, 200-product testing project behind the guide.
- **The friend who quit**: excited by the results on the host's skin, adopted DE shaving, cut himself, and went back — the concrete illustration of mistake #9.

## The "So What?" Factor
Switch to a sharp blade (a DE razor with a Feather blade, or a single-blade system like Bevel/OneBlade), pair it with a high-fat cream or soap (tube/hard soap, not aerosol), and map your hair's growth so you can do a with-then-against-grain pass sequence. Add a hot-shower pre-shave and a soothing aftershave. It's cheaper over a decade than cartridges by roughly 10×, gives a closer shave, and — once you commit to at least ten practice shaves — becomes routine.
