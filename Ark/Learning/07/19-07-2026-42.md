## The Hook & Core Thesis
[[Levi's]] (an American original making jeans since **1853**) has one big problem: **too many confusingly-numbered styles** whose numbers aren't sequential and change over the decades. This guide cuts through it by organizing every model **by fit** — skinny → slim → tapered → straight → boot cut → relaxed/loose — so you can find the right pair for your body type. Any man can wear any fit, but you generally want the one that works with your build.

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **Ignore the numbers; shop by fit.** The model numbers are historical accidents, not a logical sequence — organizing by fit is the only sane way to navigate them.
- **Match the cut to your build** — thin guys suit skinny/slim; heavier or athletic guys suit tapered/relaxed cuts with room in thigh and seat.
- **Modern stretch is a feature, not a compromise** — a bit of elastane (or even ~25% polyester blends) improves close-fitting jeans; don't insist on 100% cotton.
- **The 501 is the ancestor of everything** — 150+ years of a nearly-unchanged straight fit; most other numbers are variations of it or of the 510/511.

## High-Yield Assets (models by fit)
**Skinny** (501 skinny, 510, 519): the **510** is the main one — sits just below the waist, close to the body all the way down. Skinny works for genuinely thin guys (waist ~27–29); heavier-set men should be cautious. Comes in black, gray, dark indigo, medium, and light washes — for versatility choose **dark, black, or gray**. The **519** is extremely skinny and rare.
**Slim** (511, 513, 512/formerly 522): the **511** is "cut close but not restrictive" — the best pick for young/fit-but-thin men wanting freedom of movement (not baggy like a straight cut). The **513** ("slim straight") gives more room in waist and thigh with a lighter taper, for huskier-but-in-shape guys with bigger thighs/seat. The **512** (a "slim taper," formerly 522) is like the 511 up top but with a closer taper — about 1.5″ less leg-opening diameter.
**Tapered** (502, 501 Custom Tapered, and 512 which overlaps slim): the star is the **502** — a modernized 501 with more thigh room, a tapered bottom, more fabric options (even khaki-style), versatile enough to dress up with a sports jacket or dress shoes. The 501 stays wide/straight at the bottom; the 502 is the tapered modern update. Looser tapered options step up through the **541** and **550**.
**Straight** (501, 505, 514, 513, 5501, 5509): the **501** is the original straight cut — straight from thigh to leg opening (looks loose to a modern eye), with a classic **button fly**, the widest waist and color range (including green, medium/light washes), plus **shrink-to-fit**, **big & tall**, and reissue **1984/1993** versions. Modern 501s have some stretch, so you may size down. Variants: **505** (more relaxed thighs), **514** (lower rise, zipper instead of button fly), **513** (closer down the leg), **5501/5509** (more relaxed straight).
**Boot cut** (517, 527): the **517** is the original cowboy jean — 100% cotton, straight to the knee then a wider leg opening for boots. The **527** is the modern version — some stretch, sits a bit lower on the waist, more comfortable.
**Relaxed / Loose** (541, 550, 5509, plus 560/569/570): Levi's distinguishes **relaxed** (looser through hips, waist, upper leg) from **loose** (lots of room down the legs, closer up top). The **541** is the **athletic fit** — room for a larger seat and thick thighs while fitting the waist. The **550** (since 1985) are the affectionately-named **"dad jeans"** — classic relaxed weekend fit. The **5509** is a more modern relaxed cut sitting lower with a larger leg opening.

## Fabric & Fit Notes
- Most Levi's are **cotton with ~1%+ stretch** (elastane); a bit of stretch is good for close-fitting jeans, and modern poly/cotton blends (even ~25% poly) can improve performance — don't fear the blend.
- Because different styles have stretch, **your waist size can differ between models**; try on or check the model's fabric.

## The "So What?" Factor
Don't get lost in Levi's numbers — pick by fit for your build: thin → **510/511**; fit-but-husky → **513**; want a modern versatile everyday jean → the **502** (tapered, dressable); want the timeless original → the **501** (straight, button fly); boots → **517/527**; athletic seat/thighs → **541**; relaxed weekend comfort → **550**. Choose a dark, black, or gray wash for maximum versatility, and welcome a little stretch for close-fitting cuts.
