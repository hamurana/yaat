## The Hook & Core Thesis
The version of aging most people ignore isn't cancer or dementia — it's the quiet loss of the ability to do things that make life worth living (carry shopping, rise from a chair, climb stairs). [[Protein]] sits at the centre of that decline, and **the gap between what most people eat (~0.8 g/kg) and what the evidence supports (1.6–2 g/kg) is massive.** Protein is one of the most important — and most underrated — determinants of how well you age.

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **Muscle is biological capital.** Muscle mass in your 40s–50s is the reserve that determines whether you're independent or frail in your 70s–80s.
- **The official RDA is a floor, not a target.** 0.8 g/kg prevents deficiency; it was never meant to optimize muscle maintenance or account for age-related [[anabolic resistance]].
- **Maintaining muscle may beat a blockbuster drug.** Its mortality-risk effect appears larger than metformin's.
- **Overeating is often a protein problem, not willpower** — the protein leverage hypothesis explains why protein-poor ultra-processed food keeps you hungry.

## High-Yield Assets
- **Protein is everything structural and functional**: muscle is ~80% protein by dry weight, but so is **collagen** (skin, tendons, ligaments); **insulin** is a protein; **antibodies**, digestive **enzymes**, DNA-repair molecules, and oxygen transporters are all proteins — built from ~20 amino acids, assembled and disassembled at **millions of molecules per second** for life.
- **Inadequate protein → the body cannibalizes muscle.** Muscle is the amino-acid reservoir; chronic underfeeding quietly dismantles the tissue that determines functional capacity.
- **[[Sarcopenia]]** (pathologically low muscle mass/strength): muscle declines ~**8%/decade after 30** if uncountered — compounded over 40–50 years it's severe. It causes falls, fractures, loss of independence, and death (the elderly person who falls and can't get up; the hip fracture with no muscle reserve for rehab; the manageable illness turned life-threatening).
- **The mortality data**: a **2018 study of 4,500+ adults 50+** found low muscle mass was linked to a **40–50% higher all-cause mortality** over the next decade — and it was muscle **strength** (functional), not just volume on a scan, that drove it. For context, **metformin** (one of the world's most-prescribed drugs) reduces all-cause mortality by ~**15–30%** — so maintaining muscle may have a *larger* effect than a top medication.
- **The intake numbers**: UK/US RDA ≈ **0.8 g/kg** (~60g for a 75kg adult) — the *minimum to prevent deficiency* in a sedentary adult, not the target. Two decades of evidence point to **1.6–2 g/kg** for maintaining muscle and healthy aging (**120–150g/day** for a 75kg person — 2–3× the RDA). One paper found older adults need **≥1.6 g/kg just to *prevent* muscle loss** (not build it) — showing how outdated the guidance is. Higher protein also aids satiety, glucose regulation, and metabolic health.
- **The protein leverage hypothesis** (David Raubenheimer & Stephen Simpson): the body chases a **protein target**, not a calorie target, and keeps driving hunger until it's met. Ultra-processed food (white bread, crisps, biscuits, cereal, snack bars, ready meals, pizza) is **calorie-dense and protein-poor** and engineered (texture, salt-to-sugar ratio, mouthfeel) to be irresistible — so you overeat calories while staying protein-undernourished. **Not a willpower problem — biology in an environment it wasn't designed for.** Prioritizing protein at every meal reduces overall hunger and calorie intake almost automatically.
- **How to hit 1.6–2 g/kg** (takes intention — doesn't happen by accident on a Western diet):
  - **Animal sources** are most protein-dense and provide **complete protein** (all essential amino acids): oily fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, chicken, red meat in moderation.
  - **Plant sources** tend to be lower in **lysine and methionine** and less bioavailable — a plant-based diet can meet needs but takes more thought and variety; build meals around tofu, lentils, legumes, peas, beans.
  - **Distribution matters**: there's a per-meal ceiling on muscle protein synthesis (~**40–50g**, varying with age/size), so spread protein across **3–4 meals** rather than one shake.
  - **A protein-rich breakfast is the single highest-impact meal change** (eggs, 10% Greek yogurt, smoked salmon, cottage cheese instead of toast/cereal) — front-loading protein sets a different hunger/hormonal trajectory for 8–10 hours and gets ahead of the leverage effect.
- **Protein + resistance training are two halves of one thing**: protein is the raw material, training is the stimulus. Loading a muscle causes microdamage that repairs thicker/stronger. **Two sessions/week of compound movements** (squats, deadlifts, rows, presses) plus adequate protein meaningfully pushes back age-related decline — and works into the 60s, 70s, even 80s. Bonus effects: better insulin sensitivity, less visceral fat, stronger bones, lower blood pressure, and **BDNF** (drives neuroplasticity and cognition).

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **The polypharmacy cascade**: the older patient on 10–12 drugs (blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol, joint pain, a stomach protector for the joint-pain drug, mood, sleep) — each managing a symptom without touching the cause. Muscle loss → insulin resistance → metabolic dysfunction → type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease → less movement → more drugs, a process years/decades in the making.
- **The opposite patients**: those who arrive in their 70s–80s on *fewer* meds, with better function, bouncing back faster — almost universally people who kept their muscle and ate to support metabolic health for decades. "That's not luck, that's their biology — stacking the deck."
- **The closing line from a decade in A&E**: "I never once looked after a patient who was too strong, too well-nourished, or had too much muscle — but I've looked after lots whose outcomes were directly shaped by the absence of those things."

## The "So What?" Factor
Treat protein as medicine, not a bodybuilding supplement. Aim for **1.6–2 g/kg/day** (roughly double the RDA), spread across **3–4 meals** with a **protein-rich breakfast** to blunt all-day hunger, favoring complete animal sources (or well-varied plant sources for lysine/methionine). Pair it with **two weekly resistance sessions of compound lifts** — the protein is the material, the training the stimulus. Muscle built in midlife is the reserve that decides independence vs. frailty later. Frailty isn't inevitable; it's usually a nutritional and lifestyle deficit that was never addressed.
