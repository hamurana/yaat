## The Hook & Core Thesis
[[Dr Alex]] (10 years across ~12 UK emergency departments) argues your stroke outcome is decided years before the ambulance: ~90% of all strokes trace back to just 10 modifiable risk factors, and five mostly-free habits — led by simply *measuring* your blood pressure — prevent more strokes than anything an ER can do once "time is brain" is already running.

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **Know and control your blood pressure — by far the most important.** In the [[INTERSTROKE Study]] (~27,000 strokes across 32 countries), high blood pressure alone was linked to roughly *half* of all strokes. It does its damage silently for decades — almost everyone is asymptomatic until readings hit crisis levels (180–220+ systolic; he saw a 245 in a ~50-year-old the week before filming). The fix: a cheap home monitor, 2–3 readings at a similar time at least weekly, averaged and logged. Target below 120/80 for most people — and unless you're older, the lower the better. "You cannot manage what you never measure."
- **Keep blood sugar in a healthy range.** Persistently high glucose — [[Type 2 Diabetes]] and prediabetes — scars the delicate lining of blood vessels and accelerates the clotting that causes ischemic stroke, and it "gangs up" with high blood pressure. Get an HbA1c test at least every six months if at any risk: aim below 42 mmol/mol (UK) / 5.7% (US). A [[Continuous Glucose Monitor]] worn on the back of the upper arm shows in real time how foods, fasting, stress, morning-vs-evening exercise, and added fiber move your glucose.
- **Build muscle and keep moving.** Physical inactivity was the #2 risk factor in INTERSTROKE, and regular activity was linked to a ~40% lower stroke risk. Muscle acts as a storage tank for glucose — more muscle pulls sugar out of the bloodstream before it can damage arteries — while exercise simultaneously lowers blood pressure, improves cholesterol, and trims waist fat (another of the 10 factors). Prescription: two strength sessions a week (bodyweight squats, lunges, press-ups count) plus daily walking.
- **Clean up the rest of the lifestyle**: quit smoking (risk starts falling quickly after stopping); swap [[Ultra-Processed Foods]] back for whole food; and on alcohol the science has moved on — *there is no safe amount*, and his honest advice for serious stroke prevention is teetotal, not "cutting down."
- **Learn the warning signs and act immediately.** A transient ischemic attack (TIA / "mini-stroke") — minutes of one-sided weakness, numbness, slurred speech, a drooping or tingling face, or brief vision loss that then passes — is the brain "screaming that the big one may be on its way." After a TIA the risk of a full stroke within 90 days can be as high as **1 in 6, with almost half striking within just two days** — but urgent assessment and treatment cuts that risk by around 80% in some studies. Remember **FAST**: Face, Arms, Speech, Time to call an ambulance — even if symptoms lasted only 10–20 seconds.
- **Lifestyle is the foundation, not a replacement for medication.** Medication and lifestyle are far more powerful together; never refuse a prescribed blood-pressure drug, aspirin/blood thinner, or statin on the strength of a video.

## High-Yield Assets
- **The INTERSTROKE study**: ~27,000 strokes, 32 countries; ~90% of strokes attributable to 10 modifiable risk factors; hypertension ≈ half of all strokes; inactivity the #2 factor (~40% risk reduction from regular activity).
- **Stroke mechanics**: ischemic (clot, ~8–9 of every 10 strokes) vs. hemorrhagic (burst vessel/bleed); downstream brain tissue dies by the millions of cells per minute; ER response = immediate CT scan, then clot-busting drug or mechanical thrombectomy.
- **Numbers to know**: BP target <120/80 (most adults); HbA1c <42 mmol/mol UK / <5.7% US, retested every 6 months if at risk; post-TIA 90-day stroke risk up to 1 in 6, ~half within 48 hours, cut ~80% by urgent care.
- **[[Atrial Fibrillation]] caveat**: an irregular heartbeat lets clots form in the heart and fire off to the brain — may require a specific anticoagulant such as **rivaroxaban or warfarin**. Occasionally checking your own pulse for an odd irregular rhythm is worth doing; Apple Watch, Oura Ring, and similar wearables detect it fairly accurately. Clotting disorders likewise need specialist care.
- **The FAST mnemonic**: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call emergency services.

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **The 245-systolic patient**: a patient in their 50s walked into his ER the week before with a systolic pressure of 245 — living proof that dangerous hypertension can feel like nothing until it announces itself with pounding headaches and flashing lights.
- **His wife's low baseline**: consistently under 100 systolic — during labor she sat below 95, and every new midwife triggered alarm bells assuming she was hemorrhaging. His point: everyone's baseline differs, so you must know *yours*.
- **The pattern he sees after strokes**: patients repeatedly tell him that in the days or weeks before, they had brief odd episodes — an arm going numb, speech going strange, vision dropping out for seconds — and brushed every one of them off.

## The "So What?" Factor
Buy a home blood-pressure cuff this week and start a log — it is the single highest-leverage stroke prevention that exists. Add a twice-yearly HbA1c if you're at any metabolic risk, two strength sessions a week, and treat any fleeting one-sided weakness or speech glitch as an emergency-room trip, not a curiosity. Prevention is decided years before the ambulance; the five habits are mostly free and beat every rescue medicine has.
