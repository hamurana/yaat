## The Hook & Core Thesis
Every man should tie his own [[Bow Tie]] — pre-tied and clip-on versions are for "boys at prom," and their giveaway is being *too* perfect. A real bow tie's slight asymmetry is the proof of character, not a flaw. [[Gentleman's Gazette]] then demonstrates the simplest tying method step by step.

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **Pre-tied always shows.** Too symmetrical, too perfect, looks cheap. Elegant men like [[Winston Churchill]] wore theirs slightly askew — the askew knot *is* the signal of a self-tied bow.
- **Setup first**: adjustable bow ties get set to your shirt neck size (then tweak to taste); fixed-length ties (like Fort Belvedere's black bow ties) need no adjustment. Throw it around the neck, collar down, top button closed, pull the inside neat.
- **The method, condensed**:
  1. Left end hangs 1–1.5 inches longer than the right.
  2. Long over short, pinch, long end up through the neck loop, pull tight.
  3. Fold the short end into a bow shape using the index finger between body and tie.
  4. Fold the long end down over its front.
  5. The key move: hold the front bow together, find the small hole *behind* it, fold the hanging end into a second bow shape, and push that folded shape through the hole.
  6. Pull on the folded ends to tighten the knot.
- **Tighten by the folds, not the blades** — pulling the folded ends is what snugs the knot.

## High-Yield Assets
- **Length rule**: start with the left side ~1–1.5" longer.
- **Sizing rule**: adjustable band ≈ your shirt collar size.
- **Fort Belvedere**: his shop's self-tie bow ties in multiple colors, shapes, and materials (the video's only product reference).

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **The Churchill standard**: Churchill's deliberately askew bow tie as the historical proof that imperfection is the mark of the genuine article.

## The "So What?" Factor
Learn the fold-and-push-through move (step 5) — it's the only non-obvious part of the whole knot, and everything before it is just an overhand knot with a longer left end. Then stop fearing asymmetry: a slightly crooked self-tied bow outranks a perfect pre-tied one every time.
