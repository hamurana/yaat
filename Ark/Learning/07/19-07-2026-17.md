## The Hook & Core Thesis
Many young men grow up without a strong masculine role model — some without a man in the house at all. Beyond changing a tyre or "the birds and the bees," there's a code of conduct made of small details every gentleman understands. Eight such rules, framed as the things your dad should have passed down but probably didn't.

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **Take pride in your appearance and dress with intent.** Several rules (tie worn properly, a real watch, a fresh haircut with product) share one idea: small deliberate choices signal a man of purpose.
- **Restraint and humility are the mark of a gentleman.** Understated wedding dress and the closing "less is more" both counsel against showing off — "in a world of Andrew Tates, be a Cary Grant."
- **Conduct matters as much as clothing.** Body language, demeanor, and how you treat people are part of the code, not just wardrobe.
- **Some rules are about character and consent** — never sending unsolicited explicit photos is both ungentlemanly and potentially illegal.

## High-Yield Assets (the 8 rules)
1. **If you wear a tie, wear it properly.** A loose, scruffy tie on a commuter or at the bar reads like a schoolboy at 4 o'clock. Wearing a loose tie elegantly (as [[sprezzatura]]) is very hard to pull off — most men should avoid it. A gentleman takes pride in his appearance.
2. **Never send a photo of your private parts.** Vulgar and ungentlemanly; the less you know the recipient, the worse the offense, and an unsolicited image can bring serious legal trouble. Even in a committed, long-distance relationship where it's mutually wanted, "there's nothing wrong with making her wait for the real thing."
3. **Take care of your skin.** Skincare wasn't a mainstream idea in your dad's day, so he can't be blamed for not teaching it — but every man should now understand its value. A quality routine (face wash, exfoliating scrub twice weekly, AM moisturizer with SPF, PM moisturizer) makes a dramatic difference to appearance. *(Presented via the video's skincare sponsor; product pitch omitted per note-taking policy — the rule itself is the content.)*
4. **Always wear a timepiece.** A wristwatch is "the only accessory a man truly needs." Choose a classic or modern watch, but keep Fitbits/Apple Watches/fitness trackers for the gym or sleep. Checking your wrist reads as more confident body language than pulling out your phone; a watch signals you dressed with intent. Affordable quality brands: **Seiko, Citizen, Orient**. A watch is a great father-to-son gift, especially a family heirloom.
5. **Wear an understated suit to a wedding (as a guest).** The day isn't about you — don't distract from the groom. Skip the double-breasted pinstripe; a **navy suit** or a **stone linen suit** for summer is safe. Ask other guests what they're wearing and align. Be better-dressed via cut, fabric, simple accessories, and shined shoes — not attention-grabbing loudness. "A wedding is not the place to dress like you're attending *Pretty Woman*."
6. **Mind your body language and demeanor.** It speaks as clearly as words, yet is never taught in school. Fix screen-hunched posture with correction exercises and lifestyle changes; walk with head up, looking ahead, at a steady even pace; speak clearly and slowly, enunciating, without shouting.
7. **Get a haircut at least once a month — and use a hair product.** Scruffy hair throws off an otherwise clean look; make a **standing appointment** with your barber. In his consultations he's amazed how many men unhappy with their hair use no product at all — you need product to hold a style. He uses an **oil-based pomade** for a traditional look; waxes, creams, and gels suit other styles. Monthly cut + styling product are "two non-negotiables."
8. **Less is more.** Everyone wants more money, cars, women, success, status — but the real value is in simple pleasures (fishing in quiet, watching the sunrise, sitting by a fire with a good book). Be ambitious but peaceful and humble; carry yourself with tact, treat everyone with dignity and grace, and don't be boastful. "In a world of Andrew Tates, you be a Cary Grant."

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **The school-tie memory**: at his school, every boy loosened his tie and top button the moment the 4 o'clock bell rang, looking like "a complete scruff" by the gates — the image a loose-tied commuter now evokes for him.
- **His heirloom watch**: worn by his great-grandfather, grandfather, and great-uncle before him — his pride in the lineage grounds the "always wear a timepiece" rule and the father-to-son gift idea.
- **The closing contrast — "Andrew Tate vs. Cary Grant"** — crystallizes the humility argument into a single choice of role model.

## The "So What?" Factor
Adopt the code as small non-negotiables: wear a tie (or nothing) properly, keep a real watch on your wrist, book a standing monthly haircut and actually use product, care for your skin, and dress with restraint — loudest at a wedding is a mistake. Underneath the wardrobe, the deeper rules are character: consent and discretion, upright body language, and humility. Be ambitious but gracious — the Cary Grant, not the show-off.
