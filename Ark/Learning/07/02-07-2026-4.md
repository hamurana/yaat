**The Hook & Core Thesis**

[[Creatine]] is a genuine but *tiny* add-on for people over 50 — a multiplier on strength training, not a muscle or brain pill. It won't prevent dementia, and anyone with reduced kidney function shouldn't take it.

---
**Key Arguments Checklist**

- **It's a multiplier, not a source.** Creatine does "close to nothing" for muscle unless you're already lifting weights — "no training means no stimulus."
- **The muscle evidence is strong; the brain evidence is narrow.** Solid RCT backing for muscle/strength; brain benefit is limited to short-term memory and processing speed only.
- **It does NOT reduce dementia or Alzheimer's risk.** "Anybody telling you otherwise is selling you something."
- **The basics dwarf it.** Walking, resistance training, whole food, steady blood sugar, sleep — "nothing in a tub will ever come close."
- **A creatinine blood-test artifact scares people needlessly** — it raises the number without actual kidney harm in healthy people.
- **Some people genuinely shouldn't take it** — reduced kidney function or pregnancy.

---
**High-Yield Assets**

What creatine is:

- A **natural compound** the body makes daily (mostly in the **liver**), topped up by eating **meat and fish** — plants have virtually none, so **vegetarians/vegans run lower stores** and often respond more noticeably. The supplement is lab-made, so it's vegan-suitable.
- Works like a **rechargeable battery** for short, explosive bursts of energy — stored mostly in muscle, a smaller important amount in the brain. With age the body makes less *and* most people eat less meat/fish, so stores drop just when needed most.

The evidence:

- **Muscle (strongest):** a large analysis of **22 RCTs, 700+ older adults** found creatine + resistance training added **~1.5 kg of lean muscle** vs identical training without it, plus real strength gains. Newer work: **best benefit in the legs, weakest in the upper body.** But only in people already lifting.
- **Brain (narrow):** reliably helps **short-term memory** and **mental processing speed** only — not overall thinking, planning, or problem-solving, and **not dementia risk.** The memory benefit shows most in the **oldest adults (late 60s–80s)**, barely in the young, and most when the brain is stressed (poor sleep, illness). Unlike muscle, the brain benefit needs no "training" — it comes from simply keeping the energy tank fuller.

Safety & dosing:

- **Creatine monohydrate** is one of the most studied supplements; in healthy people it hasn't been shown to harm kidneys or liver (the old kidney fear traces to a few case reports). Minor effects: mild stomach upset (from too much at once) and **~2 weeks of small weight gain that is water in the muscle, not fat.**
- **The creatinine artifact:** creatine raises blood **creatinine ~10%**, which lowers the estimated **eGFR** and makes a healthy kidney look like it's declining — a *measurement artifact*, confirmed normal by other markers. Fix: tell whoever takes your blood, or **stop creatine ~1 week before a blood test.**
- **Dose:** **3–5 g/day** plain creatine monohydrate (white powder or tablet). **No timing needed**, **no loading phase** (the "20 g/day for 5 days" on labels is unnecessary). Buy from a **reputable brand, not random Amazon sellers.**
- **Who shouldn't:** safety studies excluded people with disease, so there's no good evidence it's safe for an already-struggling kidney. **Kidney disease = eGFR persistently below 60** → avoid. Also avoid in **pregnancy.** Over 40–50 or with medical problems → check with a doctor first.

**Case Studies / Anecdotes**

- **The staircase trip.** Same stumble, two endings: one version fractures a hip — "the ambulance, the hospital, sometimes the nursing home," muscle wasting, and for too many a slide into frailty that "ends in death inside a year." The other grabs the banister and carries on, because they were strong enough to catch themselves. The point of the "sliver of extra strength."
- **The aero triathlon helmet.** Starting from unfit, Dr Alex wanted a shortcut and eyed an aerodynamic helmet. The shop owner was honest: the helmet only helps if you're *already* fast enough for wind resistance to matter — "the gains come from the fitness." Creatine is the same: without the training underneath, the powder has nothing to multiply.
- **The credibility note:** Dr Alex says he's turned down "tens and tens of thousands of pounds" — multiples of his doctor salary — from supplement brands, and never sells supplements on the channel.

---
**The "So What?" Factor**

Master the foundations first — resistance training, whole food, sleep, steady blood sugar — because they do far more for muscle *and* brain than any powder. Only then does **3–5 g/day of creatine monohydrate** buy a small, real edge in leg strength and short-term memory that, in an older body, can be the difference between catching yourself and fracturing a hip. But skip the marketing myths: it won't stop dementia, doesn't need loading or timing, and isn't for anyone with reduced kidney function or in pregnancy. Education, not medical advice — check with your doctor.
