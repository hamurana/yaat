## The Hook & Core Thesis
Once you've built a basic investing foundation and have spare cash you can afford to lose, the smartest "upgrades" aren't luxuries — they're things that buy back your time, raise the ceiling on your skills and relationships, and compound. Spend on assets, people, tools, and experiences, not on stuff that just sits there going out of date.

## Key Arguments Checklist
*(Framed as 10 upgrades to make once money starts coming in, in delivery order.)*
- **1 — Upgrade your investments.** After a solid index-fund foundation, spare risk capital can go into individual stocks or alternative assets (classic cars, watches, fine wine, art) that you can "live with and enjoy." Individual stocks carry real risk — pick well.
- **2 — Buy back your time (outsourcing).** The average person spends 4–5 hours/week cleaning — ~234 hours/year. Outsource cleaning, cooking, gardening, driving, dog-walking. Decide with your **effective hourly rate**: annual earnings ÷ hours worked. The moment you can pay someone *less* than that rate to take a task off your hands, seriously consider it.
- **3 — Books.** For the price of a couple of coffees you absorb decades of someone's life in a couple of hundred pages. The cheapest item on the list with a massive ROI.
- **4 — Your partner.** The partner you choose is one of the single biggest factors in how wealthy you become — pick someone who backs your goals and sacrifices with you.
- **5 — Upgrade your teachers (mentors).** Formal education isn't built to make you wealthy. The people you learn from set the ceiling on what you'll achieve. Mentors are now easier to find than ever — free, via events, podcasts, and YouTube. The biggest lesson from his own mentor: **learn a high-income skill.**
- **6 — Upgrade your tools.** A side hustle/business is a "money-making machine" — reinvest its output into better tools (camera, microphone, laptop, AI software) that make it bigger, faster, more efficient. A better tool isn't a cost, it's an investment, and the expense is often tax-deductible.
- **7 — Upgrade your accountant.** Never be a big fish in a small pond — get an accountant used to clients well ahead of your position who've faced challenges you haven't hit yet. To cut the bill, use finance software to manage your own books.
- **8 — A good chair.** Desk-bound work means a comfortable chair that doesn't wreck your back is a genuine long-term investment. He bought his before posting his first YouTube video.
- **9 — Sleep.** Sleep deprivation cuts decision-making ability to the level of being legally drunk. Work hard, but listen to your body — a fitness wearable helps track it.
- **10 — Experiences over things.** Every purchase has three emotional phases; experiences win on two of the three.

## High-Yield Assets
- **His golden outsourcing rule:** if you earn $80,000/year working ~2,000 hours, your effective hourly rate is **$40/hour** — pay less than that to offload a task and it's worth it.
- **Four book recommendations by stage of journey:**
  - **The Millionaire Fastlane** (M.J. DeMarco) — exposes traditional financial advice as a trap; "fastlane" framework to build wealth quickly.
  - **The E-Myth Revisited** (Michael Gerber) — why most small businesses fail; build one that runs without you so you own a business, not a job.
  - **The Little Book of Common Sense Investing** (John Bogle) — the simple hands-off way to grow money that beats the professionals.
  - **How to Win Friends and Influence People** (Dale Carnegie) — everything comes down to relationships.
- **Losing My Virginity** — Richard Branson's biography [transcribed as "Veraging King"]; the book a friend gave him that changed his perspective. Branson was a dyslexic British entrepreneur written off by teachers — mirroring the speaker's own dyslexia and exam failures.
- **The three emotional phases of a purchase** — Anticipation / The Moment / The Aftertaste. Things: medium → high → **low**. Experiences: high → high → **high**. Products get outdated; experiences get retold and last a lifetime.
- **Wealth-and-marriage stats:** married people average ~**77% more wealth per person** than singles, and net worth grows by roughly another **16% per year** they stay married. [Transcript imprecise — figures stated as-heard.]
- **Mentorship stat:** researchers tracked nearly a thousand kids for decades; those who had a mentor went on to earn substantially more.
- **High-income digital skills to learn:** video editing, copywriting, brand design, building website funnels, AI automation. Example — build high-converting sites; if you help a business make an extra $20,000/month, they'll happily pay $2,000–3,000/month.
- **[[Whoop]]** — fitness wearable he uses to track sleep, recovery, and daily strain. **Explicitly stated not sponsored.**

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **The Ford Fiesta XR2 flip:** as a young man he bought one for £1,750 (~$2,350) and sold it for ~£2,700. Held to today it would've been a ~1,000% return as the car's value climbed — his case for cars as appreciating alternative assets.
- **The dartboard stock pick:** in an old video he blindfolded himself and threw a dart at a stock board — it landed on **Samsung**, now sitting at nearly a **500% return**.
- **His ex-girlfriend → wife:** while still broke he warned an early-dating partner that hitting his goals would take real sacrifices, expecting it to scare her off. She backed him fully, helped in the business, and he "upgraded" her to his wife — crediting her with a major impact on everything he built.
- **"Dave" from the flying club:** a businessman, not a formal teacher, who first made him realise starting a business was even an option — the accidental mentor who told him to learn a high-income skill (he learned joinery/carpentry and sold his own products).
- **His son's videography business:** started by saving for a first camera; once his skills outgrew the gear, the speaker reframed a better camera as an investment, not a cost — clients noticed, and he made the money back and more.
- **The stairs accident (~3 years ago):** exhausted and not thinking straight, he slipped on his own stairs, cracked three ribs and punctured a lung, missing his kidney by millimetres — the wake-up call on sleep.
- **Experiences he still treasures:** flying a plane solo, trackside at the Monaco Grand Prix, flying RC helicopters for Ridley Scott on *Black Hawk Down*, and Richard Branson cooking him spaghetti bolognese followed by deep conversation about business.

## The "So What?" Factor
Spare money is a tool — direct it at things that compound rather than depreciate. Calculate your effective hourly rate and start outsourcing below it; reinvest business income into skills, mentors, and better tools (all often tax-deductible); protect the assets that actually produce the money (your sleep, your back, your relationship); and when you do spend on yourself, buy experiences over objects because the memories keep paying out long after the buzz of a purchase fades.
