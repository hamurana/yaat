**The Hook & Core Thesis**

Stop spraying fragrance on your wrists and neck — the **supraclavicular fossa** (the hollow above your collarbone) gives better longevity and a steady "scent bubble." And most reapplication is unnecessary: you've just gone nose-blind.

---
**Key Arguments Checklist**

- **Spray location changes performance.** The collarbone hollow beats wrists/neck for longevity and controlled release.
- **"Nose-blind" ≠ gone.** Olfactory fatigue makes you stop smelling your own scent, so you over-apply. Test with a walk, not a re-spray.
- **Dose is personal — and has a social ceiling.** Find your own "too much"; have mercy on people who don't want to be overpowered.
- **Wear it daily, for yourself.** Fragrance is a mood/confidence tool — "you're wearing this for you."
- **A bottle lasts far longer than people think**, so daily use is affordable.
- **Smell shapes self-perception**, not just how others perceive you.

---
**High-Yield Assets**

- **The best spray spot: the supraclavicular fossa** — the small indentation right above the collarbone. Three reasons: (1) **Heat** — as warm as the neck but less exposed, so it evaporates slower for a steadier release; (2) **Partial clothing coverage** — shielded from sun and wind, the scent lingers and builds a bubble; (3) **The scent bubble + movement** — warm air escapes from under the collar as you move, releasing the fragrance "in small waves." Application: **one spray on each side.**
- **Olfactory fatigue ("nose-blind")** — a human defense mechanism that normalizes surrounding smells so you can detect new ones. It's why you can't smell your own fragrance after a while. *Test:* go outside for **~5 minutes of fresh air**, come back, and smell for it before deciding to reapply.
- **Longevity math:** a **100 ml bottle ≈ ~1,400 sprays**; at 3–4 sprays/day that's roughly **a full year** of use.
- **Reducing overpowering others:** spray on **clothing** or apply less if worried; "everything has a smell."
- **Travel atomizers** — decant into small refillable atomizers (male/female halves push together) instead of hauling a 100–200 ml bottle through airport security; alternatively buy a **50 ml bottle** (more per ounce but pocketable).
- **Layering (advanced, not for beginners):** wear a **light/clean/crisp** scent in the morning and a **heavier/deeper/darker** one later in the day — never the reverse, since the heavy ones last longer and overpower the light ones even hours later.
- **Layering with grooming products:** strong scented shampoo/moisturizer usually dissipates in **15–30 min**; if it lingers all day, switch to unscented (it's "probably overpowering and cheap smelling anyway"). A little residual scent under a fragrance is fine.
- **Safety:** no known cases of "overdosing" on fragrance, but "with anything — even water — you can overdo it." Don't go to excess; spray on clothing or use less if concerned.
- **Concept named:** **[[enclothed cognition]]** — applied loosely here to scent — the idea that certain smells make you feel more confident and "put you in the zone." *(Speaker applies the clothing-research term to fragrance.)*

**The "So What?" Factor**

Change three habits: spray the collarbone hollow (one per side), stop reapplying on the false "it's gone" signal — take a 5-minute walk and re-smell first, and wear something daily because it's for *you*. Dial the dose to your own nose while respecting the people around you, and use travel atomizers so good fragrance actually leaves the shelf. The right scent, worn right, is a cheap and daily confidence lever.
