**The Hook & Core Thesis:** When Antonio Centeno cut his 1,000+ bottle [[Style|fragrance]] collection down to about 10, the deciding factor was almost never how a fragrance smelled — it was whether the bottle was tied to a person, a trip, or a specific memory.

**Key Arguments Checklist:**
- **Emotional attachment beats objective quality as a keep/sell filter.** Several fragrances he calls "good" or even "great" still got sold because he never personally bonded with them; several he rarely wears anymore got kept purely for nostalgia.
- **Irreplaceable memories are the hardest to part with.** Fragrances tied to a specific trip or person (his son, his wife, a late friend's gift) were the ones he refused to sell, even when he could easily rebuy a near-identical bottle.
- **A fragrance that only exists in a specific place is worth protecting.** He distinguishes between scents he could "go out and buy again" versus ones discovered on a one-off trip that can't be cheaply or easily replaced — those get an automatic pass from the purge.
- **Some fragrances earn a permanent spot by working on other people, not himself.** A scent his wife reacts strongly to stays in the collection as what he calls an "aphrodisiac," independent of whether he'd choose it for himself.
- **First-fragrance-that-hooked-you status is sticky.** Two bottles gifted early in his fragrance journey by fellow YouTuber Jeremy Fragrance stayed in the collection as markers of when he first got serious about the hobby, even though he no longer follows where Jeremy's brand has gone.
- **Unisex fragrances require confidence, not permission.** Wearing scents marketed mostly to women (Tom Ford Black Orchid, a coconut-forward tropical scent) taught him that pulling off a fragrance is about wearing it with confidence rather than staying inside gendered category lines.
- **Counterfeiting is a real risk in the fragrance resale market**, which is why he sells his surplus through a marketplace (Rescentage) that vets sellers rather than an anonymous listings site.

**High-Yield Assets — kept ("survivors"):**
- **Viktor & Rolf Spicebomb** — his and his son's shared go-to scent for a full fall/winter/spring; kept purely for nostalgia even though he hasn't worn it in over a year.
- **Atelier Rebul Bosphorus** — discovered in the brand's Istanbul shop on a trip with his son; his personal favorite of the four Atelier Rebul bottles he bought that day, and a scent his wife also loves.
- **Creed Bois du Portugal** — his "old-school barbershop" fragrance, associated with the place his own father got haircuts.
- **Creed Erolfa** — named by Creed's founder using the initials of his wife and children; one of his longtime top summer fragrances.
- **Roja Dove Elysium** — preferred over the entire Creed Aventus line for being more uplifting, easier to find, and still exclusive-feeling, with strong longevity and a citrus opening.
- **Hermès H24** — kept for its distinctive metallic note, which reminds him of firing ranges from his time in the Marine Corps.
- **An Ojar fragrance** (bought via the retailer Max Romano; exact name unclear in the transcript) — kept because of his wife's strong positive reaction to it.
- **A Tony Iommi collaboration fragrance** (house name transcribed unclearly — possibly Serge Lutens-adjacent; product name given as "Monkey Special") — bought with his own money after researching Iommi's Black Sabbath-era backstory; kept for its distinctive 1970s patchouli character.
- **Parfums de Marly Carlisle** — a longtime top-3-to-5 favorite; sweet tobacco, smoke, and fruit notes he describes as "the perfect sweater fragrance."
- **A niche house fragrance he calls "Love Flame"** (house name transcribed as "Distance Unseen") — a musk, linen, and milk-note scent with 8+ hours of longevity.

**High-Yield Assets — sold or being sold:**
- **Orto Parisi Bergamask** (house/product name uncertain in transcript) — called "on the border" but ultimately listed for sale.
- **Most of his Mugler fragrances**, including a peppermint-chocolate-cookie-like scent — liked but never personally worn.
- **Bvlgari Aqua Amara** — a fragrance he calls hard to find and well made, but never got attached to.
- **Three Creed fragrances** — Original Vetiver, Original Santal, and Viking — all "good fragrances," none he fell in love with.
- **Tom Ford Black Orchid** — kept for years as a deliberately "unisex-first" fragrance recommended for its unusual truffle/Mexican-chocolate note, but ultimately listed since he could easily rebuy it if he changed his mind.
- Selling primarily through **Rescentage**, a marketplace built specifically to combat counterfeit fragrance resale by vetting buyers and sellers.

**Case Studies/Anecdotes:**
- His son first wore the Viktor & Rolf Spicebomb bottle at 13; it later cycled back to Antonio's own office, and he still refused to part with it when offered.
- A trip to Istanbul with his son and friend Ryan Masters led to discovering Atelier Rebul's Bosphorus scent in the brand's own shop, in the rain.
- A separate trip to Colorado's Red Rocks with his wife and friend Ryan cemented a different fragrance (a pine-and-earth scent from a niche house he calls "M. Mekola," transcript unclear on exact name) as tied specifically to that place.
- Jeremy Fragrance gave him two bottles at two different career milestones — one around 2016–2017 when they were both building YouTube channels, another around 2019–2020 at a John Barbados (transcript unclear on shop name) event in London — both kept as markers of his early fragrance journey.
- Fragrance consultant "Stephen" (surname unclear in transcript) recommended Tom Ford Black Orchid specifically to round out a 10-fragrance starter collection because of its distinctive truffle note.

**The "So What?" Factor:** A large fragrance collection isn't really a collection of scents — it's a collection of memory triggers, and most of them turn out to be redundant once you notice which ones you'd actually fight to keep. The practical filter Centeno applies: if you could easily rebuy it, it's replaceable; if it's tied to an irreplaceable person, place, or reaction, it stays — everything else is just inventory.
