## The Hook & Core Thesis
Most people struggle with money not because they're bad at math, but because they **don't have a plan**. A concrete **90-day (12.5-week) reset**, broken into one action per week: understand your spending, cut the fat, automate saving, kill high-interest debt, build an emergency fund, start investing, grow income, set and track goals. Run your finances like a business — know your revenue, expenses, and profit.

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **Treat your finances like a business** — every successful business knows its revenue, expenses, and net profit intimately; you should too.
- **Automation ("pay yourself first") is the single most important move** — money moved out before you can spend it removes all friction.
- **You can only cut costs so far, but income is unlimited** — grow earnings, don't just trim.
- **Writing goals down (and sharing them) raises achievement odds ~42%** — plans beat intentions.

## High-Yield Assets (the 12-week plan)
- **Week 1 — Review where you stand.** Pull the last 3 months of statements (bank, credit cards, debt) and categorize every expense into **fixed** (rent, utilities, car, subscriptions, groceries), **discretionary** (wants — eating out, travel, shopping), and **debt payments**. Find your **average monthly spend per category** and your **savings rate** (income minus spend = your "net profit"). This surfaces overspending (e.g. forgotten subscriptions, too much Uber).
- **Week 2 — Cut the fat.** Sort expenses largest to smallest; ask of each whether you can cut it **10–30%**. Rent is hard (biggest but stickiest); easier wins in the example: ~$225/mo Uber (halve or eliminate via walking/transit), $750/mo car+insurance (call around to compare providers, ~$100/mo), shopping and eating out ($50–100 each), and cancel unused subscriptions ($100–200). Cutting **$400/mo = $4,800/year** — a Europe flight, a Roth IRA contribution, or debt paydown.
- **Week 3 — Automate ("pay yourself first").** Open a **high-yield savings account** (~3.8–4% at recording) for 1–5-year money — $10,000 at 4% is $400/year free. Set up **automatic transfers on payday** so a preset % leaves before you can spend it. Aim to automate **≥10% of take-home** (e.g. 5% to HYSA, 5% to investing). Psychologically it works like a 401(k) — you never see it, so you never touch it.
- **Week 4 — Plan for consumer debt.** For high-interest (credit card) debt, total it and use a **credit-card debt calculator**: an extra $75/mo on a $2,500 balance saves ~8 months of payments. Set up automatic payments. **Call your card issuer and ask to lower the interest rate** (cite competitor rates, loyalty) — 5 minutes, can save hundreds/thousands. Average card APR is **>21%**, so paying it down is an instant guaranteed return.
- **Week 5 — Emergency fund to $1,000.** A meaningful psychological milestone (crossing 4 digits); **59% of Americans can't cover a $1,000 emergency**. If you already have $1,000, aim for **3–6 months of living expenses**. Speed it up by selling unused stuff or a side hustle, and redirecting the Week 1–2 savings. Keep it in a HYSA (e.g. **Wealthfront, SoFi, Ally**).
- **Week 6 — Set up investing.** Stocks have the best 100-year returns of any asset class (real estate ~10th, per the Max Klimenko Wall Street quiz). Buy an **S&P 500 index fund/ETF** — a diversified portfolio of hundreds of top US companies in one purchase, passive "set and forget," ~**8–10%/year** historically. Open a brokerage (Vanguard, Fidelity, Schwab, M1 Finance), route part of your automated savings in, and buy the ETF regularly. **$1,000/month for 30 years at 8% → ~$1.49M.** Consistency and trusting the process matter most.
- **Week 7 — Level up income.** Cutting has a floor; earning doesn't. Options: **ask for a raise** (especially if none in 1–2 years — inflation has eroded your pay; research salaries and present your case), **job-hop** (~30–40% more per switch every ~2 years), start a **side hustle** (freelance, flip on Facebook Marketplace, dog-sitting, DoorDash), or learn a **high-income skill** (coding, video editing, sales, design). Identify one income move.
- **Week 8 — Write down a savings goal.** Pick a goal (emergency fund, down payment, wedding) and the amount needed, then the monthly savings to hit it (e.g. $8,000 fund from $1,000 = $583/mo for a year). **Writing goals down, planning, and sharing them raises achievement ~42%** (per a psychology-professor study) — so tell friends/family.
- **Week 9 — Credit cards (know yourself).** If you have **self-control**, run all spending through a card and **pay in full monthly** for cash back, points, lounge access — "free money" from issuers. If **not disciplined**, avoid cards (average American carries **$7,236** of card debt at 21%). Test with a **low limit for 3–6 months** to gauge your control. Used responsibly, cards **build credit** (payment history = 35% of your score), unlocking better rates on mortgages/loans.
- **Week 10 — Track net worth.** **Assets − liabilities = net worth** (e.g. $100k house + $25k accounts − $30k car = $95k). Track monthly/quarterly to see the long-term trajectory; what gets watched tends to grow.
- **Week 11 — Review spending (weeks 2–10).** Eight weeks after the first audit, re-categorize the last two months, recompute average monthly spend, and compare to your pre-reset baseline. Ideally expenses stayed flat or fell (especially targeted categories); patch any new leaks.
- **Week 12 — Set stretch goals and a review cadence.** Define **1-year, 5-year, and 10-year** goals (e.g. save $10k → buy property/hit a net-worth milestone → financial independence/early retirement), break each into monthly steps, and **schedule quarterly check-ins** (month-end + quarter-end reviews).

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **His own automation setup**: paid monthly on the 21st into checking, with two automatic transfers moving a preset slice to savings and investing *before* he can spend it — the concrete "pay yourself first" mechanism.
- **The hypothetical $4,650/month budget** used across Weeks 1–2 (rent, car/insurance, Uber, eating out, shopping, subscriptions, etc.) is the worked example showing where the ~$400/month of cuts come from.

## The "So What?" Factor
Run the 12 weeks in order: audit and cut spending (weeks 1–2), automate saving before you can spend it (week 3), attack high-interest debt and negotiate the rate down (week 4), build a $1,000 → 3–6 month emergency fund in a HYSA (week 5), start dollar-cost-averaging into an S&P 500 ETF (week 6), grow income deliberately (week 7), write down and share goals (week 8), master or avoid credit cards based on your self-control (week 9), track net worth (week 10), re-review spending (week 11), and set stretch goals with quarterly check-ins (week 12). Wealth isn't overnight — it's consistency, discipline, and a long-term vision, and this plan installs the system.
