## The Hook & Core Thesis
A step-by-step technique for shaving with a **double-edged (DE) safety razor** — first the classic multi-pass method, then a faster hybrid that yields the same baby-smooth result. The result rivals any cartridge razor (and single-blade systems like Supply/OneBlade), and lasts smooth for ~24 hours. Two non-negotiables underpin everything: a **pre-shave routine** and **plenty of high-fat lubrication**.

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **A sharp blade demands preparation and lubrication** — skip either and you get pulling, nicks, and irritation (pain or pulling means bad prep *or* a dull blade).
- **Know your beard's growth direction and pass with it first** — first pass *with* the grain (gentlest), then across, then against for the closest cut.
- **Keep skin taut** at all times — loose skin is where you cut yourself.
- **The fast hybrid method** swaps the manual first passes for a wet electric shaver, cutting a full DE shave down to ~5–6 minutes while keeping the DE-quality finish.

## High-Yield Assets (the classic technique, in order)
1. **Pre-shave** — soften the hair: a hot shower, or a microwave-heated wet towel on the face, or splashing hot water for a minute or two.
2. **Apply lubrication** — shaving cream from a **tube** (never aerosol cans — too little fat), or a **shaving soap** lathered with a **brush** (best, since it coats around each hair and lifts it slightly).
3. **Load the blade straight** in the head (crooked = cuts).
4. **Map your grain** — study your beard's growth in the mirror (sketch arrows if needed); everyone's is different, so memorize yours.
5. **First pass, with the grain** — least stressful on skin.
6. **Keep skin tight** — stretch with your free hand (pull the sideburn up, etc.), puff cheeks for air, use your tongue against the inside for the areas below nose/mouth.
7. **Angle and pressure** — hold the handle at **30–45°** (you'll hear the correct angle); use almost no pressure, let gravity slide it down; short, slow strokes at a 90° straight stroke when learning. Advanced: a slight diagonal "stride" (or an angled/slant head) enhances the cutting motion — but slant heads are more aggressive, suited only to **thick** hair; thin-bearded men get the same result from a gentler razor.
8. **Rinse the blade** between strokes; **reapply fresh cream** each pass (you can't over-lubricate; dried cream clogs and loses protection — re-wet and re-apply if your face feels dry).
9. **Second pass, across the grain** (90° to the first); **third pass, against the grain** for the closest cut (feels more aggressive, cuts below skin level, stays smooth ~24 hours). Most men stop after the second.
10. **Finish** — splash **cold water** (some use ice water to close pores/stop bleeding; regular cold tap is fine), then a **post-shave balm**. Avoid traditional **alcohol** aftershave (dries skin). He recommends an inexpensive **Nivea sensitive-skin after-shave balm**.
- **Problem areas**: under the nose and mouth (tongue + puffed air), the jawline (pull skin up to shave smooth), under the ear (needs a different direction), and the chin (fingers + face muscles, shave multiple directions).

## The Fast Hybrid Routine (~5–6 minutes)
1. A normal **warm shower** (no separate towel/splash prep).
2. **Shaving cream from the tube, applied by hand** (skip lathering and brush cleanup).
3. A **wet-capable electric shaver** to remove excess hair — equivalent to the first (and maybe second) pass, but faster and with no cut risk (a *wet* electric shave beats dry — closer). Ensure the shaver is rated for water.
4. **Reapply cream by hand and go against the grain once** — a single manual pass, which also makes DE blades last longer.
5. **Cold water + post-shave balm.**
- **Trade-off**: needs a waterproof electric shaver (~$200–300 extra upfront), but saves meaningful time over the years.

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **The "baby-butt" test**: his wife is "amazed every time she feels my smooth… skin" after a three-pass DE shave — his proof the against-the-grain finish lasts a full day.
- **Straight-razor as a weekend ritual**: he rates a straight razor's result slightly above a DE shave, but it takes too long and too much technique for everyday use — "something you'd use on the weekends when you want to enjoy the process."

## The "So What?" Factor
To get a DE shave right: prep (hot shower/towel), lather generously with a tube cream or brush-and-soap, map your grain, and pass with → across → against the grain, keeping skin taut at ~30–45° with no pressure, then cold water and a non-alcohol balm. When you don't have 10 minutes, use the hybrid — shower, hand-applied cream, a wet electric shaver for the bulk, then one against-the-grain manual pass — for the same smoothness in 5–6 minutes.
