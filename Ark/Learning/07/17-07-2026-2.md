## The Hook & Core Thesis
A rundown of every [[loafer]] and slip-on style a man can wear with denim in summer — what each one is, where it came from, and how dressy it actually is. The through-line: the **[[penny loafer]]** is the one to start with (simple, versatile, [[Lindy effect]]-proof), while the shoe of the title — the **double monk strap**, which "most guys have never even heard of" — is the standout most men are missing.

*(Note: the title promises a single shoe, but the video is delivered as a survey of loafer/slip-on styles, with the monk strap arriving late as the titular "99% don't wear it" pick.)*

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **Definition first: a loafer is a dress shoe with no lacing.** If it has laces, it isn't a loafer. Most quality loafers are leather; **suede** is a leather that shows more texture. Many men avoid suede fearing damage — it *is* more delicate, but treated and cared for it's a great wardrobe addition.
- **Start with the penny loafer — it's the safest first buy.** Designed in the 1930s, it dominated menswear in the 1950s–60s as a piece that dressed up or down, favoured by college students who kept wearing it into their 40s, 50s, and 60s. Its simplicity is the appeal: nothing flashy, solid colour. In **ox blood** or **dark brown** you can wear it with a casual suit and with jeans on the same day.
- **The [[Lindy effect]] justifies splurging on quality.** The longer something has been around, the longer it will stay around — penny loafers have lasted a hundred years, so you can be confident they'll last another hundred. That makes a quality pair worth the money.
- **Match new loafers to the shoes you already own.** If your dress shoes are all black, buy black penny loafers; if you wear browns, bring in a similar brown. You're changing the *style*, not the palette — so it mixes with your existing wardrobe and works with more than just jeans.
- **Black is the most formal dress-shoe colour.** For loafers that pair with the widest range of denim, go **lighter** rather than black (though he has seen men pull off black loafers with denim).
- **Denim mistakes to avoid with loafers.** Loafers are technically dress shoes, and pairing them with **distressed denim** (ultra-casual) is "like mixing oil and water" — unless you're a rock star, skip it. Best pairings: **slim cut and straight cut** jeans. Relaxed cut can be managed (especially for bigger guys) as long as the leg opening isn't too wide. Skin-tight jeans and the currently fashionable **loose-fit baggy jeans** are both a no-go.
- **For summer specifically, expose more of the foot.** Look for **unlined** suede — lighter and more breathable — and a **lower vamp**, meaning less of the foot is covered and a cooler wearing experience than sneakers.
- **The style ladder, casual → dressy:** moccasins and driving shoes (most casual, made for denim) → Belgian loafers (casual, versatile, flashier) → horsebit loafers → penny loafers → tassel loafers (dressiest, hardest to wear with denim).

## High-Yield Assets
- **Penny loafer**: designed 1930s, classic through the 1950s–60s; the recommended first loafer; best in ox blood or dark brown. Pairs with chinos + navy blazer + collared **Oxford shirt**, optionally a striped **regimental tie**.
- **Horsebit loafer**: emerged around **1953**, popularised by **Gucci**; initially worn only in Italy, spread out of Europe and became a major trend in the **1980s**. Now made widely. The metal bit is decorative with no functional purpose, so it takes some confidence to wear (he owns a Gucci pair).
- **Moccasin**: originates with North America's Native population (other cultures made similar shoes); classic versions had no true sole, just a leather covering that wore out and was re-soled while the top lasted. Identified by stitching running around the top outside. The **most casual slip-on** available — essentially made for denim, arguably shorts; not for suits.
- **Driving shoe**: a moccasin variant with rubber bits instead of a full leather bottom; genuinely made for driving (pedal feel plus grip), not for walking around in — he admits to wearing his out and finding they don't hold up.
- **Tassel loafer**: emerged right after **World War II** in Europe, then reached the US; among the dressiest loafers, rarely made in light colours. Hardest to wear with denim — possible with dark denim + a medium brown pair, better with denim and a sports jacket, but not a first loafer.
- **Belgian loafer**: much more casual than the tassel; often suede and available in standout colours (green suede, blue suede); versatile across casual suits and jeans, and very comfortable in a quality pair.
- **Double monk strap** (the titular shoe): in vogue for over a decade and considered overplayed by some, yet most men have never heard of it. Double buckles give a bit of flash, slips on and off easily (handy at the airport), and works with a suit, a sports-jacket-and-trouser combination, or dressed down with chinos and a linen shirt.
- **[[Brogues]]**: four common styles — **quarter brogues, semi-brogues, full brogues (aka wing tips), and long wing brogues**. **Broguing** = cut holes/perforations in the leather, historically for hunting in marshes so water draining through the shoe could escape. On most modern dress shoes the broguing is decorative and doesn't pierce the leather. He loves them because from a distance they read as classic dress shoes — the detail only appears up close.
- **[[Chukka]] / desert boot**: not a shoe but a boot; came out of **World War II Cairo**, designed for British officers in North Africa who needed traction, comfort, and tolerance of hot conditions.
- **The summer outfit formula**: no-show socks + cream chinos + a **chambray shirt** (a very lightweight denim, usually a shade of blue) with sleeves rolled — "you're going to look like you belong on the French Riviera."
- **Dressing loafers up**: a cotton or **seersucker** suit with an open collar, or a lightweight summer silk-cotton blend tie.

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **His own Gucci horsebit pair**: he wears them with denim and enjoys the fun, casual bit across the vamp — but is candid that the shiny hardware serves no purpose and isn't for everyone, which is exactly why he steers beginners to penny loafers.
- **His tassel loafers**: he owns a pair and wears them with casual suits, but concedes that making them work with denim is "a push… a little bit of a stretch."
- **Driving shoes worn out**: he's worn driving shoes out and about himself and realised mid-wear that they simply aren't built for it.
- **Penny loafers across a lifetime**: college students who adopted them kept wearing them into their 40s, 50s, and 60s — "I'm sure there are guys in their 90s still wearing penny loafers," his illustration of the Lindy effect in menswear.

## The "So What?" Factor
If you own no loafers, buy one pair of quality **penny loafers** in ox blood or dark brown matched to the shoes already in your closet — that single purchase covers casual suits and jeans in the same day. For summer, choose unlined suede with a low vamp, wear it with no-show socks, cream chinos, and a rolled-sleeve chambray shirt. Keep denim slim or straight, never distressed or baggy. And if you want the shoe nobody else is wearing, the double monk strap is the pick.
