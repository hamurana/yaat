## The Hook & Core Thesis
[[Humphrey Yang]] and guest [[Graham Stephan]] review three real subscriber portfolios (ages 20, 28, 24) and, despite the "roast" framing, land on the same verdict each time: the allocations are mostly fine — the wins come from income growth, adequate cash reserves per rental property, and stripping out psychological holdings (dividends, gold) that quietly cost long-term return.

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **Dividend funds like SCHD are a tax-bracket decision, not a forever holding.** They make sense while you're in the 0% capital-gains bracket; once dividends start getting taxed, the drag outweighs the payout. The appeal is mostly psychological — watching "estimated next-12-months income" tick up — and mathematically it stops making sense.
- **Keep ~$20K cash per rental property, not a percentage.** Graham's rule: a roof, HVAC unit, or eviction costs the same on a $400K house as a $1M one, so the reserve is a flat unit cost. This flipped Humphrey's initial "too much cash" read of submission #2 on the spot.
- **Gold and silver don't belong in a young person's Roth IRA.** Submission #3 holds ~$17K of precious metals out of fear of economic uncertainty and inflation — Graham's worry is that this exact fear makes people underperform for a decade while markets rip higher. Fix: sell at least half, [[Dollar-Cost Averaging|DCA]] the proceeds into the S&P 500.
- **Judge holdings by cost basis, not the snapshot.** Both reviewers refused to score hype-adjacent positions (SanDisk, a quantum stock, gold near highs) without knowing *when* they were bought — bought early it's conviction, bought last week it's chasing.
- **Renting beats a rushed house purchase in your early 20s.** The 20-year-old's 5–6-year down-payment goal got the same pushback as in their other conversations: don't lock into a 30-year mortgage at 25 unless you know you're staying put.
- **Simplify 401(k)s to one fund.** A scatter of large/mid/small-cap and balanced funds should be consolidated into a single S&P 500 or large-cap fund.
- **Paying off a rental mortgage isn't automatic** — the interest is deductible against rental income (taxed at ordinary rates), so depending on the rate it can be better left alone.

## High-Yield Assets
- **Tickers discussed**: QQQM (the cheaper, lower-expense-ratio QQQ variant — trades the same but with less liquidity and no options; fine for buy-and-hold), SCHD (Schwab dividend ETF with a big Reddit following), SCHG (Schwab US Large-Cap Growth, 0.04% expense ratio), SMH (VanEck semiconductor ETF), VT, VXUS, VOO, AVUV [ticker as-transcribed "ABUV"], IAU (iShares Gold Trust), SLV, plus individual names Nvidia, Google, Meta, Amazon, SanDisk, Robinhood stock.
- **[[Coast FIRE]] back-of-napkin formula**: annual expenses × 12 months × 25 (the [[4% Rule]]) = target portfolio; then check whether current assets doubling every 7–8 years get there by retirement age. Submission #2 ($4,300/month expenses → ~$1.29M target, ~$400K in assets at 28) is effectively already there.
- **CD reality check**: banks push CDs hard on walk-ins (Graham was pitched one three days earlier while parking his late father's estate proceeds — he demanded a liquid money-market account at 3.5% instead). CDs paid ~5% around 2006; today's are mostly 3–12-month terms.
- **The rating gimmick**: scores delivered simultaneously "on three" with one decimal, borrowed from Ludwig and Michael Reeves' tip-to-tip meal reviews — submission #2 earned a 9.2 (Humphrey) and 8.0 (Graham).
- **Crypto sizing sanity check**: submission #2's Bitcoin looks like ~10% of liquid investments but only ~4–5% once real estate counts — inside the acceptable band.

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **Submission #1** — 20-year-old Alabama server assistant in college: $21K/year income, an excellent $600/month in expenses, goals of graduating debt-free, maxing the Roth IRA, and a house down payment in 5–6 years. Holds QQQ/SCHD/SMH plus a maturing CD [Transcript unclear on the exact cash and CD dollar figures]. Verdict: allocation is already good — the only real levers are raising income after graduation and eventually rotating out of SCHD; the reviewers guess the CD was a grandmother's gift.
- **Submission #2** — 28-year-old Georgia computer scientist/project manager: $99K salary + $18K/year from a $410K rental (~$150K equity), $6,500/month net, $4,300/month expenses, $33K cash, $194K invested, possibly a government employee (has a Thrift Savings Plan). The panel's star pupil — "28 with $400K+ in assets is killing it" — already at coast FIRE by their math.
- **Submission #3** — 24-year-old married grocery manager in Texas: $130K combined income, $8K/month in, $4,200/month out, $12K cash, $49K invested, $310K house (~$276K owed), goals of paying off a roof loan and saving for 2–4 kids. Too real-estate-heavy and too conservative (gold/silver in the Roth); told to build cash and equities to rebalance, and warned that his $1M-by-65 worry is justified — it won't go far.
- **The Chipotle avocado hack**: Humphrey once skipped ~$3 guac by buying a $1.80 avocado at the grocery store in the same complex and slicing it onto his order — spawning a joke business plan of selling avocados and knives outside Chipotle.

## The "So What?" Factor
Audit your own portfolio the way they audit these three: is every holding there for math or for feelings? Rotate dividend funds out once you leave the 0% capital-gains bracket, cap gold/fear hedges before they cap your returns, hold a flat ~$20K reserve per rental property, and consolidate scattered 401(k) funds into one broad index. Then stop fiddling — all three verdicts reduced to "raise income, keep expenses low, keep buying the same things."
