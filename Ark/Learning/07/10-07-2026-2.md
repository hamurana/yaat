## The Hook & Core Thesis
After ten years in emergency medicine and 50–60,000 patient encounters, [[Dr Alex]] concludes that most of what ends a person's life or independence comes down to a small set of things that were *knowable and changeable* long before the patient reached the hospital — ten lessons, each backed by research, that stack the deck toward a long healthy life. (The title frames these as "my 10 biggest health mistakes"; the content is actually ten preventive lessons he'll carry for the rest of his career — mistakes he watches *patients* make, not his own.)

## Key Arguments Checklist
- **Learn the symptoms of the fastest, commonest killers — minutes change outcomes.** Heart attack: chest pain or pressure, but it can travel to the stomach, arm, neck, or jaw, and in some people shows up as breathlessness or a cold sweat rather than movie-style pain. Stroke: sudden one-sided numbness or weakness, facial droop, slurred or muddled speech, sudden dizziness, or vision change. Sudden severe abdominal pain out of nowhere can be a perforated or ischemic bowel. The common thread: the person who dials 999/911 *early* does far better than the one who waits — and waiting is the mistake he sees most. Especially over 50, don't take chances.
- **Only air should ever enter your lungs.** Smoking and vaping, but also the fine dust from cutting stone with a Stihl saw (his father-in-law, pointedly), sawdust, and fumes — all need a tight-sealing mask. Lung tissue doesn't grow back like other tissue; he sees people in their 40s and 50s who will never run properly again. A properly sealing mask costs almost nothing against a lifetime of being unable to climb stairs.
- **Ultra-processed food is an opportunity cost, not just a poison.** Every [[Ultra-Processed Foods|UPF]] item displaces a whole food rich in fiber, potassium, and polyphenols that would be quietly lowering your blood pressure and blood sugar. The randomized [[DASH Trial]] showed a whole-food eating pattern lowers blood pressure substantially on its own — sustained over decades, the effect can rival a low-dose blood-pressure tablet in some people. What sits on your plate every day is doing real pharmacology. (Explicit caveat: never stop existing medication without your doctor.)
- **Muscle has two jobs that matter enormously with age.** (1) Fall protection — reflexes and strength to catch a stumble, plus a cushioning layer over the hip; a broken hip or cracked skull in later life can end independence and take years or decades off a life. (2) Metabolic — working muscle pulls sugar out of the blood and blunts post-meal spikes; long-run, adequate muscle can do more for blood sugar than many diabetes tablets. [[Resistance Training]] twice a week plus enough protein is "some of the highest-value time you will ever spend on yourself."
- **A regularly challenged mind stays young; a switched-off mind ages faster.** Patients who still work, stay active, and hold complex conversations give clear histories and are easier and safer to treat; those who've stopped using their minds answer slowly and confuse easily. Retiring at 60, 65, or 70 without replacing the mental demand is one of the worst things you can do to your brain — but the challenge doesn't have to look like work: new places, cooking, planning trips, a book with the morning coffee. Passive TV drift is the failure mode.
- **Act on hearing loss early.** Untreated hearing loss is one of the largest modifiable dementia risk factors identified by the [[Lancet Commission]], and the [[ACHIEVE Trial]] found treating it in older adults at higher dementia risk slowed cognitive decline over three years. Senses left to fade pull you out of the world and speed decline. Rule: over 50, get eyes *and* hearing tested once a year (Specsavers in the UK) — treat it as a non-negotiable annual service; it's cheap and quick.
- **New dizziness always deserves investigating.** Best case, blood pressure running slightly low (itself protective) — but stiffening arteries under-perfuse the brain on standing, causing falls and head injuries. Worst case, sudden dizziness is a stroke. In between sit very fixable things: [[BPPV]] (benign paroxysmal positional vertigo, often correctable by a doctor in minutes) and dehydration. In the elderly, dizziness can be the *only* clue to a UTI or chest infection, because the usual infection signs are silent.
- **Speed limits and seat belts save lives — and so does attention.** Ten years of road-traffic deaths, over and over from unworn seat belts. And the other half: you can drive perfectly and still be killed by someone overtaking blind — "you can be right, and still be dead." Stay alert; assume nothing.
- **Check your blood pressure at home weekly from age 30.** Pick a day, take two readings morning and two evening, write them down, every week for life. Below roughly 180, high blood pressure usually causes *no symptoms* — which is exactly why it's dangerous: it massively raises heart attack, stroke, kidney damage, and dementia risk. Caught early there's usually an underlying cause and simple fixes; the [[SPRINT Trial]] showed proper control genuinely reduces major cardiovascular events and death.
- **Seek a second — even third — opinion when an explanation doesn't sit right.** The profession is poor at prevention; if a diagnosis feels wrong or reassurance still feels off, keep asking until you understand what is happening to your body. Nobody will advocate for your health as hard as you.

## High-Yield Assets
- **Named trials/reports**: the [[DASH Trial]] (randomized; whole-food pattern lowers blood pressure), the [[SPRINT Trial]] (intensive BP control cuts major cardiovascular events and death), the [[ACHIEVE Trial]] (hearing treatment slowed cognitive decline over 3 years in at-risk older adults), the [[Lancet Commission]] on dementia (untreated hearing loss among the largest modifiable risk factors). He promises links to the studies in the video description.
- **Home BP protocol**: weekly from age 30 — 2 readings AM + 2 readings PM on one fixed day, logged for life; <~180 mmHg is typically symptomless.
- **Strength prescription**: resistance training 2×/week + adequate protein.
- **Annual senses check**: eyes + hearing yearly after 50 (Specsavers offers both in the UK).
- **Symptom checklists**: heart attack (chest pressure radiating to stomach/arm/neck/jaw; or breathlessness/cold sweat), stroke (one-sided weakness, face droop, slurred speech, sudden dizziness, vision change), surgical abdomen (sudden severe pain → perforated/ischemic bowel).
- **BPPV**: benign paroxysmal positional vertigo — an inner-ear cause of dizziness a doctor can often fix in minutes.
- **Respiratory protection rule**: any non-air inhalant (smoke, vape aerosol, stone dust, sawdust, fumes) = tight-fitting sealed mask or don't do it; lung tissue does not regenerate.

## Case Studies / Anecdotes
- **The father-in-law and the Stihl saw**: his own family example of stone-cutting dust as an overlooked lung destroyer needing a sealed mask.
- **The clear-history patients**: mentally engaged older patients give him accurate histories, making them literally easier and safer to treat in the ED — his firsthand evidence that cognitive engagement compounds.
- **Ten years of trolleys**: the framing device — the same preventable diseases arriving on the same trolleys year after year, prompting the list.
- **Seat-belt deaths**: he has "lost count" of road deaths where the difference was an unworn seat belt.

## The "So What?" Factor
This is a prevention checklist from someone who sees the endgame daily: memorise the heart-attack/stroke symptom patterns and call early rather than wait; put nothing but air in your lungs (mask up for dust); crowd out ultra-processed food with whole food; lift twice a week; keep the mind loaded after retirement; test hearing and eyes yearly after 50; investigate any new dizziness; belt up and stay alert; log weekly home blood pressure from 30; and push for second opinions until an explanation makes sense. Even a few of these, taken seriously, measurably shift the odds.
