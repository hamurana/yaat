# You're Not Boring. You're Just Playing It Safe.

Notice something about the people everyone loves being around? They're rarely the ones with the best jokes. They're the ones who don't seem to care if a joke lands.

That's the whole trick. Being fun isn't about better material. It's about caring less whether it works.

### The joke isn't the skill. Not flinching is.

Most people edit themselves before a word comes out. They run the joke through a little committee in their head, decide it's risky, and swallow it.

Funny people just... don't do that as much. They say the thing. Sometimes it flops. They say the next thing anyway.

- Self-censorship is the actual enemy of wit, not a lack of material.
- Every "naturally funny" person you know has bombed plenty of times. They just kept going.
- The willingness to fall flat is what lets you find out what works.

### Vague isn't funny. Specific is.

Here's a small trick that does a lot of work: get specific.

- "I don't like the news" — flat, nobody laughs.
- "The news is like pouring hot lemon juice in your eyeballs" — now there's a picture.
- "He looked pale" — nothing.
- "He looked like a baby fish you can see through" — that lands.

Your brain doesn't laugh at ideas. It laughs at pictures. So paint one.

A second move that pairs well with this: set someone up like you're about to say something sincere, then flip it. Ask an obvious question and give the opposite answer, or the most ridiculous one that could still technically count. A one-second pause before the punchline helps — it gives people's brain time to guess wrong.

### Confidence is doing more work than you think

None of this lands if you're secretly begging for approval underneath it. That energy leaks out.

- Go into a conversation to enjoy it, not to be liked. Trying hard to be liked reads as needy, and people can feel it.
- Tell the embarrassing story. The one you'd normally edit out. Owning it makes you look more solid, not less — you're showing you don't need to seem perfect.
- If something awkward happens right in the moment, just say it out loud and laugh. Don't pretend it didn't happen.

There's a good example of this in one of the notes: a well-known actor stayed completely calm when the wrong film got announced as Best Picture live on stage. His reaction, more or less, was: they read the wrong name, nobody got hurt. That's the whole mindset in one line — *want it, don't need it.* Most of what makes a moment feel high-stakes is just your own anxiety, not the actual moment.

### Be warm, not just sharp

Being funny can tip into being a jerk if you're only ever pointed at other people. A few small habits fix that:

- Tease yourself too, not just everyone else — it signals you're playing, not attacking.
- Keep the teasing aimed at things that aren't real insecurities.
- Notice people who usually get skipped over, not just the ones you're trying to impress.
- When someone says something honest or a little vulnerable, actually acknowledge it instead of deflecting.

### The short version

You don't need sharper jokes. You need a lower filter and less need for the room to approve of you. Say the specific, vivid version of the thought instead of the vague one. Let a joke bomb sometimes. Tell the embarrassing story. Notice the people other people don't.

Nobody remembers the joke that didn't land. They remember that you weren't scared to try.
