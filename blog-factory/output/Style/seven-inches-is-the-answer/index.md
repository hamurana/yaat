Most men own one pair of shorts and have never thought about the inseam. It's the number that decides whether they read as tailored or as gym kit, and there's a clear sweet spot. Seven inches. Modern, flattering, and still smart enough for dinner outside.
{{< /lead >}}

### The full range

- **5 inches** — short, casual, beach. Works, but it's a specific look.
- **7 inches** — the answer for most men. Sits mid-thigh, reads current without being a statement.
- **9 inches** — approaching the knee. Fine if the cut isn't boxy.
- **11 inches** — at the knee. Risks looking dated or sloppy, though taller men can carry it.

The general rule underneath: mid-thigh to just above the knee. And whatever the length, the shape has to be clean. Nothing baggy and nothing obviously built for a gym.

### Heat is the hardest test

There are no coats or layers to hide behind, so every remaining piece carries more weight. A jacket forgives a bad shirt. In July nothing forgives anything.

Which is why the two decisions that come *before* the outfit matter most.

**Fabric.** Linen, cotton, seersucker, lightweight denim, open-weave knits, tropical wool for anything tailored. Winter cloth in summer is the single most common error.

**Colour.** White, cream, beige, stone, light blue, olive, soft browns. All of them absorb less and all of them are easy to build around.

### Stop buying slim

Skinny clothing leaves no room for air, so you overheat and the fabric sticks to you, which looks exactly as bad as it sounds.

Relaxed or natural cuts, not oversized. A relaxed linen shirt with straight-leg trousers is the shape. The skinny era has properly ended, so this is no longer hard to find.

On linen's wrinkles: they're the point, not a defect. Summer is a more relaxed register and a slightly crumpled linen shirt reads as someone at ease. Ironing it into submission every morning misunderstands the fabric.

### Wear a shirt, and wear trousers

Two upgrades, both bigger than they sound.

**Replace the tee.** A linen button-up, a camp collar shirt, a lightweight Oxford, or a knit polo. Often the entire distance between average and put-together is that one swap.

**Wear trousers more than you think you can.** Most men abandon them entirely once it's warm, which is why the men who don't stand out. Linen, lightweight cotton, relaxed chinos, drawstring. It's how men in genuinely hot countries dress — and the trick is the weight of the cloth, not some superior tolerance for heat.

The same linen shirt goes two ways. With shorts it's a casual afternoon. With cream trousers and loafers it's dinner.

### Get shoes that aren't your only sneakers

The most-missed opportunity in summer. Match the shoe to the mood of the outfit rather than defaulting.

- **Espadrilles** for anything relaxed
- **Suede loafers** when you want polish
- **Boat shoes, driving shoes, or good leather sandals** for casual

### Three outfits that cover a season

- **Casual day** — light blue linen shirt, stone tailored shorts around seven inches, espadrilles or leather sandals. Sunglasses and a watch to finish.
- **Evening** — a textured brown polo in knit, cotton, or a linen-cotton blend, not a shiny golf shirt, with cream drawstring trousers and brown suede loafers.
- **Something that matters** — taupe linen shirt, white linen-blend trousers with side adjusters, sand espadrilles, and an unstructured linen blazer if the room calls for it.

You don't need a hundred pieces. Three reliable combinations cover the whole season.

A few small things to try:

- Measure the inseam on the shorts you actually wear.
- Buy one pair at seven inches and compare them honestly.
- Swap the t-shirt for a camp collar shirt once this week.
- Wear trousers on a hot day and notice you're fine.
