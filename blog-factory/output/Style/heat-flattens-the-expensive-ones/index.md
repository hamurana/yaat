{{< lead >}}
The richest bottle you own is probably your worst summer fragrance. Elixirs and parfum flankers are built with weight, and weight is exactly what heat destroys. In July they turn syrupy and close. The cheaper citrus sitting next to them holds up all day.
{{< /lead >}}

### What heat rewards

Bright citrus, aquatic, and coconut. Not because they're light — because they keep projecting once you're warm and slightly sweaty, which is the actual test.

Dark and rich scents don't fail by disappearing. They fail by getting cloying. Save them for cool nights and winter, where they're superb.

### The reliable summer picks

- **Prada Luna Rossa Ocean.** Close to a no-brainer — good longevity, good projection, and it genuinely smells better as you heat up. **Luna Rossa Carbon** is the more versatile original and holds up the same way.
- **Versace Man Eau Fraîche.** The cheap one that keeps earning its place. A cold drink on a hot day, and hard to wear wrong.
- **Acqua di Gio Parfum Intense.** A darker, more citrus-forward turn on the 1990s original and less oceanic than you'd expect — green apple and bergamot up top, clary sage and marine underneath, wood and amber at the base. Stronger and longer-lasting than the older versions.
- **Jean Paul Gaultier Le Beau Paradise Garden.** Mint and ginger are what separate it; coconut, fig, tonka, and sandalwood do the tropical part. Lasts absurdly well — you could get thrown in a pool and still smell like it. Skip the original Le Mâle, which has dated, and the Elixir, which is a winter scent.
- **Chanel Allure Homme Sport Eau Extrême.** Fifteen years old and still one of the easiest reaches in hot weather.
- **Dolce & Gabbana Light Blue.** Everyone has smelled it, which is the only mark against it. **Light Blue Eau Intense** is roughly twice the strength if you want the same idea with more behind it.

### The ones people get wrong in summer

- **Dior Sauvage.** The EDT is the world's best-seller, but the EDP is the better summer choice. **Sauvage Elixir** is a genuinely excellent dark, liquorice-heavy scent and completely wrong for August.
- **Prada Luna Rossa Le Parfum** and the newer **Carbon EDP** — both heavier, both better in autumn.
- **YSL La Nuit de L'Homme EDP.** Lovely, and it's a skin scent. It works when someone is close to you and vanishes at a pool party. That's not a flaw, it's a mismatch.
- **Dior Homme Sport** is quietly underrated and often discounted, though it leans dress-up. **Dior Homme Cologne** is the more citrus-forward option.

### Match the scent to the room, not the season alone

Season is the first filter, not the only one. An intimate scent and a club scent both fail when swapped.

- Office in summer → something simple and safe. **Versace Pour Homme** does this without thinking.
- Going out → **Versace Eros Energy**, a bright lemon-lime-grapefruit opening over the usual Eros base. Over-spray it and it survives.
- Getting close to someone → the skin scents, which is where La Nuit de L'Homme earns back everything it lost at the pool.

### Sample the strange ones

Most of the list above is safe to buy without smelling first. One category isn't: anything deliberately polarising.

The purple Uomo Born in Roma flanker from Valentino is the current example — lavender pushing it masculine, coconut making it fun, rich and creamy and odd. Some people love it immediately. Your skin chemistry gets a vote, and it votes differently from mine.

A few small things to try:

- Move the elixirs to the back of the shelf until October.
- Buy one citrus you can reach for without deciding anything.
- Wear a scent on a genuinely hot day before judging it. Air conditioning tells you nothing.
- Sample anything described as unusual. Buy blind only on the boring ones.
