Nobody fails at grooming through lack of standards. They fail by only ever noticing the problem once it's already visible, in a mirror, on the way out. Everything below is solved by one recurring slot in the week and a drawer with the right tools.
{{< /lead >}}

### What quietly goes wrong

- **A beard or moustache left alone.** Stray uneven hairs turn a tidy man into someone who looks like he hasn't showered. Committing to facial hair doubles the maintenance rather than removing it.
- **Stubble worn as a choice but not managed.** Stubble genuinely works — there's a study from 2012 that gets quoted a lot about women preferring some over none, and I'd take the general direction of it rather than the precise finding. But it grows at different lengths on different parts of the face, so it needs clippers run over it to even out. Deliberate stubble and neglected stubble look nothing alike.
- **Eyebrows going wild.** This one is underrated. Eyebrows are among the strongest signals in the whole face — that's essentially their evolutionary job — and a brow that's grown heavy or joined in the middle reads as a scowl you didn't intend. You're not plucking them into a shape. You're stopping them from saying something.
- **Nose hair.** Only a problem when it gets away from you, and worse the taller you are, because people are looking up.
- **Ear hair.** Arrives around the same time, from the same conspiracy.
- **A hairy neck.** Turns up a few days after a haircut. Fine at a week. Past that, the neck growth is longer than the cut hair above it, and that contrast is what people notice.
- **Hair over the ears.** Small, and it reads clearly at close range as overdue.

### The pattern is age, not carelessness

Somewhere after thirty, hair starts leaving the top of your head and reappearing in your ears and nose, and the stray eyebrow hairs get thicker and grow faster.

Nobody warns you. The routine that worked at twenty-five simply stops covering the territory, and most men interpret that as having got sloppy when the actual change is that there's more to do.

So the routine has to grow too. That's it. That's the whole insight.

### Two fixes, and only two

**Book the time.** One dedicated slot a week, in the bathroom, doing all of it at once whether or not anything looks urgent. Beards and fast stubble want something closer to daily. Doing it on a schedule means you never once do it in a hurry.

**Own the tools.** Clippers and a shaver, at home. Your barber will happily trim eyebrows and ear and nose hair while you're in the chair — most will do it for nothing if you ask — but a haircut is every few weeks and the hair doesn't wait.

### Why bother

Because none of these register as individual faults. They register as an overall impression, formed by a stranger in a second, and it's the wrong one.

The good news is that this is the cheapest problem in this entire subject to solve. No skill required, no taste required, nothing to learn. Just a repeating appointment with yourself.

A few small things to try:

- Pick a day. Sunday evening works for most people.
- Buy clippers if you don't have them. That's the entire equipment list.
- Ask your barber to do the eyebrows and ears next time.
- Check your neck a week after a cut, not three weeks after.
