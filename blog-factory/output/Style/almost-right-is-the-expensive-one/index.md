The most expensive thing in most wardrobes is the discounted jacket with shoulders slightly too wide. It cost less and it gets worn twice a year. Price per wear is the only honest measure, and "almost right" always loses it.
{{< /lead >}}

### The check before the wallet opens

Fit doesn't get more forgiving with age — it gets less, because bodies and posture both change. So the discount is irrelevant until the garment passes.

Look at, in order: **shoulders, sleeve length, waist, rise, and how the fabric falls when you stand normally.** Not how it looks when you square up in the mirror. How it hangs when you forget you're being looked at.

You don't need more clothes that almost work. You need fewer that work without negotiation.

### Trendy clothes are rented

That's the honest description. A statement piece buys one good weekend and then starts working against you — by the third wear it isn't fashionable, it's *memorable*, which is a different and worse thing.

Men don't look dated because they're older. They look dated because they keep buying clothes designed to expire, and then replacing them with the next batch of the same.

The alternative is quiet anchors that give an outfit structure without asking for attention: a navy blazer, a charcoal overshirt, a dark brown suede jacket, an olive field jacket, a decent wool coat.

### Five tests worth memorising

Run any purchase through these and most of them fail, which is the point.

1. **Will this make me easier to dress, or harder?** A loud jacket needs the rest of the outfit to accommodate it. A navy one doesn't.
2. **Is this quiet depth, or is it asking for attention?**
3. **Does this make the outfit stronger, or just the feet louder?**
4. **Does this fight my shape or clean it up?**
5. **Can I wear this ten different ways, or am I buying it for one compliment?**

That last one is the killer. Most bad purchases are a single anticipated compliment, bought in advance.

### Texture instead of pattern

The good move for anyone past a certain age is to get interest from the cloth rather than from print. Big patterns and shiny fabrics pull the eye to the garment; texture makes a plain colour look richer and lets you keep the attention.

Oxford cloth, brushed cotton, chambray, linen blends, fine-knit polos, soft flannel, a quiet check. The shirt sits closest to your face, so it has more power than anything else you own.

### The rest of the shape

- **Shoes need a spine.** Thick-soled, bulky, brightly branded trainers make feet look heavy and legs look shorter. Clean leather sneakers in white, cream, navy, or taupe. Suede loafers, chukka boots, plain-toe derbies. The test: comfortable enough to wear, clean enough to respect, simple enough to repeat.
- **Trousers set the whole line.** Skinny draws attention to knees and stomach and makes a man look trapped. Oversized shortens and blurs. The middle is reliable: straight-leg chinos, tailored five-pockets, dark denim with a natural opening, wool trousers with some drape. Falling clean from the hip, skimming, touching the shoe without piling.
- **Colour changes how rested you look.** Bright orange, neon, icy pastels, and hard black right under the chin all drain a mature face. Navy, charcoal, olive, camel, cream, stone, chocolate brown, soft denim blue, deep burgundy. Hold it near your face and ask whether you look healthier or more tired. The answer is immediate.

### What you're aiming at

A small wardrobe where nothing surprises you. Two good jackets, three quality shirts, two knits, three pairs of trousers or dark jeans, two pairs of grown-up casual shoes, one proper belt.

Everything speaking the same language, everything working with everything else. The goal isn't looking fashionable — it's being harder to misread.

A few small things to try:

- Find the "almost right" item you keep not wearing and be honest about why.
- Run your next purchase through the ten-ways question before anything else.
- Buy texture instead of pattern once and see whether you notice the difference.
- Count your jackets. If four of five never get worn, you know which one fits.
