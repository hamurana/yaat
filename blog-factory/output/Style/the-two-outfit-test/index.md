{{< lead >}}
One filter sorts everything you carry: does it work with jeans and a t-shirt, and does it also work with a suit? Anything that only passes one of those is a specialist item you'll end up swapping in and out, which means eventually you won't.
{{< /lead >}}

### Why the test works

Most pocket clutter comes from owning two versions of everything and switching badly between them. Pick items that clear both settings and the switching disappears.

- **Wallet.** Slim or nothing. A bulky one bulges in every trouser cut ever made. A leather card holder with a fold-over closure beats an open-top one, because cards poking out is the whole problem you were solving. Bellroy's eight-card holder with a magnetic closure and RFID shielding is the reference version.
- **Watch.** Heritage, sport, or dress doesn't matter — subtlety does. Longines DolceVita around $1,500 sits in the sweet spot, Tissot Gentleman is the affordable end, Cartier Tank is the splurge. The test is the same for all three: t-shirt *and* tie.
- **Pen.** Reliable, refillable, and nice enough to hand someone. Montblanc Meisterstück if you're keeping it for twenty years, Cross Townsend for a similar look at a fraction.
- **Notebook.** A5 gives you room to actually think; A6 or passport size goes anywhere. Refillable is what justifies buying a good one — Aspinal's refills run about $30.
- **Bag.** It has to hold a laptop, the pen, the notebook, headphones, and sometimes clothes, and still look like something you chose. A leather briefcase for office days, a backpack when you're walking a lot.

### The cheapest win is the atomiser

This is the item nobody carries and everybody should.

A pocket cologne atomiser turns a long day into a reset. Two sprays before drinks or dinner and you've handled the one thing a good outfit can't cover. No 100ml bottle in a bag, no bathroom trip with a suitcase.

Two rules make it work:

- **One fragrance per atomiser.** Residue lingers, so mixing gives you a third scent you never chose.
- **Carry two if you want** — a daytime one and an evening one — but keep them separate.

Refilling is easier than it looks: the hole in the base sits over the stem of the full-size bottle, a few pumps, done. Creed's black leather refillable is the nice one, Travalo Milano Black is the sensible one, and generic versions work fine.

### Run sunglasses at two tiers

The usual advice is buy one good pair. Better advice is to match the pair to the risk.

- **Expensive pair** for dinner, the office, drinks — anywhere they'll sit on your face or on a table.
- **Cheap pair** for boating, the cottage, anything where they're going to get sat on or dropped in water.

Shapes stay where they've always been: Wayfarer, round, aviator. Black or tortoise when you're unsure. Always try them on — fit is most of whether a shape suits you, and no photograph will tell you.

### The last one, and the least obvious

An insulated bottle that handles hot and cold. Not exciting, and it quietly replaces both the desk water glass you never fill and the takeaway coffee you buy at eleven.

### Where the filter has limits

The two-outfit test is a good default and it isn't a law. Some genuinely great items are single-context by design — a proper dive watch, a rugged wallet if you're outdoors constantly. The test isn't there to ban those. It's there to stop you buying a third mediocre thing that's trying to be both.

A few small things to try:

- Empty your pockets and hold each item against both outfits.
- Buy an atomiser this week. It's the cheapest upgrade on this list.
- Get a second, cheap pair of sunglasses so the good ones stop going on boats.
- Swap an open card holder for one that closes.
