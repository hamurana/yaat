{{< lead >}}
The first thing that makes a man look old has nothing to do with skin, hair, or clothes. It's the look of someone who has decided the good part is behind him. That reads across a room instantly, and no grooming routine covers it.
{{< /lead >}}

### The free one is the strongest one

Purpose is the whole thing. Standing for something, having work you're not finished with, believing there are decades left rather than a wind-down.

The arithmetic supports it more than people admit. At forty you plausibly have forty more good years. People start companies at sixty-five and get fifteen genuinely productive ones out of it. A man who believes that carries himself like it, and that posture is most of what anyone reads as youth.

The stakes are real too, and worth naming. Re-entering dating after a divorce and not wanting the age gap to be the first thing anyone notices. Restarting a career at sixty against hiring managers who legally cannot ask your age and will absolutely form a view on whether you look healthy and ready to put in front of a client.

### Sun, and then everything else

If you only take one physical habit, take this: **sun damage is the biggest single cause of a face looking older.**

A daytime moisturiser with sunscreen in it, every day, indefinitely. If you know you won't apply it, wear a hat, which does the same job with no discipline required.

Beyond that, a microdermabrasion tool at home does what a spa charges $150 to $200 a session for, across the three or four sessions they'll recommend. Roughly a thousand pounds of treatments against one device you keep.

### Your teeth are more visible than your wardrobe

Gums recede with age, plaque builds, and the whole thing is gradual enough that you won't notice.

Go every six months. Not when something hurts. There's a common story here worth heeding — eighteen months skipped, then a diagnosis somewhere past gingivitis and a dentist doing damage control on gum disease that a routine clean would have prevented entirely.

Whitening is reasonable as teeth yellow. Veneers for badly damaged teeth are expensive and defensible if you're dating or moving into work where you're in front of people.

Worth being blunt about why: when someone is deciding whether they'd like to kiss you, they look at your mouth.

### Own only clothes you like

By forty or fifty most people can afford this, and almost nobody does it.

Everything in the wardrobe should make you feel good when it's on. The too-tight shirt and the jacket you tolerate aren't neutral — they quietly drain your confidence at exactly the moments you needed it, because part of your attention is on the garment instead of the room.

Get rid of them. Not to buy replacements. Just get rid of them.

### Use the change in your colouring

This one is genuinely cheering. As hair silvers and skin shifts, colours that did nothing for you at twenty-five start working.

Most men are creatures of habit and wear the same three colours for forty years, then conclude that colour isn't for them. The palette changed underneath you. Try something you ruled out decades ago.

### Where the framing is wrong

"Look ten years younger" is the promise and it's the wrong target. Nothing here makes you look twenty-nine, and chasing that is how men end up in clothes cut for their sons.

What all of it actually does is make you look **well** — rested, deliberate, in reasonable order. That reads far better than young, it's available at any age, and unlike young it doesn't expire.

A few small things to try:

- Put sunscreen on tomorrow morning and keep going.
- Book the dentist for six months out, today.
- Take three things you never enjoy wearing to a charity shop.
- Buy one colour you'd have rejected at thirty.
