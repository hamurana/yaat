Nobody is standing there cataloguing your mistakes. They register something and think slightly less of you, and if you asked them why they couldn't tell you. Cargo shorts loaded with objects make a man look lopsided. The viewer doesn't think "asymmetry". They just think *hm*.
{{< /lead >}}

### Two filters catch almost everything

Ask of any item: does this make me look **immature**, or does it make me look **cheap**? Nearly every menswear mistake is one or the other.

**Immature** — sandals and flip-flops out in public, a sports jersey on a day with no game, a slogan t-shirt, a backpack with a suit, a big loud watch demanding attention. The slogan shirt is the clearest case: it reads as a thirteen-year-old wanting to express something and not knowing how. Leave the backpack to the schoolboy and carry a satchel. A smaller, slimmer watch has always been the more elegant one.

**Cheap** — a matching pre-folded tie and pocket square set in shiny satin, which is so obviously a kit that you're better off skipping both. Shiny mall ties. Square-toed shoes, which almost never cost more than a hundred pounds and always look it.

The Windsor knot belongs in the second group, which surprises people. It's popular for being large and perfectly symmetrical. Put it in a shiny tie and it reads as someone about to sell you a car. A half-Windsor is smaller, slightly uneven, and better.

### The fit tells nobody mentions

These are silent and they're the ones actually doing damage.

- **A collar that gapes when you're standing still.** Ignore the two-finger rule — necks bend, so the test tells you nothing. The real test is whether there's a visible gap at the front while you stand normally. If there is, the shirt is wrong.
- **A tie hanging below the jacket hem.** It drags the eye downward to your crotch instead of up to your face. Ties come in short, regular, and long; if you're not average height, buy the length rather than the one on the shelf.
- **Short socks with a suit.** Sit down and there's a strip of calf. Over-the-calf, always, with tailoring.
- **A belt worn with a waistcoat.** It creates a gap between waistband and vest that shouldn't be there. Side adjusters or braces instead.
- **Armholes cut too large.** They restrict how you move and make the whole silhouette look padded.

### Keep context-specific things in context

Tennis socks belong at tennis. Mirrored sports sunglasses belong on a bike, not with a jacket — aviators or Wayfarers do the job everywhere else. A jersey belongs at the match.

The summer sandal problem has a genuinely good answer that almost nobody knows: **alpargatas**, from Mallorca, jute sole and fabric upper. Same airiness, none of the awkwardness. Perforated leather or linen shoes work too, and linen has the advantage of absorbing what feet do in heat.

### Half-committed is worse than not at all

A necktie over an unbuttoned collar is the standing example. A tie has no function whatsoever — it exists purely to be worn properly. Either do that or take it off, roll it up, and put it in your pocket.

Same logic makes a regular tie at a black tie event plainly wrong, which doesn't stop it happening on every red carpet every year.

### Price is not a style signal

There's a good cautionary story in this. A German brand called **Lloyd** sold square-toed shoes at mid-range prices for years. Because they cost a bit more than the cheap ones, a generation of buyers assumed they were proper dress shoes. An ugly shape stayed alive purely because it was priced as though it weren't.

Costing more doesn't make something right. It just makes people assume it is.

### The exception that proves the rest

None of this means never break a rule. Deliberate rule-breaking is most of what style *is* — a peak lapel on a three-piece with an action back is formally incorrect and looks tremendous if you've copied it from a 1930s magazine on purpose.

The difference is entirely in whether you knew. Broken knowingly reads as taste. Broken accidentally reads as not knowing, and everyone can tell which is which without being able to say how.

A few small things to try:

- Stand in front of a mirror and look at your collar without moving. Gap or no gap.
- Check where your tie ends relative to your jacket.
- Retire the shiniest tie you own.
- Buy over-the-calf socks before you buy anything else.
