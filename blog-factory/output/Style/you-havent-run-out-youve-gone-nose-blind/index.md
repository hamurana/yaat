{{< lead >}}
Two hours in, you can't smell your fragrance any more, so you top it up. Everyone around you can still smell it perfectly well. You haven't run out — your nose has switched off, which it does on purpose, and the reapplication is the mistake.
{{< /lead >}}

### Why your nose quits

Olfactory fatigue is a feature, not a fault. Your nose stops reporting smells that stay constant so it can notice anything new arriving. Useful in a forest. Unhelpful in an office, where it convinces you the bottle has failed.

The fix costs five minutes:

- Step outside into fresh air for about five minutes.
- Come back in.
- *Then* decide whether you can smell it.

Nine times out of ten it's still there and you were about to add three more sprays to it.

### Spray above the collarbone

The wrists-and-neck habit is inherited from nobody in particular and it isn't the best spot. The small hollow just above your collarbone is better. One spray each side.

Three reasons it works:

- **Heat without exposure.** It's as warm as your neck, which is what pushes scent off the skin, but it's tucked away rather than sitting in open air. Warm and sheltered means slower evaporation and a steadier release.
- **Partial cover.** Your collar shields it from sun and wind, so the scent builds rather than blowing off.
- **It releases in waves.** Warm air escapes from under your collar as you move, so the scent arrives in small pulses through the day instead of all at once at 8am.

The result is a bubble that holds its shape rather than a cloud that peaks and vanishes.

### Dose has a ceiling, and it isn't yours

There's a comfortable idea that fragrance is only for you. Half true, and worth being honest about the other half.

Someone sitting in a waiting room once looked up and asked out loud who on earth was wearing all that perfume. Nobody in that room was enjoying it. Your own nose is the least reliable instrument for judging how much you've put on, because it's the one that's already given up.

If you're unsure, spray onto clothing rather than skin, or simply use less. Everything has a smell and yours doesn't need to arrive first.

### The maths says wear it daily

Most people save fragrance for occasions, which is why bottles go stale on a shelf.

A 100ml bottle holds roughly 1,400 sprays. At three or four a day, that's about a year. Daily use isn't extravagant — it's the only way a bottle gets finished while it's still good.

Two practical things that help:

- **Decant into a travel atomiser.** Small refillables push together in two halves and clear airport security without a fight.
- **Or buy 50ml.** More expensive per millilitre, but it goes in a pocket, and a bottle you carry is a bottle you use.

### Layering, if you want to

Light and crisp in the morning, heavier and darker later in the day. Never the other way round — the heavy ones outlast the light ones by hours and will simply sit on top of whatever you put on first.

Scented shampoo and moisturiser usually clear in fifteen to thirty minutes and won't interfere. If yours is still going at lunchtime, switch to unscented; something that persistent is probably fighting your fragrance and losing to it.

A few small things to try:

- Take the walk before you reach for the bottle.
- Move your two sprays to the collarbone hollow for a week and see whether anyone comments later in the day.
- Decant one bottle into an atomiser and actually carry it.
- Ask one person you trust whether you wear too much. Your nose can't answer this.
