{{< lead >}}
Almost nothing that makes daily life feel richer requires more money. It requires the same money pointed somewhere else. Four takeaways becomes good olive oil and bread from a real bakery. A season of cheap tops becomes one wool blazer. The budget doesn't move.
{{< /lead >}}

### Redirect first, then buy

Start with money already leaving your account, because that spending is invisible and enormous.

- **Takeaway → groceries.** Good olive oil, bread from a bakery that took two or three days to make it, meat from a butcher. Bread with a properly peppery olive oil and salt is a better evening than most delivery.
- **Fast fashion → one good thing.** A 100% wool coat or blazer, or vintage cashmere from the eighties and nineties, which is usually better made than what's on the shelf now and cheaper.
- **New → secondhand.** Leather bags are the clearest case. They soften and take on a patina, so age is a feature, and the best ones are found used at a fraction of retail.

### The small ones that do the most

None of these cost much, and they run every day.

- **Coffee in a ceramic cup, sitting down.** Not a takeaway cup at a desk. Annoyingly, plenty of cafés stopped putting out ceramic even for people staying in, which rather defeats the point — so this may be a thing you do at home.
- **A morning ritual you look forward to.** A Chemex and decent beans. A gooseneck kettle — a Fellow will still be going a decade later and looks like an object rather than an appliance.
- **Matching velvet hangers.** Twenty or thirty pounds turns a wardrobe into something that resembles a shop. It is a ridiculous amount of effect for the money.
- **Fruit or herbs in your water.** Lemon, mint, cucumber. Free if you grow the mint.
- **Real plants**, many of them free through propagation from someone else's.
- **Dried eucalyptus in the shower.** Lasts a week to ten days and turns a shower into something else entirely.
- **A stovetop simmer** — one lemon, a splash of vanilla, a cinnamon stick — for a house that smells like someone lives in it well.

### Buy quality only where you'll notice

Two items are worth being fussy about because you use them constantly.

**A genuinely good white t-shirt.** 100% cotton, a reinforced collar that won't go limp and wavy after a few washes, not see-through, and slightly heavier than you'd expect. The useful finding here is that this is not a price problem — testing across everything from five pounds to five hundred lands you somewhere in the middle. The expensive ones are not the best ones.

**Nice bedding.** Cotton, muslin, linen, or bamboo in summer for the cooling. Far cheaper than a new mattress and you'll notice it every night. Silk pillowcases are the small version of the same idea — less friction on skin and hair, and the difference shows up if you stop.

### Slowing down *is* the thing

The pattern under all of this is that the object isn't the luxury. The pause is.

Physical books instead of an audiobook at 1.5x. A walk in trees — the Japanese have a name for it, forest bathing, and some doctors there credit it with lowering blood pressure and calming stress. A four-product skincare routine, drugstore prices, done slowly. A monthly manicure. A farmers market instead of a supermarket run.

The list is titled thirty and lands on twenty-nine, which feels about right for something this loose. You aren't meant to do all of it.

A few small things to try:

- Pick one thing you already buy and buy the better version instead of the extra.
- Put the good coffee in a real cup and sit down with it.
- Buy the hangers. It's a silly recommendation and it works.
- Grow one herb. Basil or mint. Dried is not the same thing.
