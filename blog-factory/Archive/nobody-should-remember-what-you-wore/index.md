{{< lead >}}
The best compliment isn't about the jacket. It's that somebody remembers you looked excellent and can't recall a single item you had on. That's the whole target, and it's the opposite of how most people shop — which is for the piece somebody will mention.
{{< /lead >}}

### Seven rules, and one of them matters most

Quality over quantity. Timeless over trendy. Fit above everything. Subtlety. A conservative palette. Accessories chosen carefully. And confidence built through repetition.

**Fit is the one people skip**, and it's the expensive mistake. Men spend hundreds or thousands on clothing and never once have it adjusted to their body. An altered mid-price jacket beats an unaltered expensive one every time, and it isn't close. Budget for the tailor at the moment you buy, or only buy things that already fit close to perfectly.

On quality over quantity, the maths is plain: one piece you wear a hundred times is a better purchase than three sale items you never reach for.

### Four colours carry it

**Navy, beige, grey, white.** Everything else is seasoning.

Those four interchange with each other in every direction, which is what makes a small wardrobe feel large. Add a colour if you like, but add it as an accent rather than a foundation.

The reliable summer combination sitting inside that palette: navy blazer, white shirt, light trousers, plain white pocket square. It has worked for a century and shows no sign of stopping.

### Accessories are a chain

The weakest one decides the outfit. A good suit with a shiny tie is a shiny-tie outfit.

- **Tie**, if a tie is needed — silk.
- **Pocket square** — white, presidential fold. Clean and finished, no folding gymnastics.
- **Sunglasses** — no visible logos, a classic shape that suits your face. Aviators are the baseline, Wayfarers are strong and not for everyone, and Clubmasters in tortoise are the quietly sophisticated pick most men never consider. Modern tortoise is a plastic pattern, incidentally, not shell.
- **A straw hat**, if you'll actually wear it. The Panama is the famous one and also the most pronounced — it takes some confidence. Fedoras, trilbies, and boaters are gentler. Try them on in person; head shape decides this more than taste does.

### Summer specifics

**Buy linen and accept the wrinkles.** They're its character, not a maintenance failure. Cut it slightly looser than a normal button-down and go off-white, beige, or cream. A casual linen suit is also a strong answer for a summer wedding.

**Drop jeans for the season.** Light trousers in cream, stone, or off-white, in cotton, linen, a blend, or a performance fabric — cooler and more elegant, and it takes almost no adjustment.

**Get a knit polo.** Popularised in the 1920s and refined by the Italians in the 1930s, who made the first knitted versions. What to look for: a lightweight **12 to 16 gauge**, **fully-fashioned construction** — knitted to shape so the seams lie flat and it drapes properly — a Johnny collar or one to three buttons, ribbed hem and cuffs, and no branding to speak of.

**Low-vamp loafers.** More of the foot exposed means cooler and slightly more casual than the four-season pair. Tan, dark brown, or black. Espadrilles are the cheap elegant alternative and honestly last about a season.

Chinos, while we're here, came back with American soldiers stationed in the Philippines over a century ago. Still the best weekend trouser there is.

### The bit that takes time

Dressing well is **unfamiliar, not uncomfortable.** Those feel identical for the first month and they are not the same thing.

It works like a gym. The first few visits you don't know where anything is and you feel conspicuous. Thirty visits later it's the easiest part of the day. Tailored clothing runs on the same curve, and people quit during the awkward stretch, concluding it isn't for them.

Worth noting that a piece pitched as a hot-weather trick is really a set of rules about restraint. That's a better thing to have anyway — a shopping list expires and rules don't.

A few small things to try:

- Take one jacket you own to a tailor and see what changes.
- Count how many of your clothes sit outside navy, beige, grey, and white.
- Buy one linen shirt and stop ironing it flat.
- Wear the unfamiliar thing five more times before deciding.
