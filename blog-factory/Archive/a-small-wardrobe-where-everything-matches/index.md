# A Small Wardrobe Where Everything Matches

{{< lead >}}
Daniel Craig's Bond wardrobe was probably smaller than most people assume. Navy, grey, white, black. No patterns worth mentioning, no logos, nothing that dated. The fit did the work, which is why it aged well rather than badly.
{{< /lead >}}

### The palette is the trick

Restricting yourself to four colours sounds limiting and does the opposite. Every jacket goes with every trouser. Every knit works under every jacket. Getting dressed stops requiring decisions because there aren't any wrong combinations left.

That's the actual mechanism behind "he always looks good." It's not taste applied daily. It's taste applied once, to what got bought.

### The pieces that carry it

- **A suit that's properly tailored.** Shoulder seam landing exactly on your natural shoulder, no padding. High armholes so you can move. Trousers tapered cleanly to the ankle. Navy or grey, because the fit is doing the work and a pattern would only get in its way. Canvas construction moulds to you over years; fused doesn't.
- **A navy polo.** Slim, decent cotton that holds its shape, sleeves short enough to look athletic, body long enough to stay tucked. Works under a blazer, with tailored trousers, or with dark jeans. The Sunspel Riviera is the actual Casino Royale one.
- **Chelsea boots.** No laces, slim, ankle support, and they work with a suit or with jeans. Brown suede casual, black leather formal. Refined toe but not pointed, discreet elastic, not cowboy height. Goodyear-welted means resoling forever.
- **Trousers with no break.** Hemmed to barely touch the shoe. Flat front, no pleats, side adjusters instead of belt loops where possible, sitting at your natural waist rather than the hips.
- **Fine-gauge knitwear.** Slim, solid, thin enough to layer under a jacket without bulk. Navy, grey, charcoal. A mock neck works particularly well past 40 — it lengthens the neck and frames the jaw.
- **Rectangular sunglasses.** No logos, no gradient lenses. Squared frames tend to work better than round or aviator as features soften, because they keep some hard lines in the picture.
- **A linen shirt for off-duty.** Pale blue, white, natural. Tailored rather than the default beach tent. Linen actually improves with washing, which cheap versions can't do.
- **One diver's watch.** The Omega Seamaster in Craig's case — water resistant, legible in any light, tough enough for daily wear, and still fine with black tie. Competence rather than showing off.
- **Almost no other accessories.** A watch, sometimes sunglasses. That's close to all of it. No bracelets, no chains, no pocket square doing acrobatics.

### Why it improved with age

He turned 50 during the Bond run and looked more commanding at the end than the start, which is the opposite of how this usually goes.

The reason is that none of it depended on being young. Restraint, fit and quality don't have an expiry date, where trend-chasing does. So the approach isn't really for young men at all — it suits someone who's worked out who he is and would like the clothes to stop arguing with it.

### Where to actually start

Not by buying. By fixing what you own.

Get the suits you already have properly tailored. Find a polo that grips your arms instead of hanging off them. Have your trousers hemmed to the right length. Those three things cost less than most men spend on one unnecessary purchase and they change everything already in the wardrobe.

### A few small things to try

- Pick four colours and buy only within them for a year
- Get one existing suit altered before buying a new one
- Have every pair of trousers you own hemmed to the same length
- Take everything off your wrists except a watch, for a week
