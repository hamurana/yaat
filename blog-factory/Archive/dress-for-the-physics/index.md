{{< lead >}}
Summer dressing is usually argued as taste. Most of it is physics, and the numbers are less subtle than you'd expect. A black cotton tee absorbs around ten times the heat a white one does. That isn't a preference. That's a measurement.
{{< /lead >}}

### The reflectance numbers

Measured on cotton t-shirts, roughly:

- **White** — about 1.0, reflecting nearly everything
- **Pastels** — 0.9
- **Light grey** — 0.88
- **Sky blue** — 0.7
- **Charcoal, navy, black** — 0.1 down to 0.05

Dark colours absorb around ninety-five per cent of what hits them, and that energy has to go somewhere. It goes into the fabric, and then into you.

So the best hot-weather shirts are white or pastel — light pink, light blue, mint, lavender. Trousers in khaki, light grey, ivory, white linen. Black, navy, and charcoal are the ones actively working against you in July.

### Weave matters more than the fabric's name

People shop for "linen" as though the word does the cooling. What's doing it is gaps between yarns.

Linen, seersucker, and tropical-weight wool all work for the same reason — the weave is open enough for air to move through. That's also why a tightly woven linen can be hotter than an open-weave wool, which surprises everyone.

Worth knowing by name: **high-twist fresco**, which is worth paying for; **mohair blends**, where the fibre's near-glass surface sheds moisture and heat; and **hopsack**, which is rough to the touch and very breathable.

### Airflow can beat colour

Here's the interesting exception. Bedouins wear black robes in the desert and don't cook, which sounds like it should be impossible given the numbers above.

The answer is the chimney effect. Loose clothing lets air circulate underneath, and moving air carries heat away faster than dark fabric absorbs it. Fit can overrule colour.

Practically:

- Looser trousers, rolled at the ankle.
- Skip the belt — it seals the bottom of the chimney. Braces if you need them.
- Roomier linen shirts rather than fitted ones.

### The undershirt nobody expects

Counter-intuitive and it works: a skin-tight, very lightweight poly-blend undershirt, close to gauze weight — not a compression layer.

It does two things. It keeps sweat off the shirt you actually care about, and it creates an evaporation layer that leaves you cooler than bare skin under cotton.

### Cool your head, literally

The grooming side is small and genuinely effective.

**Menthol** works on TRPM8, the cold receptor. A mentholated shampoo or body wash tells your brain it's cooler than it is and increases blood flow to the skin, which helps dump actual heat. It isn't only a trick. Peppermint cooling powder does the same job where you'd want it.

**Switch to a water-based hair product.** Oil-based wax and clay sit on your scalp and trap heat. Water-based washes out without shampoo, which is convenient in its own right.

**Trim the beard to five to seven millimetres.** A long beard is insulation you're wearing on your face.

### What this all adds up to

Light colours, open weaves, loose fit, a thin evaporation layer, and menthol. Five decisions, all made once, none of them about taste.

Get those right and the clothes stop being the thing you're enduring in August.

A few small things to try:

- Look at what colour you reach for on the hottest day. Change it.
- Hold a shirt up to the light. If you can't see through the weave, it isn't a summer shirt.
- Take the belt off and try braces for a week.
- Buy a mentholated body wash. It costs nothing and it works.
