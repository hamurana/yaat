# Heat Is Not an Excuse to Stop Deciding

{{< lead >}}
Nine summer rules, and they all come from the same place. Warmth plus informality makes people stop choosing and start defaulting — and every item below is what defaulting looks like.
{{< /lead >}}

### The nine

- **Flip flops away from water.** Fine at a pool or a beach. Not in a supermarket or a restaurant. If there's no water in sight, leave them. Espadrilles, loafers, or leather sandals do the same job without the sound.
- **The wrong fabric.** Heavy cotton and polyester don't breathe, and almost all cheap summer clothing is polyester. Linen, seersucker, Egyptian cotton, tropical wool. This single choice does more for comfort than anything else here.
- **Shorts after dark.** Daytime and genuine heat, fine. Evening is a more formal time of day whether or not anyone enforces it, so change into trousers.
- **Shorts past the knee.** An 11 or 13 inch inseam makes you look shorter and your legs thinner. Seven or nine inches, hem just above the knee. Swim shorts can go to five.
- **Winter shoes with summer clothes.** Black leather dress shoes don't belong with shorts. Footwear is seasonal in a way people accept for clothing and forget for shoes.
- **Sports sunglasses when no sport is happening.** Wrap-around frames are built for cycling. At lunch they just look like you came from somewhere else. Tortoiseshell wayfarers or plain aviators instead.
- **Sports jerseys outside the stadium.** They're for watching the game at the game. Everything you wear says something, and this one says you'd stopped thinking about it.
- **Sunglasses indoors or after sunset.** You can see them and they can't see you, which people find genuinely off-putting rather than mysterious. Fold them into a pocket.
- **White trousers that aren't clean.** White linen is excellent and only while immaculate. Marked white is worse than not wearing white at all. If you're going to, commit to the dry cleaning, check chairs before sitting, and use the napkin.

### The one rule underneath

Read them together and none are really about summer.

They're about the fact that heat gives you a permanent excuse — it's hot, so who cares — and that excuse is available every day for three months. Convenience quietly replaces intention, one small decision at a time, and by August you're in the supermarket in flip flops wondering why you feel scruffy.

The answer isn't suffering in a jacket. Every one of these has a comfortable alternative, which is the whole point: linen is *more* comfortable than polyester, espadrilles are as easy as flip flops, nine-inch shorts are no warmer than thirteen. Nothing here trades comfort for looks. It trades defaulting for choosing.

### Worth saying plainly

Some of this is context-dependent in a way lists like these never admit. Sports jerseys are perfectly normal in plenty of places, and the sunglasses-indoors rule is about eye contact rather than etiquette.

The transferable part is the underlying question rather than the nine specifics: did I pick this, or did I reach for it because it was warm and nearby?

### A few small things to try

- Check the label on your summer clothes and count how much is polyester
- Change into trousers before going out to dinner, once, and see if you feel different
- Move the flip flops to wherever you keep beach things
- Take your sunglasses off when you walk indoors
