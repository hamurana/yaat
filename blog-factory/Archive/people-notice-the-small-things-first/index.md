# People Notice the Small Things First

{{< lead >}}
You can buy the right jacket, match your colours, get the whole thing broadly correct, and still be undone by a hem dragging on the floor. The small details register faster than the big picture, and most of them cost nothing to fix.
{{< /lead >}}

### Fit details you can sort this week

- **Sleeve length.** A quarter to half an inch of shirt cuff showing past the jacket sleeve — up to an inch if you like. Fixable by a tailor for very little.
- **Trouser break.** Taller men can take a full break; shorter men should go quarter or none. It's one of the fastest ways to change your proportions.
- **Dragging hems.** Frayed denim mopping the pavement undoes everything above it.
- **Silhouette.** A structured shoulder builds you out at the top, and buttoning the middle button gives a cleaner vertical line — genuinely useful if you're carrying twenty or fifty pounds you'd rather not advertise.
- **Monochrome.** Dark on dark, or light on light, lets the eye travel up and down rather than stopping at your middle.

### Maintenance nobody thinks about

- **Pilling.** A fabric shaver takes a bobbled sweater back to nearly new. A sweater stone, a comb, even a razor will do it.
- **Lint.** Keep a roller by the door and one in the car. Pet owners stop seeing their own pet hair entirely, and people with allergies notice it immediately.
- **Shoes.** The foundation of the outfit. Buy the best you can afford, keep them clean, and match the formality to what you're wearing.
- **Socks.** For a no-show look, buy ones that genuinely can't be seen. And never wear socks with holes — the moment that matters is shoes-off at someone's parents' house, and it will happen eventually.

### Grooming that's visible up close

- **The back of your neck.** Trim or shave the neckline. A good haircut with a shaggy neck reads as a bad haircut.
- **Nails.** Clippers work, scissors are better, and a file is the underused one — file in one direction.
- **Your tongue.** A scraper clears the grooves and it affects your breath, which is very noticeable at conversational distance.

### The watch point

A watch is jewellery and a conversation starter, and it's the easiest bit of polish available. A G-Shock for the gym, a field watch with a canvas strap, or a dive watch, which dresses up or down better than anything else — a few hundred for a Seiko or Orient, or thousands if you want.

The observation attached is the useful bit: she almost certainly won't recognise the brand. She'll notice that you're someone who sweats the details.

### What it's actually about

Read the list back and none of it is style exactly. It's evidence.

Every item is a small piece of proof about whether you pay attention when nobody's checking — because a lint roller, a nail file and a tongue scraper are all things you use alone. That's why they register so quickly on other people, and why they can't be substituted with a more expensive jacket.

The confidence cues fold into the same thing. Shoulders back rather than hunched. Eye contact instead of looking away — the person who made this admits he used to look down whenever a woman looked back at him, and fixed it by making himself greet everyone until it became automatic. And basic manners, which read within seconds.

### A few small things to try

- Check the back of your neck in a mirror with a second mirror
- Buy a fabric shaver and rescue one sweater you'd written off
- Throw away every pair of socks with a hole in it, today
- Get your sleeves altered — it's cheap and it changes every jacket you own
