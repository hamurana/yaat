# The Boring Eight

{{< lead >}}
The foods that do the most for long-term health are cheap, unglamorous, and eaten on repeat. There's no exotic ingredient on this list. The active mechanism is repetition.
{{< /lead >}}

### Why a short list beats a perfect diet

Two or three of these eaten daily, permanently, does more than an immaculate diet followed for six weeks. The failure mode in nutrition is almost never that people don't know what's healthy — it's that the plan is too complex to survive a normal week.

There's a second effect that gets overlooked. Every one of these foods displaces something else. Adding raw vegetables before dinner subtracts the crisps you'd otherwise eat. The benefit is doubled and mostly invisible.

### 1. Dark leafy greens

Spinach, kale, chard, rocket, watercress. Fibre, folate, vitamin K — and crucially, **nitrates**.

Your body converts dietary nitrate to nitrite and then to nitric oxide, the molecule that relaxes artery walls and lowers blood pressure. That is the same pathway targeted by glyceryl trinitrate, the spray given under the tongue during a heart attack or angina attack. Greens are a slow, gentle version of an emergency drug.

This is also why antibacterial mouthwash is worth avoiding — the conversion depends on bacteria living on your tongue.

### 2. Fermented food

Kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi, kombucha. These deliver live microbes rather than just fibre.

The evidence here is better than most people assume. [Wastyk et al. (2021, *Cell*)](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(21)00754-6) randomised 36 healthy adults to ten weeks of either a high-fermented-food or high-fibre diet. The fermented-food group showed **increased gut microbial diversity and decreased inflammatory markers**, with four immune cell types showing less activation — and larger servings produced stronger effects.

The genuinely surprising result: **the high-fibre arm showed neither.** None of the 19 inflammatory proteins fell, and microbial diversity stayed flat across ten weeks. That doesn't mean fibre is useless — it does other things well, including feeding butyrate production — but it does mean fermented foods are not interchangeable with fibre, and the diversity effect belongs to fermentation.

One practical caveat: buy from the **chilled section**. Shelf-stable pasteurised versions have no live cultures. Watch the sugar in sweetened kombucha.

### 3. Nuts

Peanuts, almonds, cashews, walnuts, pecans. Fats, plant protein, fibre, magnesium.

Nuts have trial evidence behind them, not just correlation: in [PREDIMED](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1800389), adding nuts to a Mediterranean diet was one of two arms that produced roughly a 30% relative reduction in major cardiovascular events versus a reduced-fat control.

They're calorie-dense, so a small handful — up to two — is the sensible quantity. Skip the ultra-processed flavour-coated versions.

### 4. Deeply coloured plants

Blueberries, blackberries, beetroot, red cabbage. The colour is polyphenols, particularly anthocyanins, with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity. Beetroot doubles as a nitrate source, feeding the same nitric oxide pathway as the greens.

### 5. Omega-3 from oily fish

Tinned sardines and mackerel are the cheap route. EPA is the more anti-inflammatory of the two main forms; DHA is a structural component of brain and retina. Five or six days a week is a reasonable target, and tinned fish makes that realistic in a way fresh salmon doesn't.

### 6. Coffee

Around three cups, black. This is the item people expect to see on a list of things to cut.

[Poole et al.'s umbrella review in the *BMJ*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5765813/), covering 201 meta-analyses of observational data and 17 of trials, found the largest risk reductions at **three to four cups a day** versus none: all-cause mortality relative risk 0.83, cardiovascular mortality 0.81, cardiovascular disease 0.85. Coffee is also among the largest polyphenol sources in most Western diets.

One rule that matters more than the quantity: **nothing after 2pm.** Caffeine's half-life is long enough that an afternoon coffee measurably degrades sleep architecture, and sleep is worth more than the coffee.

### 7. Raw vegetables before the evening meal

Carrots, celery, cucumber, peppers, sugar-snap peas, with hummus. This is a habit rather than a food.

It works twice. It replaces the pre-dinner crisps or chocolate eaten while cooking, and the fibre and protein arriving first blunt the glucose rise from the carbohydrate that follows — an effect that becomes obvious the moment anyone wears a continuous glucose monitor for a few weeks.

### 8. Eggs

Close to a complete protein, cheap, and fast. The dietary cholesterol concern has substantially receded — for most people, cholesterol eaten has limited effect on cholesterol measured in blood.

**Keep the yolks.** That's where the choline sits, along with lutein and zeaxanthin, which concentrate in the retina. A white-only omelette discards most of the nutritional argument for eating eggs.

### Summary

The recurring signals across all eight are colour, fermentation, and fibre — and the mechanism that matters most is daily repetition rather than any single ingredient. Greens and beetroot feed the nitric oxide pathway that relaxes arteries, the same target as an emergency angina spray. Fermented foods raised microbial diversity and lowered inflammatory markers in a randomised trial where a high-fibre diet did neither. Nuts have trial-level cardiovascular evidence via PREDIMED. Coffee at three to four cups shows its largest associated risk reduction for all-cause and cardiovascular mortality.

**What to actually do:**

1. **Pick two or three, not eight.** Greens, fermented food and eggs are the easiest to make automatic.
2. **Buy fermented foods from the chilled section** — pasteurised shelf-stable versions don't carry live cultures.
3. **Put raw vegetables and hummus out while you cook.** It replaces a snack and flattens the meal that follows.
4. **Move oily fish to tinned** if fresh isn't happening. Sardines five days a week beats salmon once a month.
5. **Keep coffee, cut it off at 2pm.**
6. **Stop discarding yolks.**
7. **Accept a good-enough pattern.** Occasional treats against a solid background genuinely don't matter.
