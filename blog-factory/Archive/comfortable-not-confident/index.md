# Comfortable, Not Confident

{{< lead >}}
Trying to look confident almost always fails, because it reads as trying. Looking *comfortable* — anywhere, with anyone — is the thing people actually respond to, and it can't really be performed.
{{< /lead >}}

### Why calm gets read as status

There's a shortcut running in everyone's head: a relaxed person must be important, or must know something the rest of us don't.

It's not a reasoned conclusion, it's an inference made in under a second. Panic under pressure makes people trust you less. Composure makes them assume there's a reason for it.

Which is why Lalo Salamanca dominates every room he walks into in *Better Call Saul* without doing anything forceful. He's not the strongest character on that show. He's the one people are still discussing years later, because he understood the people in front of him better than anyone else in the story.

### The pieces

- **Presence.** Full attention — eye contact, actual reactions, leaning in. It makes whoever he's talking to feel like the only person there, and the brain reads that as respect. It's also rare enough now to function as an advantage, given most conversations happen alongside a phone.
- **Humour, used sparingly.** Not constant jokes. Well-timed lightness, which is genuinely disorienting when there's tension underneath — it's hard to stay afraid of someone who seems fun. He arrives smiling, joking, sometimes cooking, while everything simmers.
- **No hunt for approval.** He never flexes, never fishes for validation, and ends up the centre of the room anyway. Desperation leaks out of people no matter how well they cover it. Self-acceptance seems to pull the other direction.
- **Slightly unpredictable.** Brains are prediction engines, and one that can't predict you stays switched on. Being entirely predictable is socially dull, which is a mildly uncomfortable thing to notice about yourself.
- **Unhurried movement.** Slow walk, loose shoulders, comfortable posture. Rushing signals anxiety even when you're not anxious.

### Where the model needs a warning

The note is honest about this and it's worth repeating: these tools are morally neutral. The exact same set inspires people or manipulates them.

The difference is what it feels like to be near you. Healthy charisma makes people feel safe. The other kind makes them feel handled — and often they can't say why until later.

Lalo is a fun character to learn from and he's also a murderer, so the model has a natural limit. The appeal is a fantasy of total ease and never caring what anyone thinks, which is exactly what someone who overthinks everything would want. Take the calm and the presence. Leave the part where other people are pieces on a board.

### The line worth keeping

*Confidence is comfort, not a pose.*

That reframes the whole project. You can't get there by adopting postures, because the posture is the output rather than the input. What you can do is spend more time in rooms until they stop being unusual, which is slower and duller and actually works.

### A few small things to try

- Put the phone away entirely for one conversation and give it everything
- Slow your walk down, deliberately, and see what it does to your head
- Notice when you're fishing for approval — that's the leak
- Ask whether people around you feel safe or handled. That's the whole test
