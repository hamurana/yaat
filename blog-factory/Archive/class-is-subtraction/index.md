# Class Is Subtraction

{{< lead >}}
Almost every signal of class is something removed rather than added. No logo, no raised voice, no scene when you're told no, no performance when nobody's watching. It's mostly an absence, which is why it can't be bought.
{{< /lead >}}

### The look

The wealthiest buyers moved away from visible branding years ago, toward things only people already in that world recognise. Researchers call it inconspicuous consumption. The practical version is simpler: **wanting to look rich is the reliable sign of someone who isn't.**

Borrow the engineering idea of signal and noise. The thoughtful, well-fitted piece is signal. Extra patterns, extra accessories, visible monograms — noise. Class is a high ratio of one to the other, and you improve it faster by subtracting noise than by adding signal.

Grooming does more than clothing here, and it counts on the days nobody will see you. That's what makes it read as self-respect rather than vanity.

### The behaviour

- **How you take a no.** This is the giveaway. Not crumbling, not overcompensating, not making it anyone's problem. Turned down at a bar, you order another drink and carry on.
- **Listening more than talking.** The strongest person in most conversations says the least. Chris Voss's ratio from hostage negotiation is roughly 80% listening, 20% speaking — and making that 20% count.
- **The pause before reacting.** When something insults or threatens you, stress hormones flood in and you get measurably worse at thinking. Whatever you say in that window is written by chemistry. Waiting is not weakness; it's the only way to answer with your actual intelligence.

### The person

The foundation is behaving the same way when nobody's watching. There's a nice piece of evidence for how rare that is — psychologists put a picture of eyes above a café tip jar and takings went up roughly threefold compared with other images. People behave differently when they feel observed, which is exactly why doing it unobserved means something.

Validation has to come from inside, which is harder now than it's ever been given how much of it is available on tap and how little it's worth.

And depth comes from having actually done things — new places, new skills, failures. That's what people are picking up on when they say someone has presence. It's accumulated experience rather than a technique.

### The thing it's all downstream of

Purpose. Class isn't really pursuable directly; it's what falls out of someone absorbed in something that matters to them.

Viktor Frankl's observation from the camps was that survival didn't track physical strength — it tracked having a reason. That's a much heavier example than a style video needs, and the smaller version holds too: someone genuinely occupied by their work doesn't need the room to confirm anything, and that's most of what reads as class.

There's a related point about reputation. Cutting corners can be quick and it's a one-time trade — get caught and nobody works with you again. Doing honest work quietly compounds into something you can actually trade on for decades. Slower, and it's the only version that keeps paying.

### Where I'd add a caveat

The trouble with any list like this is that it can be run as a performance — quiet luxury as its own kind of signalling, restraint deployed to be noticed.

The test is whether you'd keep doing it with nobody around to register it. If the unbranded coat is a different way of being seen, it's the same game with better taste.

### A few small things to try

- Take one logo out of your regular rotation
- Aim for listening about 80% of your next conversation
- Wait a beat the next time something stings before you respond
- Notice one thing you do differently when someone's watching, and stop doing it differently
