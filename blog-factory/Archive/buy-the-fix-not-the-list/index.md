Every everyday-carry list is really fifteen answers to fifteen problems. Most people read it as a shopping list and end up with a heavier bag and the same frustrations. The useful move is smaller: name the two or three things that actually annoy you, then buy only those.
{{< /lead >}}

### Start from the annoyance

Working out of a bag has a short list of real pain points, and they're specific enough to name.

- **Cables knotted at the bottom of the bag.** A dedicated pouch fixes this permanently. The Alpaka Air Pouch 2 in Ultra is 1.5 litres, stands upright, and has a hi-vis green liner so small black things stop disappearing into a black hole.
- **The laptop dying mid-call.** The Anker Prime 26K holds 26,250mAh and pushes 300W across three ports — enough to take a 16" MacBook Pro to half charge in half an hour. At 99.75Wh it stays under airline carry-on limits, which is the detail that matters if you fly.
- **The person on speakerphone in the coffee shop.** Bose QuietComfort Ultra, thirty hours with the noise cancelling on. This is the single item most likely to change your day.

If none of those are your problem, none of them are your purchase.

### Standardise on one cable

The quiet win running through any good kit is USB-C on everything. One charger, one spare cable, nothing proprietary.

A GaN charger like the Nomad 130W does three USB-C ports with smart wattage routing, which means the wall adapter stops being three wall adapters. Torches, power banks, headphones, handhelds — if it takes USB-C, it joins the system. If it needs its own brick, it's a tax you pay every time you pack.

### Buy over-built, not more

The pieces worth spending on are the ones you'd otherwise replace twice.

- **Evergoods Civic Panel Loader**, 24L in X-Pac X42 — waterproof shell, PU-coated YKK zips, clamshell opening, coyote brown inside so you can see, and a lifetime warranty.
- **Olight Arc Pro** — 1,500 lumens, 205m throw, IPX7, and it charges over USB-C rather than something you'll lose.
- **The James Brand Kent** bolt-action pen — stainless, takes standard Parker-style G2 refills, so it never becomes a pen you can't feed.
- **Casio G-Shock GW-5000HS-1** — solar, atomic timekeeping, made in Japan, and about ten months of running on a full charge.

### The caveats matter more than the specs

This is the part most lists skip, and it's the part worth reading.

- The **SanDisk Extreme Portable SSD** is fast and rugged, and there are enough reports of drives failing or dropping out under sustained use that the sensible rule is to treat it as working storage and never as your only copy.
- The **Satechi On-the-Go hub** is a genuinely clever puck with seven ports, and its single USB-C connection means you can charge or move data, not both.
- The **Stanley Wellspring** is a good bottle whose push-button lid will stick if you don't rinse it before bed.

None of these are reasons not to buy. They're the difference between a recommendation and an advert.

### The honest limit of any list

A bag full of excellent gear you don't use is just a heavier bag. The aim is fewer things that each remove a specific irritation — not a kit that could survive a week in the woods when you're going to a café.

A few small things to try:

- Empty the bag completely and only put back what you touched this week.
- Write down your three actual frustrations before browsing anything.
- Replace one proprietary cable with USB-C.
- Buy one over-built thing instead of two adequate ones.
