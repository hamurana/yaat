The usual objection to a safety razor is time. Three passes, a brush, a lather, cleaning up afterwards — fine on a Sunday, impossible on a Tuesday. There's a hybrid that gets the same day-long smoothness in five or six minutes.
{{< /lead >}}

### The fast one

1. **Normal warm shower.** That's your preparation. No towel, no separate soak.
2. **Cream from a tube, applied by hand.** Skip the brush and skip washing it out afterwards.
3. **A wet-capable electric shaver** to take off the bulk. This replaces the first pass and possibly the second, it's quicker, and there's no cut risk. Wet beats dry here — check your shaver is actually rated for water.
4. **Reapply cream, then one manual pass against the grain.** One. This is where the closeness comes from, and doing only one pass makes your blades last considerably longer.
5. **Cold water, then a post-shave balm.**

The cost of entry is a waterproof electric shaver, two or three hundred pounds you weren't necessarily going to spend. Over years of mornings it's an easy trade.

### The full version, when you have ten minutes

The classic method is worth knowing because the hybrid is a compression of it.

**Prepare.** Hot shower, or a wet towel warmed in the microwave held on the face, or just splashing hot water for a minute or two. Hair needs softening before a blade goes near it.

**Lather.** Cream from a tube, or a hard soap worked up with a brush. The brush is genuinely better — it coats around each hair and lifts it slightly rather than just sitting on top.

**Load the blade straight.** A crooked blade cuts you. Check it every time.

**Know your grain**, and go with it first — that's the gentlest pass. Then across. Then against, which cuts below the skin surface and is what keeps you smooth for about twenty-four hours. Most men stop after the second pass and that's a reasonable place to stop.

### The two things that prevent cuts

**Keep the skin taut.** Loose skin is where every nick comes from. Pull the sideburn upward with your free hand. Puff your cheeks out. Push your tongue against the inside of your cheek for the awkward areas under the nose and mouth.

**Angle and pressure.** Hold the handle at roughly **30 to 45 degrees** — you can hear when it's right — and use almost no pressure at all. Let the weight of the razor do it. Short, slow strokes, straight down, while you're learning. A slight diagonal drag comes later, and slant heads are aggressive enough that they only suit thick hair; if your beard is fine, a gentler razor gets you the same result with less blood.

Rinse the blade between strokes, and put fresh cream on for every pass. Dried lather clogs the head and stops protecting anything.

### The awkward bits

- **Under the nose and mouth** — tongue and puffed air to give yourself a flat surface.
- **The jawline** — pull the skin upward.
- **Under the ear** — needs a different direction from everything around it.
- **The chin** — use your fingers and your own face muscles, and go at it from several directions.

### Finishing

Cold water at the end. Some people use ice water to close pores and stop any bleeding; cold tap is fine.

Then a post-shave balm. Avoid the traditional alcohol aftershave — it stings because it's drying your skin out, which is not the same as it working. An inexpensive sensitive-skin balm does more.

### One honest note

Pain or pulling is not something to push through. It means one of two things: your preparation was insufficient, or your blade is dull. Both are five-minute fixes and neither improves by continuing.

A few small things to try:

- Buy the waterproof shaver and run the hybrid on weekdays.
- Reapply cream between passes rather than shaving through what's left.
- Check your blade is seated straight before every shave.
- Swap alcohol aftershave for a balm.
