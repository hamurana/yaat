### What lateness actually says

Not that you're disorganised, although you might be. It says the other person wasn't worth planning around. That's the message received, every time, regardless of the message sent.

It compounds quietly. People stop giving you real times. They stop counting on you for things where the timing matters. Nobody announces any of this.

### The other six

- **Never being the one who says "my fault."** In an ambiguous bump, the well-mannered move is to take it, even if it probably wasn't you. It costs you nothing and it ends the moment.
- **"I'm just blunt."** Bluntness that wasn't necessary usually creates more clarifying, more smoothing over, more work. A softer version of the same sentence sets the same boundary without the cleanup.
- **Being polite only upward.** Manners that appear around people who can do something for you and vanish elsewhere aren't manners. Everyone can see the switch, including the people you're trying to impress.
- **The phone.** The problem isn't using it — it's using it without noticing. The check is a question: am I handling something, or did my hand just do that?
- **Ignoring casual invitations.** A group dinner needs an answer as much as a wedding does. Reply the way you were invited — text for a text, call for a call. Confirm whether you're bringing someone, and say the moment your plans change, because somebody is buying food based on your answer.
- **Thinking etiquette means cutlery.** Which fork is the least important thing in the whole subject and it's the part everyone means when they say the word.

### The Irish exit, adjudicated

Leaving a large casual party without saying goodbye to everyone is fine. Nobody needs a farewell tour.

Tell the host, though. That's the one person who is keeping a mental count of who's still in the house.

### Where the habit actually forms

Here's the part that reframes the rest: you can't build this in the situations where it matters.

Anything you only do while being observed will fail you the moment you're tired, distracted, or up against something that matters — because it was never a habit, only a performance, and performances need attention to run.

The place it gets built is the unglamorous middle of the week. Nobody to impress, nobody impressing you. How you speak to someone on the phone about a delivery. Whether you say the awkward thing to the friend who won't remember either way.

That's not a moral point, it's a mechanical one. Habits formed in private run without supervision. That's the only kind that shows up when you need them.

### One caveat on the phone rule

The goal isn't zero. There's an opposite failure — being so removed from the thing that you take days to reply and become the person nobody can reach, which is its own inconsiderate.

The instruction is just to notice your own pattern. Most people have one, and almost nobody has looked at it directly.

A few small things to try:

- Pick your worst one and only that one, for a week.
- Say "my fault" once when it might not have been.
- Reply to the group invite you've been leaving.
- Leave the next party via the host.
