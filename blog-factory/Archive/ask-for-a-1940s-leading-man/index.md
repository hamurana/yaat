# Ask for a 1940s Leading Man

{{< lead >}}
The Old Hollywood cut looks like it needs a stylist and a film crew. It's a scissor cut, a side parting, and the right kind of pomade — and it's genuinely easier in the morning than most modern styles.
{{< /lead >}}

### The three things that make it

- **Oil-based pomade.** Not water-based. This is the one non-negotiable, because the shine and the way it holds a comb line only come from oil.
- **Natural length.** No clippers, no undercut, no fade. The sides are cut with scissors and left in proportion, which is what stops it reading as a modern style with product in it.
- **A side parting.** Or slicked straight back, which is what Bogart did.

That's the whole specification. It was the default for well-off men from the 1920s through to the late 1960s — Cary Grant, James Stewart, Bogart, and on screen, Don Draper and Connery's Bond.

### What to actually say

Two practical notes that save a bad haircut.

Go to a stylist or a traditional barber rather than a clipper-only shop. This is a scissor cut and plenty of places genuinely don't do those any more.

Then ask for a "classic side part, scissor cut," bring a photo of Don Draper or Cary Grant, and say you want to look like a leading man from the 1940s. That last sentence does more work than any technical description, because it tells the barber the whole picture at once.

Length matters more than people expect. Too short and it won't comb into clean lines; too long and it goes greasy and shapeless. Medium is the target.

### The morning routine

Comb it, add pomade, done. A plain Wahl pocket comb is all the equipment involved.

One thing worth knowing: oil pomade doesn't fully wash out in a single shampoo, and that's a feature rather than a problem. You leave it in and top up, and the hair stays combable through the day instead of going dry and crusty by lunchtime.

The person who made this switched from a pompadour specifically because this is quicker — no blow dryer, no stacking three products.

### Whether it suits you

It shows your whole face, so it flatters defined features and gives you nowhere to hide, which is worth knowing before committing.

On hairlines it's more forgiving than expected. A high hairline and large forehead works — that's Don Draper. A low hairline with a widow's peak works. Even thinning hair works, because the style doesn't rely on volume.

The honest caveat is that it reads *mature*. It sits naturally with suits and traditional clothes, and it's a deliberate contrast with modern casual clothes rather than an accident. If you're twenty-two and want to look twenty-two, this isn't it.

And the Mad Men observation is a fair one — Pete and Don have essentially the same haircut and it does very different things for each of them. The cut is the smaller variable.

### A few small things to try

- Grow it to medium length before booking anything
- Find a barber who does scissor cuts and ask specifically for one
- Buy an oil-based pomade, not water-based — check the tin
- Say "1940s leading man" out loud in the chair and see what happens
