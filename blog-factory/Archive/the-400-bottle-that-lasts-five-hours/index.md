{{< lead >}}
Creed Aventus costs over $400, lasts about five hours, and projects a foot. It comes in a cardboard box with a plastic lid. It is also genuinely beautiful. All true at once, and only one of those is on the price tag.
{{< /lead >}}

### Price is not performance

The two things people assume move together are longevity and cost, and they mostly don't. Clones sold at a tenth of Aventus money routinely last longer and project further. Not "close enough for the money" — further.

It's also reformulated most years, so the bottle you loved and the bottle you rebuy aren't reliably the same thing.

That doesn't make the expensive one a fraud. It makes it jewellery. Fine, as long as you know that's the purchase you're making.

### Cheap bottles that outperform their price

- **L'Eau d'Issey Pour Homme.** A fresh scent that actually lasts, which is rare — the yuzu is what does it, a slightly pungent Asian citrus. Twenty-plus years old and still one of the best values in the category.
- **Montblanc Legend Spirit.** Sits very close to the much pricier Invictus Aqua. Note that it's unrelated to regular Legend, and better than it.
- **Nautica Voyage.** Ozonic and oceanic with a stronger dry-down than the price suggests.
- **Versace Eros** and **D&G The One EDT.** Both safe, both effective. The One is a close-quarters date scent; Eros is a going-out scent that works, with the caveat that you will smell like several other men in the room.

### The ones worth paying for

Distinctiveness is the thing money can occasionally buy, and it's a better reason to spend than concentration.

- **Green Irish Tweed** (Creed, 1985). The one that made Creed's name — lemon verbena, iris, violet leaf, ambergris, sandalwood. Nobody argues with it and it hasn't dated.
- **Armani Code** (2004). Star anise up against citrus, tobacco and leather underneath. Fresh at the top, properly masculine on the dry-down.
- **Dior Fahrenheit** (1988). The famous petrol-and-leather scent, which is actually built from about twenty-five notes, most of them floral. Genuinely divisive. Maybe one man in five can wear it — and that one cuts straight through a room where everything else smells pleasant and identical.

### Safe is a real cost

Gucci Guilty Black and Burberry Touch are perfectly good lavender-aromatic scents. Office-safe, versatile, inoffensive. Nobody will ever remember them.

That's the trade nobody prices in. Versatility is bought with memorability, and if you only own safe fragrances you've spent money on a series of things that smell like the absence of a decision.

### The ones to stop recommending

- **Versace Pour Homme.** Excellent for two hours, then it's gone.
- **Montblanc Legend** (2011). A weaker take on Abercrombie & Fitch Fierce, needing two or three times the sprays to register.
- **Polo Blue** and **Kenneth Cole Reaction.** Fresh melon openings with nothing holding them up — they want more musk and sandalwood at the base. Polo Blue is overpriced besides.
- **JPG Le Mâle** (1995). A classic, and it now smells like one. The flankers, Ultra Male in particular, have aged better.
- **Versace Dylan Blue** (2016). Competent, and largely redundant if you already own Bleu de Chanel or Sauvage.

### The honest version of the advice

Don't read price as quality. Read it as materials, marketing, and the bottle. Then judge the thing on how long it lasts, how far it travels, and whether anyone can tell it apart from what everyone else is wearing.

A few small things to try:

- Time your most expensive fragrance. Actually time it.
- Buy one distinctive scent to sit alongside the safe ones.
- Test on your own skin before committing — longevity varies more between people than between price tiers.
- Match the bottle to the occasion instead of reaching for the dearest one.
