There's a quick test for whether your trousers are too big. Take a step and watch. If the fabric moves before your leg does, there's more of it than there needs to be — and that surplus is what's making you look shorter and heavier than you are.
{{< /lead >}}

### Six adjustments, none of which need new clothes

**Fit.** Wide through the thigh, loose at the calf, bunching at the ankle — that combination adds visible weight to the lower body and breaks the line running from your waist to your shoe. What you want is **slim-straight**: a little room in the thigh, a gradual taper, nothing stacking at the bottom. Not skinny. Skinny fails in the other direction.

**Length.** Excess fabric folding over the shoe drags the eye down and shortens you. Too short reads as trying. The target is a slight break or none at all — the cloth just touching the shoe, or sitting cleanly above it.

This one is worth stating plainly: **don't replace them, hem them.** A tailor charges very little, and a correct length produces a clean vertical line that reads taller and leaner on trousers you already own.

**Colour.** Very pale beige and washed-out tones go flat and make an older face look tired. Loud trend colours look forced. The ones that carry weight are navy, olive, a proper khaki rather than a bleached one, and charcoal. All easy to repeat and all read as controlled.

**Shoes.** Chunky running shoes disconnect the outfit at exactly the point it should be resolving. Minimal leather sneakers, classic loafers, or plain derbies. If your shoes are louder than your outfit, they're the problem.

**The top.** A faded tee hanging past your hips cancels everything below it, however well the trousers fit. A fitted polo, a crisp Oxford or lightweight cotton shirt, or a light knit sweater — nothing tight, nothing hanging.

### The one that's invisible until you see it

**Proportion.** You can have all five of the above right and still look slightly off, because shirt length, trouser rise, and leg width have to agree with each other.

A long shirt over a low-sitting waistband and a wide leg makes the torso read long and the legs short, which makes the whole body look heavier. Three things fix it:

- Shirt ending around mid-fly.
- Chinos sitting at your natural waist, not below it.
- The leg following your shape without surplus.

That's what "style is balance, not pieces" actually means. You can own five good garments and wear them in a way that adds up to less than they should.

### The formula that always works

Well-fitted chinos in **navy or olive**, a clean polo or button-down, leather sneakers or loafers.

That's it. No overthinking and no trying. It works for a casual dinner, a weekend lunch, or anything in between, and it repeats indefinitely without anyone getting bored of it.

### What this is really about

Not looking younger. Nothing here makes anyone look younger and chasing that produces worse outcomes than doing nothing.

It's about clarity — fewer pieces, better decisions, a silhouette that reads as deliberate. A well-dressed older man isn't chasing anything. He's controlling what people see, which is a considerably easier job.

A few small things to try:

- Take a step in your usual chinos and watch the fabric.
- Take your best-fitting pair to a tailor for the hem alone.
- Put the palest pair you own at the back of the wardrobe.
- Check where your shirt ends relative to your fly.
