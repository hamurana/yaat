# Dress Your Age, Not Your Twenties

{{< lead >}}
Past thirty the goal quietly changes. It stops being about looking young and starts being about looking like a grown man. Most guys miss the switch and keep dressing the way they did at 23, while everyone around them has started responding to them differently.
{{< /lead >}}

### What's worth retiring

None of this is about spending more. It's mostly about noticing that a few things stopped working:

- **Graphic tees and logo shirts.** Unless someone's paying you to advertise, a solid or V-neck tee in decent thick fabric does the same job better.
- **Anything oversized.** Fit is the whole game. Wrong size cuts your leg line short, gets in the way when you move, and reads as not paying attention. Even casual stuff can be tailored.
- **Baggy and distressed denim.** If the wear didn't come from actual work, it's costume. Dark denim that fits does more than any ripped pair.
- **Running shoes as everyday shoes.** They're for running. Worn all day they drag the whole outfit down with them.
- **Hoodies as your outer layer.** Fine for the gym. Otherwise a sweater with some structure or texture reads better without being less comfortable.
- **Varsity and denim jackets.** They look good on younger guys. A structured jacket does more for you now.

### What goes in instead

- **Shoes first.** Clean white leather sneakers, or grey if you want something that goes with everything. Then Chelsea boots — easy to get on, slight heel, slim shape — or loafers, or brogues.
- **One structured jacket, minimum.** A suit that actually fits in charcoal or navy, plus a sports jacket or plain blazer that works over jeans and chinos both. Skip loud patterns until the plain ones are covered.
- **Sweaters instead of hoodies.** Look for a collar, a rib pattern, some texture. Small difference, reads much older in a good way.
- **Natural fabrics.** Linen, cotton chinos, lightweight wool, denim. The stretchy technical trousers everyone's wearing don't hang right and never will. That trend'll pass.

### The accessories that give you away

- **Caps.** If you wear one, make it fitted, plain, muted. Not the faded one from university.
- **Watches.** Move off fashion watches toward something with actual quality behind it. Seiko is a reasonable place to start without spending much.
- **Bags.** The canvas backpack is a student signal. A leather briefcase, if your work justifies it, says something different.

### The question underneath all of it

For each thing in your wardrobe: *does this serve who I am now?*

That's really the whole principle. Fit matters more than the label. Condition matters more than whether it's current. And someone who's clearly dressed on purpose has said something about himself before he opens his mouth.

It's also worth saying this doesn't need to happen at once. Replacing one category at a time — shoes, then jackets, then trousers — gets you there without a painful weekend and a big bill.

### A few small things to try

- Take everything you've owned for over five years and try it on honestly in a mirror
- Take one thing you already like to a tailor and see what a proper fit feels like
- Replace the daily running shoes first — it's the single biggest visible change
- Buy one plain blazer that works with jeans, and wear it somewhere ordinary
