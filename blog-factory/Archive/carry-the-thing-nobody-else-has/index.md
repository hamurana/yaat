# Carry the Thing Nobody Else Has

{{< lead >}}
Most men carry a phone, keys and a wallet. The interesting thing about the five additions below is that almost none of them are for you — they're for the moment when someone nearby needs something and nobody has it.
{{< /lead >}}

### The five

- **A handkerchief.** Not a pocket square, which is decoration. A plain one, in a trouser or jacket pocket, for actual use. The moment it matters is never scheduled — a funeral, a spill, someone upset. Being able to hand one over is a small act of care people tend to remember for a long time. Two rules: it isn't for blowing your nose, and it goes in the wash immediately after. Buy five so there's always a clean one.
- **A decent pen.** Phones were supposed to kill this and didn't — "does anyone have a pen?" still happens constantly, and the usual answer is a chewed biro or a blank look. A Parker is inexpensive and does the job; spend more if you want to. Once you own a pen you actually like you stop losing it, which is its own small surprise.
- **A refillable fragrance atomiser.** Most scent is gone by mid-afternoon. A small refill in your bag means you can top up before an evening without carrying a full bottle around. Genuinely overlooked — most men have never thought about it despite the obvious use.
- **Cash.** Unglamorous and repeatedly necessary. Cash-only bars exist. Valets sometimes can't take a card. Amex isn't accepted everywhere. A small spread of notes covers parking, a round, or a tip without a fuss, and bar staff would rather have a cash tip than wait on card processing.
- **A lighter.** Even if you don't smoke. "Got a light?" is one of the oldest openers there is, and if you don't have one the moment simply goes to whoever does. A Zippo costs very little and is more distinctive than a plastic one.

### What they've got in common

Four of the five are things you're carrying for other people.

That's the actual case for any of it. The handkerchief matters on the day someone else is crying. The pen matters when someone needs to write down a number. The lighter matters because it hands you a reason to talk to someone.

Preparedness sounds like a boy-scout virtue and mostly functions as a social one. Being the person who has the thing is a very cheap way of being useful, and useful is more memorable than well-dressed.

### Where I'd be careful

There's a version of this that curdles into collecting — the Dunhill lighter, the Mont Blanc, the initialled handkerchief, all of it slightly performed and none of it ever used.

The tell is whether you'd be happy to give the thing away. A handkerchief you'd hesitate to hand to a stranger because it's expensive has stopped doing its job. Same with the pen: if you'd be irritated by not getting it back, it's an object you own rather than a thing you carry.

The cheap versions of all five work fine. What matters is having one at all.

### A few small things to try

- Put a plain handkerchief in a pocket tomorrow and forget about it
- Buy one pen you like enough to notice if it's gone
- Keep £20 in small notes somewhere you won't spend it
- Ask yourself, for each item, whether you'd hand it to a stranger without flinching
