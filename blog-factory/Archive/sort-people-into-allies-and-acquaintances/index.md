# Sort People Into Allies and Acquaintances

{{< lead >}}
You can't cut everyone difficult out of your life. Some of them are your colleagues, some are family. What you can do is stop being confused about which category someone's actually in.
{{< /lead >}}

### The seven worth spotting

- **Gossips.** If they're talking about someone else to you, they're talking about you to someone else. Worth stopping early — "I'd rather not hear that" does it. Listening either drags you in or quietly poisons how you see the person being discussed.
- **Jealous ones.** They shrink your good news the moment you share it. Odd thing is a lot of them do want you to do well, they just can't say it. Reframe that helps: critics make you do it better, haters make you do it faster.
- **Time wasters.** Not usually deliberate. They ramble, they want your attention without a reason. Redirecting kindly works — ask for the short version, say you've got something you need to get to.
- **Money wrappers.** The ones always asking, always vanishing when the bill lands. Worth actually checking your statements for. Are you buying things for people who need it, or people who just ask?
- **Oversensitive people.** Carrying enough that they feel got-at most of the time. They can be in your life. They shouldn't have a big role in it if you're trying to go somewhere, because the emotional bandwidth they need is the same bandwidth direction needs.
- **Excuse makers.** Always tired, always broke, always a reason it won't work. Sometimes they're actually shielding you from their own fear. Doesn't mean the fear is right.
- **Miseducated people.** Not unintelligent — just holding wrong ideas about what actually gets you somewhere, and degrees don't protect anyone from that. The tricky version is the friend who uses supportive language while quietly getting in the way.

### The bit that actually matters

The list is less useful as a hit list than as a sorting tool.

Avoidance mostly isn't available. What is available is being honest with yourself about whether someone's an ally or an acquaintance, and then giving them the amount of room that matches. Most of the damage doesn't come from difficult people existing. It comes from treating an acquaintance like an ally — taking their opinion seriously, giving them a say, being surprised each time it goes the way it always goes.

You can be warm to someone and still not hand them a vote on your decisions. Those are separate things, and people mix them up constantly.

### Weight the other direction too

The flip side of this gets less attention and is probably worth more: deliberately spend more time around people who raise what you think is normal.

The company you keep does more to your trajectory than nearly any other input, and that works both ways. Most people are careful about who they let in and passive about who they seek out. The seeking-out part is the half you have to do on purpose.

### A few small things to try

- Take one relationship that reliably leaves you flat and ask honestly: ally, or acquaintance?
- Next time gossip starts, say you'd rather not — once, and see how it feels
- Look at last month's spending and notice who it went to
- Pick one person who's clearly ahead of you and find a way to spend more time near them
