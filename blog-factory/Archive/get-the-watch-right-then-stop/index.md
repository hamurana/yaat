# Get the Watch Right, Then Stop

{{< lead >}}
Seven accessories, and the same instruction attached to every one of them: choose for fit and personality, skip the branding. Get the watch sorted first and the rest is small adjustments.
{{< /lead >}}

### The watch, because it matters most

Decide three things before looking at anything: **strap, case size, budget.** That order saves you from falling for something that doesn't suit your wrist.

Research online, then try it on in a shop — the size especially is impossible to judge from a photo. Reasonable starting points are the Seiko SARB or SNK, the Tissot PRX Powermatic, or the Hamilton Khaki Field. If you're spending properly, the Cartier Tank, a Rolex Submariner, or the Omega Seamaster.

One note on size: 33–41mm covers most wrists comfortably, and the era of enormous watches is behind us.

### The rest

- **Necklaces and bracelets.** These do nothing practical and that's fine — they exist because you like them. Silver or gold, shorter length, a pendant rather than a bare chain. Pyrra and Miansai at sensible prices, David Yurman if you're going further.
- **A real dop kit.** Graduating from the plastic bag that came free with something to an actual leather one is a small upgrade you feel every time you pack.
- **A grown-up bag.** Leather or leather-trimmed backpack, or a duffel. Carl Friedrik, Troubadour, Filson, Shinola. Start in black or brown. A grown man in a school backpack is the thing to avoid, not backpacks as a category.
- **Sunglasses — never buy these unseen.** The one item where fit is everything, and faces vary too much for a photo to tell you anything. Moscot, Oliver Peoples, Persol.
- **A slim wallet.** Audit what you're actually carrying first, throw out most of it, then buy a card holder or pocket organiser rather than a bulging bifold.
- **A plain leather belt.** Thin, brown or black, ordinary buckle. Around $100 is genuinely as much as this needs to cost, and it's back to being useful now that shirts get tucked again.

### What's actually being said

Strip the brand names out and the advice is consistent: **buy for fit, and signal nothing.**

Every category repeats it. Plain buckle. No monogram. Simple chain. Smaller frames. The accessories that work are the ones that don't announce themselves, which is a slightly odd conclusion for a list of accessories and is also clearly right.

There's a useful test buried in it — the "what would Bond do" question, applied to whether you pull out a slim card holder or a velcro wallet on a date. Silly framing, correct instinct: would this object make sense to someone who isn't thinking about it at all?

### One thing to hold lightly

Advice like this always arrives attached to a lot of shopping, some of it from brands with a stake in the recommendation, and the price points run from $100 to genuinely serious money.

The framework survives that, though. Fit over brand, few over many, plain over loud — none of it requires the specific items. A $40 belt that fits properly does the same work as a $100 one, and the watch guidance is about *your wrist* rather than which name is on the dial.

### A few small things to try

- Decide your strap, case size and budget before you look at a single watch
- Empty your wallet, keep only what you used this month, and buy something slim
- Try sunglasses on in person, whatever the internet costs less
- Count the logos currently visible on you, and remove one
