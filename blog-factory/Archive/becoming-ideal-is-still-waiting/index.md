# Becoming Ideal Is Still Waiting

{{< lead >}}
45% of men aged 18–25 have never approached a woman in person. 62% of Gen Z men have stepped out of dating altogether. Underneath that is a strategy that feels like effort but isn't: become impressive enough that the thing you want arrives on its own.
{{< /lead >}}

### The passive fantasy

Jack Reacher is the clean example. Hugely capable, obviously masculine, and romantically completely passive — the women in those books make the first move, and the second, and the third.

That's the fantasy, and it's a comfortable one: get good enough and you never have to risk being turned down. But it leaves you in the passenger seat of your own life, waiting to be picked rather than picking.

The same shape shows up well outside dating. A lot of what gets sold to men — get lean, get rich, get status — is the identical bet. Become ideal, then wait. It's not that the self-improvement is bad. It's that on its own it's still waiting, just with better arms.

### Direct, but reading the room

The alternative isn't charging at things. Matt Murdock is the counterexample worth borrowing from — he initiates, but he's paying attention the whole time.

Attraction doesn't jump from stranger to kissing. The zoologist Desmond Morris laid out something like twelve steps in *Intimate Behavior* — hand, shoulder, face, and so on, in order. Going a step at a time means you're reading her continuously. She leans in, you go forward. She pulls back, you hold. Nothing feels like a leap because nothing is one.

Practically that means:

- Skip sitting across a table on a first date. Somewhere with movement — a walk, dancing — lets early contact happen naturally
- If it is dinner, sit catty-corner rather than opposite
- When she's reaching toward you, don't just receive it. Match it and take one step more

### Playful and forward, both

Flirting needs two ingredients and most people only bring one.

**Playfulness** is lightness, teasing, making it fun. **Forwardness** is being clear you're interested.

Only playful and you become the funny friend who she never realises was interested. Only forward and it lands as intense, or oddly humourless. The mix is what works — a direct compliment, then something self-deprecating right after to take the pressure back out. Craig Ferguson does this constantly. The compliment lands, and then nobody has to sit in the tension it created.

### What it actually rests on

The part I keep coming back to: none of this works without knowing what you want.

Direct action needs a direction. If you're not clear on what you're actually after, you can't take a step toward it — all that's left is making yourself generally impressive and hoping something finds you.

So the useful question is probably less "how do I approach" and more "what do I actually want here." The first one has techniques. The second one is the bottleneck.

### A few small things to try

- Ask what you actually want from a situation before working out how to act in it
- Take one small direct step toward something you've been positioning yourself for
- On a date, pick somewhere with movement instead of a table
- Notice if you're always playful and never forward — or the reverse — and add the missing half
