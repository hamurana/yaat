The most aging thing most men wear isn't a garment. It's the sentence "this'll do" on the way out of the door. Said once it means nothing. Said every day for a decade it becomes a wardrobe, and then a way of being seen.
{{< /lead >}}

### The good clothes in the cupboard

Here's the habit almost nobody names. The navy jacket, the merino sweater, the decent shoes — all of them exist, and all of them come out for weddings and funerals.

So the actual life gets lived in the tired stuff. Lunch with a friend, coffee, visiting family, the shops. *It's only lunch.* Nobody says anything, and the impression forms anyway.

But those days aren't the gaps between the important ones. They're the bulk of it. Wear the better sweater to coffee. Take the clean jacket to dinner. Put the good shoes on to see your children.

### Standards, not garments

Every other habit on this list is a version of the same slow slide.

**Comfort with nothing holding it up.** Shapeless sweatshirts, sagging jeans, once-white trainers gone grey, jackets with no shape in the shoulder. That reads as neglected rather than relaxed. The same comfort with a bit of structure — a knit polo, a lightweight quarter-zip, dark straight-leg jeans, clean leather sneakers — costs nothing extra and lands completely differently.

**Dressing for the body you remember.** The blazer bought when your shoulders were broader is still hanging there with sleeves too long and the front pulling at the button. That isn't age. It's a piece of clothing and a man who no longer match. Once or twice a year, try the important things on in a full-length mirror and look at four things: shoulder seam, sleeve length, pull at the stomach, gather at the ankle. A small alteration makes an old jacket look expensive again.

**Shoes past saving.** Broken in is not the same as broken down. Cracked, dirty, or with the sole gone soft, they age everything above them.

**Everything the same flat tone.** Faded grey tee, washed-out jeans, a black jacket with no depth left — no contrast anywhere, so the face looks tired by association. The fix isn't bright colour, it's controlled contrast: an ivory shirt under a navy jacket, a light blue Oxford with dark jeans, a camel sweater against charcoal trousers. Done right, grey hair reads as distinguished rather than washed out.

**The edges.** Collar holding its shape, sleeves ending near the wrist bone, a belt that isn't cracked, trousers hemmed to sit without a heavy break, an untucked shirt landing around the middle of the zip rather than halfway down your thighs. Small, and it's where the polish lives.

### Restraint is not the same as surrender

The last habit is the subtlest, because it starts as good instinct.

Not wanting to look like you're trying is correct. Taken too far it becomes a reason to avoid better fit, cleaner shoes, a modern jacket, a knit polo, dark denim — anything that might suggest you thought about it.

At which point "age-appropriate" has quietly turned into giving up. There's a lot of room between dressing like a man half your age and dressing like a man who's stopped. Current and intentional isn't trendy. It's just present.

### What it actually is

Not vanity, and not an attempt to look younger. It's walking into a room looking like someone who still has regard for himself, his age, and the people he's meeting.

That's mostly built out of small decisions on unremarkable days.

A few small things to try:

- Wear one of the good things this week to something that doesn't matter.
- Clean your shoes today rather than at the weekend.
- Get one pair of trousers hemmed.
- Notice the next time you say "this'll do", and change one thing.
