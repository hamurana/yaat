# Keep the Casual Pieces Casual

{{< lead >}}
Craig's Bond never looks scruffy dressed down, and the reason isn't only fit. It's a rule he keeps: the polo is a casual piece, always. Put on a blazer and he switches to a button-down. That discipline is doing more than it looks like.
{{< /lead >}}

### Why the rule matters

A polo sits in a specific gap — smarter than a tee, less formal than a proper shirt. That's a genuinely useful slot to fill.

The moment you start wearing it under a jacket it stops occupying that slot and starts being a slightly worse shirt. You've lost the piece that was working and gained one that isn't. Keeping it strictly casual is what preserves what it was good for.

Small thing. But most of dressing well is this sort of small thing applied consistently.

### The three pieces

- **A navy polo.** No logo, no contrast details, slim through the body. Navy specifically, because it goes with everything else you own.
- **A tan suede jacket.** The most flexible layer of the three — over a button-down, over a polo, over a plain tee. Suede reads adult and masculine without going formal. Worth buying real rather than faux; that's the difference between a wardrobe piece and a thing you own for one season. Craig's was by Matchless and isn't sold any more, but the principle carries: decent suede, tan or cognac.
- **Chukka boots.** He wore them constantly, with chinos and a polo right up to a suit. Suede, open lacing, lighter and more breathable than a leather Chelsea, so they work in warm weather. The wider point is just that a decent boot reads more grown-up than a sneaker, nearly always.

### The two supporting habits

**Keep the colours boring.** Whites, navys, tans, greys. No neon, no pastels, no loud patterns. Then match your neutral base to your own colouring — Craig's tan lets him wear a lot of warm beige, which won't work identically on everyone.

A limited palette makes getting dressed easier rather than harder, which is the thing people don't expect until they try it.

**Wear one watch and nothing else.** No rings, no bracelets, no chains. One good watch does all of it. The Seamaster if you're spending Bond money; Seiko gets you the same look for a fraction, and that substitution genuinely works.

Same with sunglasses — he wore Persol, Barton Perreira, Tom Ford across the films, but always a classic shape that suited his face. The brand was never the point. The shape was.

### What makes it copyable

This is the part I like about it. None of these are film-budget items or hard to find. A navy polo, a suede jacket, a pair of chukkas, four colours, one watch.

The look isn't the result of access to a studio wardrobe. It's the result of buying few things, choosing them to work together, and then having the discipline not to muddle their jobs.

### A few small things to try

- Stop wearing polos under blazers and see whether the polo looks better on its own days
- Buy one decent suede jacket instead of three cheap layers
- Try boots instead of sneakers for a week and notice how it changes the rest
- Take everything off except the watch
