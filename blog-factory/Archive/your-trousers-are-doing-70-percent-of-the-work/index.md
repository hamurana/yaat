# Your Trousers Are Doing 70% of the Work

{{< lead >}}
Men agonise over shirts and buy trousers almost by accident, which is backwards. Trousers decide roughly 70% of whether you look put together, and almost nobody is thinking about them.
{{< /lead >}}

### Build a system, not a pile

The underlying idea is that every piece has to work with several others. Buy that way and a small wardrobe covers the school run, a date, a barbecue and a video call. Buy single-purpose things and you end up with a full wardrobe and nothing to wear.

Five categories cover it.

### 1. Shirts

Button-downs first, in **white and light blue**. Then a navy polo, a Henley, and some decent plain tees.

Fit rules that matter more than the brand: it has to fit at the shoulders, because that's the one thing a tailor can't easily fix. Worn untucked, the hem should land around mid-fly. And in the fitting room, lift your arms — if the whole shirt rides up with them, it's wrong.

### 2. Trousers — the rule of three

Navy chinos, dark jeans, khaki chinos. That's it, and it covers nearly everything.

Dark jeans means dark blue or black with no rips, fading or distressing. **Mid-rise after 40**, never low-rise. Straight or slightly tapered — not skinny, not baggy. The hem should just touch the shoe with the smallest break.

This is the category worth fixing first, because it's the one carrying most of the impression and the one nobody audits. The example that stuck: a man whose shirts were genuinely good still looked sloppy, and fixing only the trousers took about ten years off him.

### 3. Layers

Casual: a clean zip-up hoodie in grey, navy or black, and a cardigan over a Henley or button-down.

Sharper: a light jacket — bomber, casual blazer, or field jacket — and a quarter-zip.

If you're buying one thing, buy the light jacket. It's wearable about nine months a year. And every layer has to work over more than one shirt or it isn't earning space.

### 4. Shoes — where most of it falls apart

Shoes can make cheap clothes look expensive and expensive clothes look cheap, and this is where most men lose it.

Three pairs: clean white or grey **leather** sneakers (not running shoes), brown leather casual shoes — loafers or lace-ups, real leather, and mid-range is fine — and one boot, chukka or casual work boot, in brown or tan.

One thing worth doing: get your feet measured again. They've changed since your twenties, and quite a lot of adult men are wearing the wrong size out of habit.

### 5. Accessories — the rule of two

Never more than two at once. Watch and belt, or watch and sunglasses.

The watch can be a £50 Timex or something far dearer — what matters is that it fits and goes with most of what you own. Belt matches the shoe family: brown belt, brown shoes. Sunglasses in classic shapes.

### Where to start tonight

Find your weakest category and fix the **fit** there before buying anything new. For most men that's the trousers, and it's also the cheapest fix, because a tailor costs less than a shopping trip.

### A few small things to try

- Try on every pair of trousers you own and be honest about the rise and the hem
- Get your feet measured again
- Check whether each layer you own works over more than one shirt
- Count your accessories and take one off
